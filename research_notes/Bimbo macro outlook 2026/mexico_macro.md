## 1. GDP: 2025 actual, 2026 quarterly prints, 2026-2028 forecasts

### Takeaway
Growth is weak but recovering. GDP grew only about 0.6-0.8% in 2025. 1Q26 contracted, and 2Q26 rebounded by +1.4% q/q, helped by World Cup spending. Banxico expects a slowdown in 3Q26. Consensus for 2026 is about 1.4-1.5% and for 2027 about 1.8-2.0%. The one 2028 forecast found (World Bank) is 2.2%.

### Cited Findings
- **2025 actual:** INEGI reported +0.8% real growth seasonally adjusted, or +0.6% on original figures, against a government target of 2-3%. 4Q25 grew 1.8% y/y on original figures (published 2026-02-23) — [La Jornada](https://www.jornada.com.mx/noticia/2026/02/23/economia/el-pib-de-mexico-crecio-08-por-ciento-en-2025); [El Universal (0.6%)](https://www.eluniversal.com.mx/cartera/pib-de-mexico-crece-06-en-2025-inegi-confirma-repunte-trimestral-tras-dos-caidas/); [México ¿cómo vamos? notes GDP per capita is back at 2017 levels](https://mexicocomovamos.mx/publicaciones/2026/02/la-economia-crece-0-8-en-2025-vs-2024-el-pib-per-capita-en-niveles-de-2017/)
- **1Q26:** GDP contracted q/q. Sources disagree on the size: one reports -0.6% q/q and another -0.3%. Banxico's May statement cites "la contracción de la actividad económica en el primer trimestre de 2026" — [Mexico Business News](https://mexicobusiness.news/finance/news/mexico-real-gdp-grows-14-qoq-2q26-just-beating-projections); [Proceso, 2026-05-07](https://www.proceso.com.mx/economia/2026/5/7/con-dos-votos-en-contra-banxico-baja-la-tasa-650-concluye-ciclo-de-recortes-373610.html)
- **2Q26:** INEGI's revised figure (2026-08-24) is +1.4% q/q seasonally adjusted, against a 1.5% flash estimate on 2026-07-30. Annual growth was +1.9% seasonally adjusted and +2.1% unadjusted. By sector q/q: primary +2.4%, secondary +1.6%, tertiary +1.4% — [Mexico Business News](https://mexicobusiness.news/trade-and-investment/news/mexicos-economy-grew-14-2q26); [Líder Empresarial](https://us.liderempresarial.com/posts/pib-de-mexico-crece-14-en-el-segundo-trimestre-de-2026)

- **3Q26:** Banxico's statement of 2026-09-24 says the economy "habría moderado su ritmo de expansión" in 3Q26 and sees "condiciones de holgura" (slack) over the forecast horizon, with downside risks to activity — [Banxico statement PDF, 2026-09-24](https://www.banxico.org.mx/canales/%7B64E93EB1-E9FF-891E-60C6-9BBC812A50C6%7D.pdf)
- **Banxico (Quarterly Report Apr-Jun 2026, published 2026-08-26):** 2026 GDP forecast raised to 1.5% (range 1.0-2.0%) from 1.1%. The 2027 forecast is 2.0% (range 1.2-2.8%), down from 2.1%. Banxico expects investment to stay weak because of uncertainty over US trade and USMCA ratification — [Expansión](https://expansion.mx/economia/2026/08/26/banxico-economia-pib-2026-1-5); [El Financiero](https://www.elfinanciero.com.mx/economia/2026/08/26/banxico-le-apuesta-a-que-pib-de-mexico-crecera-15-en-2026/); [Banxico executive summary](https://www.banxico.org.mx/publicaciones-y-prensa/informes-trimestrales/%7B327C4C24-14D5-9E05-DD4F-6F5DB01DCAFE%7D.pdf)
- **Banxico private-analyst survey (published early Oct-2026, fielded Sep 15-28; 42 firms):** 2026 GDP 1.4% (up from 1.3%), 2027 GDP 1.8% (unchanged) — [Forbes México](https://forbes.com.mx/sector-privado-baja-pronostico-de-inflacion-en-mexico-para-2026-y-sube-el-del-pib-sondeo-banxico/); [El Universal](https://www.eluniversal.com.mx/cartera/elevan-de-13-a-14-la-prevision-del-pib-para-2026/)
- **IMF:** The staff review of 2026-10-02 puts 2026 at 1.5% and 2027 at 1.8%, and conditions this on fiscal consolidation. Earlier vintages: WEO April 2026 had 1.6% for 2026 and 2.2% for 2027. The mid-2026 update cut these to 1.2% for 2026 and 1.9% for 2027. **The October 2026 WEO had not been found/published as of 2026-10-08** — [Mexico News Daily](https://mexiconewsdaily.com/news/imf-growth-forecast-mexico-2026/); [Mexico Business News](https://mexicobusiness.news/finance/news/imf-sees-modest-recovery-mexico-growth-outlook-2026-2027); [MND on the mid-year cut](https://mexiconewsdaily.com/business/imf-lowers-mexican-growth-forecast-citing-uncertainties-and-mideast-conflict/)

### Inferences
- The SHCP range for 2026 (1.8-2.8%) sits well above Banxico (1.5%), the IMF (1.5%) and consensus (1.4%), so official budget revenue assumptions look optimistic.
- 2Q26 strength looks partly one-off (World Cup), which matches Bimbo's 2Q26 Mexico +4.7%. With a 3Q26 slowdown, the 2H26 comparisons for Bimbo Mexico volumes get tougher.
- Base case for 2026-2028: real GDP growth of about 1.4-1.5%, then about 1.8-2.0%, then about 2.2%. That is roughly 1-2 pp below what a mass consumer staple would want for volume growth.

### Gaps

- The 1Q26 q/q contraction figure conflicts across sources (-0.6% vs -0.3%).
- No 2028 forecast from Banxico or the analyst survey was retrieved.

## 2. Inflation: headline, core, food, Banxico forecasts

### Takeaway
September-2026 headline CPI rose to 3.45% while core fell to 3.75%, its lowest since March 2025. Food, beverages and tobacco are running hotter at 4.57%. Banxico expects headline and core inflation to reach the 3% target only in 4Q27.

### Cited Findings
- **September 2026 (INEGI, published 2026-10-08):** headline 3.45% y/y (+0.42% m/m), up from 3.26% in August. Core 3.75%, down from 3.88% in August and below the Citi survey's 3.78%. Non-core 2.45%, up from 1.13% — [México ¿cómo vamos?](https://mexicocomovamos.mx/publicaciones/2026/10/la-inflacion-anual-sube-a-3-45-en-septiembre-por-el-alza-de-la-no-subyacente/); [La Razón](https://www.razon.com.mx/negocios/2026/10/08/inflacion-en-septiembre-de-2026-sube-a-345-inegi/); [Expansión](https://expansion.mx/economia/2026/10/08/inflacion-inegi-mexico-a-3-45-en-septiembre-2026)
- **Food:** "Alimentos, bebidas y tabaco" is the highest-inflation goods group at 4.57% y/y. Fruits and vegetables rose 4.40% m/m and 8.78% y/y, with tomato, onion and LP gas driving the increase. Within core, services were 4.27% and goods 3.21% — [México ¿cómo vamos?](https://mexicocomovamos.mx/publicaciones/2026/10/la-inflacion-anual-sube-a-3-45-en-septiembre-por-el-alza-de-la-no-subyacente/)
- **First half of September (INEGI, 2026-09-24):** headline 3.42% and core 3.79% — [INEGI bulletin 605/26](https://www.inegi.org.mx/contenidos/saladeprensa/boletines/2026/inpc/inpc_1q2026_09.pdf); [Reuters via Investing.com](https://www.investing.com/news/economic-indicators/mexicos-inflation-picks-up-more-than-expected-in-early-september-4915351)
- **Banxico forecast table (statement of 2026-09-24; annual % change of quarterly averages):**
  - Headline: 3Q26 3.3, 4Q26 3.5, 1Q27 3.4, 2Q27 3.3, 3Q27 3.2, 4Q27 3.0, then 3.0 through 3Q28.
  - Core: 3Q26 3.9, 4Q26 3.6, 1Q27 3.4, 2Q27 3.3, 3Q27 3.2, 4Q27 3.0, then 3.0 through 3Q28.
  - The balance of risks is tilted upward. Banxico expects headline to converge to target in 4Q27.
  - [Banxico statement PDF](https://www.banxico.org.mx/canales/%7B64E93EB1-E9FF-891E-60C6-9BBC812A50C6%7D.pdf)
- **Upside risks named by Banxico:** core persistence, trade policy disruptions or geopolitical conflict (Middle East), weather, cost pressures, and peso depreciation — [Banxico statement PDF](https://www.banxico.org.mx/canales/%7B64E93EB1-E9FF-891E-60C6-9BBC812A50C6%7D.pdf)
- **Analyst survey (early Oct-2026):** 2026 headline 3.87% (from 3.90%) and 2027 headline 3.82% (from 3.84%). One outlet reports 3.85% for both years — [Forbes México](https://forbes.com.mx/sector-privado-baja-pronostico-de-inflacion-en-mexico-para-2026-y-sube-el-del-pib-sondeo-banxico/); [IDC Online](https://idconline.mx/finanzas/2026/10/06/expectativas-banxico-2026-2027-tasas-de-interes-tipo-de-cambio-e-inflacion-para-el-cierre-de-ano)

### Inferences
- Analysts expect year-end 2026 headline of about 3.87%, which is above both Banxico's 4Q26 forecast of 3.5% and the 2025 outturn of 3.69%. Private expectations remain de-anchored above 3%.
- Processed-food and bread pricing power is supported by food CPI running about 1.1 pp above headline. Core goods inflation of about 3.2% caps how far Bimbo can push price above general inflation without volume elasticity.
- The IEPS shock to sugary drinks (Section 9) feeds into non-core and food prices in 2026.

### Gaps

## 3. Banxico policy rate: level, 2026 decisions, guidance, expected path

### Takeaway
Banxico cut from 7.00% at end-2025 to **6.50%**. The last 25 bp cut was on 2026-05-07 and was framed as the end of the easing cycle that began in March 2024. The rate has been held at 6.50% in the three decisions since, including a unanimous hold on 2026-09-24. Guidance is now data-dependent: the "appropriate to maintain" language was dropped. Consensus sees about 6.50% through 2027, and the main debate is between a resumed cut and a late-2027 hike if the Fed keeps tightening.

### Cited Findings
- **2026-05-07:** 25 bp cut to 6.50%, effective May 8. The vote was split 3-2: Rodríguez Ceja, Cuadra and Mejía for the cut, while Borja and Heath dissented in favour of holding at 6.75%. The board said the cut concluded the cycle that started in March 2024 and that "será apropiado mantener la tasa de referencia en su nivel actual" — [El Financiero](https://www.elfinanciero.com.mx/economia/2026/05/07/banxico-decision-tasa-de-interes-hoy-7-de-mayo-en-cuanto-quedo/); [Proceso](https://www.proceso.com.mx/economia/2026/5/7/con-dos-votos-en-contra-banxico-baja-la-tasa-650-concluye-ciclo-de-recortes-373610.html); [Bloomberg Línea](https://www.bloomberglinea.com/latinoamerica/mexico/banxico-baja-la-tasa-de-interes-a-650-y-cierra-ciclo-de-recortes/)
- Because the pre-cut rate was 6.75%, there was at least one other 25 bp cut between January and April 2026 (7.00% to 6.75%). The exact date was not retrieved — inference from [El Financiero](https://www.elfinanciero.com.mx/economia/2026/05/07/banxico-decision-tasa-de-interes-hoy-7-de-mayo-en-cuanto-quedo/)
- **2026-09-24:** held at 6.50% unanimously, the third consecutive hold. The statement says that, because Mexican conditions differ from the US, "la política monetaria no tendría que reaccionar de manera mecánica ante los ajustes previstos a la tasa de fondos federales". **The Fed raised its rate by 25 bp in September 2026** — [Banxico statement PDF](https://www.banxico.org.mx/canales/%7B64E93EB1-E9FF-891E-60C6-9BBC812A50C6%7D.pdf); [El Financiero 2026-09-24](https://www.elfinanciero.com.mx/economia/2026/09/24/tasa-del-banxico-hoy-24-de-septiembre-2026-en-cuanto-quedo/)
- El Financiero notes that the phrase about it being "apropiado mantener" the rate disappeared from the September statement, so future moves depend on inflation, the exchange rate and activity — [El Financiero 2026-09-25](https://www.elfinanciero.com.mx/economia/2026/09/25/banxico-se-desmarca-de-la-fed-y-mantiene-tasa-de-interes-en-65-por-ciento/)
- **2026-10-08 (September minutes coverage):** the board is split on whether to cut further. Two members ruled out a cut, while the discussion of lowering the rate remains open — [El Financiero 2026-10-08](https://www.elfinanciero.com.mx/economia/2026/10/08/cisma-en-el-banxico-por-bajar-o-no-la-tasa-de-interes-debemos-estar-abiertos-a-la-discusion/)
- **Expected path:** a private-sector analysis (Oct 6) puts the average funding rate at 6.51% for both 2026 and 2027. The Citi survey expects 6.50% to be held over the coming months. Some analysts rule out a move in 2026 but do not exclude a hike in late 2027 if the Fed tightens — [IDC Online](https://idconline.mx/finanzas/2026/10/06/expectativas-banxico-2026-2027-tasas-de-interes-tipo-de-cambio-e-inflacion-para-el-cierre-de-ano); [Investing.com](https://mx.investing.com/news/economy-news/fin-de-los-recortes-banxico-anuncia-la-ultima-bajada-a-tasas-se-quedan-en-650-3603666); [El Financiero 2026-09-25](https://www.elfinanciero.com.mx/economia/2026/09/25/banxico-se-desmarca-de-la-fed-y-mantiene-tasa-de-interes-en-65-por-ciento/)

### Inferences
- The real ex-ante rate is about 6.50% minus about 3.8% expected inflation, or roughly 2.7%. That is still restrictive with an output gap of slack.
- A rising Fed rate against a flat Banxico rate narrows the carry spread and is a peso-depreciation risk. That matters for Bimbo's USD-linked inputs (wheat) and for translating Mexico results.
- For BIMBOA's MXN debt cost, assume a flat rate of about 6.5% through 2027. The risk is skewed toward a hike rather than further cuts.

### Gaps
- The date of the first 2026 cut (7.00% to 6.75%) and the full 2026 meeting calendar were not retrieved.
- Market-implied TIIE swap curve pricing was not found.

## 4. Minimum wage, 40-hour week, real wages, formal employment

### Takeaway
The minimum wage rose 13% in 2026 (5% in the northern border zone), which keeps pushing up low-income purchasing power and Bimbo's labor costs. The 40-hour week phases in from 2027 to 2030 at 2 hours per year with no pay cut. Formal employment is at a record 22.9 million but grew only 1.6% y/y. The 2027 minimum wage will be decided in December 2026.

### Cited Findings
- **2026 minimum wage (CONASAMI, agreed 2025-12-03, effective 2026-01-01):** general zone +13%, from MXN 278.80 to 315.04 per day (MXN 9,582.47 per month). This combines MXN 17.01 of MIR (Monto Independiente de Recuperación) and a 6.5% percentage increase. Northern border zone (ZLFN) +5%, from MXN 419.88 to 440.87 per day (MXN 13,409.80 per month) — [La Jornada](https://www.jornada.com.mx/noticia/2026/01/02/politica/entra-en-vigor-alza-de-13-al-salario-minimo-en-zona-fronteriza-libre-crece-5); [El Universal](https://www.eluniversal.com.mx/consultas/blog/rrhh/salario-minimo-2026-montos-por-zona-geografica/)
- **2027 minimum wage:** no official figure yet. CONASAMI typically decides in December, with the increase taking effect on January 1 — [Infobae, 2026-08-07](https://www.infobae.com/mexico/2026/08/07/jornada-de-40-horas-y-salario-minimo-como-cambia-tu-pago-desde-enero-de-2027/); [WorkAxle](https://www.workaxle.com/es/blog/jornada-40-horas-mexico)
- **2025 minimum wage:** not re-verified in this search. It was +12% general / +5% border per earlier public data — **label: from memory, unverified**.
- **40-hour work week:** the maximum weekly hours fall from 48 to 46 on 2027-01-01, then to 44 in 2028, 42 in 2029 and 40 in 2030, and the cut cannot reduce pay. Dates conflict across sources: one says the Article 123 constitutional reform took effect on 2026-03-03 and the LFT decree was published 2026-05-01, while another describes 2026-05-01 as the effective date — [El Heraldo de México, 2026-09-21](https://heraldodemexico.com.mx/nacional/2026/9/21/la-jornada-laboral-baja-46-horas-el-de-enero-de-2027-no-podran-reducir-tu-sueldo-892260.html); [WorkAxle](https://www.workaxle.com/es/blog/jornada-40-horas-mexico); [El Cronista](https://www.cronista.com/mexico/actualidad-mx/oficial-y-confirmado-la-reduccion-se-la-semana-laboral-a-40-horas-semanales-llega-en-enero-del-2027-y-las-horas-extras-cambian-por-normativa/)
- Estimated cost per ordinary hour: about +4.3% in 2027 (48 to 46 hours) and about +20% by 2030 (48 to 40 hours). This is a consultancy estimate — [WorkAxle](https://www.workaxle.com/es/blog/jornada-40-horas-mexico)
- **Formal employment (IMSS, September 2026, published 2026-10-08):**
  - 22,927,276 jobs, a record; 87.1% permanent.
  - +128,803 jobs in September; +410,200 year to date.
  - +355,594 jobs over 12 months, or +1.6% y/y.
  - [Infobae/EFE](https://www.infobae.com/america/agencias/2026/10/08/el-empleo-formal-en-mexico-sube-en-septiembre-y-alcanza-record-de-229-millones-de-puestos/); [Excélsior](https://www.excelsior.com.mx/finanzas/record-empleos-imss-septiembre-2026)
- **IMSS average contribution wage (SBC):** MXN 666 per day in September 2026, the highest for any September, down from MXN 673.1 in August. One source gives the 2025 average as MXN 624.77 — [Infobae/EFE](https://www.infobae.com/america/agencias/2026/10/08/el-empleo-formal-en-mexico-sube-en-septiembre-y-alcanza-record-de-229-millones-de-puestos/); [Diario de México](https://www.diariodemexico.com/mi-nacion/reporta-imss-cifra-record-empleo-formal-en-septiembre-2026)

### Inferences
- Rough real-wage estimate: MXN 666 against an annual 2025 average of MXN 624.77 is about +6.6% nominal. That is not a clean y/y comparison (September 2025 SBC was not retrieved), but at 3.45% CPI it implies roughly 3% real wage growth. This is supportive for packaged-food demand at the low end.
- Bimbo has a large route-sales and plant workforce, so the 40-hour phase-in adds about 4% to hourly labor costs in 2027 and about 20% cumulative by 2030, unless offset by productivity or schedule redesign. This is a material margin headwind on the Mexico adj. EBITDA margin of about 20.7%.

### Gaps
- The y/y nominal and real IMSS SBC growth for September 2026 was not reported in the sources found.
- No CONASAMI guidance or president's statement on the size of the 2027 increase was found. The administration had previously targeted the minimum wage covering 2.5 basic baskets by 2030 (from memory, unverified).
- The 2025 minimum wage increase was not verified with a source in this session.

## 5. Consumption: private consumption, ANTAD, consumer confidence, Bienestar transfers

### Takeaway
Consumption is soft after the World Cup. Private consumption was flat in July. ANTAD same-store sales rose only 1.7% nominal in August, which is negative in real terms. Consumer confidence fell 1.2 points in September to 45.1, the largest drop in 10 months, with all five components down.

### Cited Findings
- **INEGI private consumption (IOCP):** 0.0% m/m in July 2026 (published 2026-10-05). August is not yet released — [summary of search results; see UnoTV/INEGI coverage](https://www.unotv.com/negocios/confianza-consumidor-mexico-cae-inegi-economia-empleo-compras/) *(note: the July consumption figure came from the search engine's summary; I could not open a dedicated article to confirm it)*
- **ANTAD:** same-store sales rose 1.7% nominal in August 2026. ANTAD's 2026 targets are +3.9% same-store and +6.3% total-store nominal. In 2025, autoservicio sales grew 3.1% and total ANTAD sales were MXN 1,675.5 bn. The September 2025 base was weak at +0.5% same-store — [ANTAD indicators](https://antad.net/indicadores/crecimiento-nominal-en-ventas/); [La Jornada (2025 full year)](https://www.jornada.com.mx/noticia/2026/02/03/economia/ventas-de-tiendas-de-autoservicio-suben-31-en-2025); [Urbe Económica](https://urbeconomica.com/antad-cerro-2025-con-ventas-por-1-675-5-mil-millones/); [Retailers.mx (Sep-2025 +0.5%)](https://retailers.mx/antad-crecio-0-5-en-ventas-de-septiembre-a-tiendas-iguales/)
- **Consumer confidence (INEGI ENCO, published 2026-10-06):** 45.1 points in September 2026, down 1.2 from August and down 1.0 from September 2025. All five components fell. Ability to buy durables fell to 32.0 (-0.5). This was the deepest monthly drop in 10 months, after three monthly gains — [UnoTV](https://www.unotv.com/negocios/confianza-consumidor-mexico-cae-inegi-economia-empleo-compras/); [Diario.mx](https://diario.mx/economia/2026/oct/06/registra-confianza-del-consumidor-mayor-caida-en-10-meses-1141968.html); [Líder Empresarial](https://www.liderempresarial.com/confianza-del-consumidor-cae-en-septiembre-de-2026-revela-inegi/)
- The IEPS increase on soft drinks hit "tienditas" (traditional channel) traffic and Coca-Cola volumes in Mexico in 1H26 — [El Financiero, 2026-05-12](https://www.elfinanciero.com.mx/empresas/2026/05/12/falto-la-coca-ieps-a-bebidas-azucaradas-les-pega-a-las-tienditas/); [El Imparcial, 2026-05-04](https://www.elimparcial.com/dinero/2026/05/04/el-aumento-al-ieps-en-bebidas-azucaradas-impuesto-por-claudia-sheinbaum-ya-impacto-a-coca-cola-y-a-pequenos-comercios-mexicanos-con-menor-consumo-precios-mucho-mas-altos-y-compras-aun-mas-pequenas/)

### Inferences
- ANTAD growth of +1.7% nominal against about 3.3% CPI means negative real same-store sales in August. Formal retail is lagging the minimum-wage-driven income gains.
- Lower traffic in the traditional channel from soft-drink IEPS is relevant because Bimbo shares the tiendita basket with soft drinks (the "torta + refresco" occasion). This is a second-order volume risk.

### Gaps
- ANTAD September 2026 had not been published. The August 2026 private-consumption reading and its y/y figure were not found.
- **The 2026 budget for Bienestar social programs** (pensions for older adults, Becas, Mujeres Bienestar) and the 2027 proposal were not retrieved in this session. This is an important gap for low-income consumption support.

## 6. Remittances

### Takeaway
Remittances are flat to slightly up in USD in 2026 but falling sharply in real peso terms, which removes a support for low-income consumption in central and southern states. 2025 was down 3.9%.

### Cited Findings
- **2025:** remittances fell 3.9% (cited by Banamex) — [La Silla Rota, 2026-10-01](https://lasillarota.com/negocios/2026/10/1/remesas-caen-36-en-agosto-banamex-preve-recuperacion-moderada-531089.html)

  - USD 5,452 mn, down 3.6% y/y and the worst drop in nine months.

## 7. USMCA/T-MEC joint review and US tariffs on Mexico

### Takeaway
At the 2026-07-01 joint review the US **declined to renew** USMCA, so the agreement moves to annual reviews and, absent renewal, expires on 2036-07-01. Preferential terms remain in force. The US Supreme Court struck down IEEPA "fentanyl" tariffs on 2026-02-20. They were replaced by a 10% Section 122 surcharge, which expired 2026-07-24, and then by a 10% Section 301 (forced-labor) duty on Mexican goods from 2026-07-24, with USMCA-compliant goods exempt. Section 232 tariffs of 50% on steel/aluminum and tariffs on autos persist, and a Section 301 excess-capacity probe including Mexico is pending.

### Cited Findings
- **2026-07-01 joint review:** USTR said USMCA is not renewed in its current form and that the US will keep engaging on "shortcomings" and deficits. Annual reviews follow, with expiry on 2036-07-01 absent renewal. Mexico and Canada supported a 16-year extension. Preferential tariffs, rules of origin and dispute settlement are unchanged — [Thompson Hine SmarTrade](https://www.thompsonhinesmartrade.com/2026/07/ustr-issues-statement-on-status-of-usmca-review/); [UC Davis MexAg](https://mexag.ucdavis.edu/reports/2026-07-08-usmca-review-report.html); [CRS R48787](https://www.congress.gov/crs-product/R48787); [FreightFigures](https://www.freightfigures.com/articles/usmca-2026-joint-review-not-renewed-importer-impact)
- **Open issues:** the US is demanding 50% US content in autos. Mexico seeks Section 232 relief on autos, steel and aluminum. USTR Greer said there may be "options for … interim arrangements" by end-2026 — [AS/COA tracker](https://www.as-coa.org/articles/tracking-us-mexico-talks-usmca-review); [CSIS](https://www.csis.org/analysis/usmca-review-2026)
- **IEEPA:** on 2026-02-20 the Supreme Court ruled 6-3 in *Learning Resources v. Trump* that IEEPA does not authorize tariffs, invalidating the Mexico/Canada fentanyl tariffs. CBP stopped collecting on 2026-02-24. Before the ruling, Mexico's fentanyl rate was 25% on non-USMCA goods; sources conflict on whether it was raised to 35% for Mexico or only for Canada — [Steptoe](https://www.steptoe.com/en/news-publications/global-trade-and-investment-law-blog/us-supreme-court-strikes-down-ieepa-tariffs.html); [BDO](https://www.bdo.com/insights/tax/supreme-court-invalidates-ieepa-tariffs-administration-replaces-with-new-surcharge-what-importers); [Baker Donelson](https://www.bakerdonelson.com/trade-policy-shifts-ieepa-tariffs-end-section-122-begins-and-sections-301-and-232-activity-grows)
- **Section 122:** a 10% global surcharge ran from 2026-02-24, with USMCA-compliant goods exempt. It expired on 2026-07-24 after its 150-day limit and was not extended. The CIT had ruled it unlawful on 2026-05-07; that appeal is pending — [Sandler Travis (STR)](https://www.strtrade.com/trade-news-resources/tariff-actions-resources/section-122-tariffs); [Kelley Drye](https://www.kelleydrye.com/viewpoints/blogs/trade-and-manufacturing-monitor/understanding-the-new-section-122-tariffs-and-the-end-of-ieepa-tariff-actions)
- **Section 301 forced labor:** USTR proposed the duty on 2026-06-02 and took final action on 2026-07-23, imposing a **10% tariff on products of Mexico** (except Annex I/II) effective 2026-07-24. Ebrard said it simply replaces the Section 122 10%. A modification followed on 2026-07-31 (content unverified) — [Federal Register 2026-15181, 2026-07-28](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/07/28/2026-15181/notice-of-actions-in-section-301-investigations-of-acts-policies-and-practices-of-various-economies); [Prodensa](https://www.prodensa.com/insights/blog/section-301-tariffs-mexico); [USTR report, 2026-06-02](https://ustr.gov/sites/default/files/files/Press/Releases/2026/USTR%20Report%20Sec%20301%20FL%20301%206-2-26%20FINAL%20for%20upload.pdf)
- **Section 301 excess capacity:** launched 2026-03-11 against 16 economies including Mexico. Final action was still pending as of late July 2026, and no August or September determination was found — [USTR fact sheet](https://ustr.gov/about/policy-offices/press-office/fact-sheets/2026/march/fact-sheet-ustr-initiates-section-301-investigations-structural-excess-capacity-and-production); [Mexico Business News](https://mexicobusiness.news/trade-and-investment/news/mexico-faces-new-tariff-round-301-probe-nears-close)
- **Section 232:** steel and aluminum tariffs were reimposed on Mexico in March 2025 and raised to 50% in June 2025 (pre-2026 actions), and they remain in place — [Rio Times USMCA guide](https://www.riotimesonline.com/usmca-2026-trade-pact-guide/)

### Inferences
- **Direct Bimbo exposure is limited.** Bakery is produced locally in each market, and Mexico-to-US food that is USMCA-compliant (most agri-food qualifies) still enters at 0%. The 10% Section 301 duty applies mainly to non-compliant goods; whether it covers compliant goods depends on Annex treatment, which was not verified.
- **Indirect exposure is material.** Annual reviews create a recurring uncertainty overhang through 2036. Banxico explicitly links weak investment to USMCA uncertainty. Tariff-related peso volatility also affects Bimbo's USD-denominated wheat and packaging inputs.
- Bimbo's aluminum or tin packaging and its equipment imports may carry Section 232-related cost pass-through, but this is small.

### Gaps
- The outcome of the September 2026 US-Mexico negotiating round and any excess-capacity tariff determination after July were not found.
- The exact treatment of USMCA-compliant goods under the Section 301 10% duty (Annex I/II) was not verified.
- **Mexico's own tariff decree** (higher tariffs on imports from non-FTA countries such as China, approved late 2025) was not researched in this session.
- No source addressed cross-border bakery or snack trade specifically.

## 8. Nearshoring, FDI and Plan México

### Takeaway
FDI hit a nominal record of USD 34.97 bn in 1H26 (+2.1%). But 88.5% was reinvested earnings, and new investment fell sharply, so nearshoring momentum is stalling under USMCA uncertainty.

### Cited Findings

Reinvested earnings were 88.5% of the flow — [gob.mx/SE](https://www.gob.mx/se/prensa/mexico-registra-un-maximo-historico-de-inversion-extranjera-directa-ied-para-un-primer-semestre-34-mil-968-millones-de-dolares-en-2026); [La Jornada](https://www.jornada.com.mx/noticia/2026/08/24/economia/registra-inversion-extranjera-directa-record-en-el-primer-semestre-del-2026)
- **New investments:** USD 2,726 mn, down 42% y/y per Gaceta Mexicana, while an El Financiero-cited specialist gives a 13% decline. ## 9. Fiscal: 2026/2027 budget, deficit, IEPS "impuesto saludable", Pemex, ratings

### Takeaway
The 2026 package nearly doubled the IEPS on sugary drinks (from MXN 1.64 to 3.08 per litre) and added a tax on zero-sugar drinks, which visibly hit soft-drink volumes. The 8% IEPS on high-calorie snacks and confectionery (over 275 kcal per 100 g), which covers Bimbo's cakes, cookies and Barcel snacks, was unchanged. The 2027 package (submitted 2026-09-08) announced no new taxes or rate increases, but NGOs are lobbying to raise the snack IEPS to 20% and Congress said sodium and alcohol items remain "pending". Ratings sit one notch above junk at Moody's (Baa3, cut 2026-05-20) and Fitch (BBB-), and S&P is at BBB with a negative outlook (2026-05-12).

### Cited Findings
- **IEPS 2026 on drinks:** sugary drinks went from MXN 1.64 to 3.0818 per litre (about +87%). Drinks with non-caloric sweeteners pay MXN 1.50 per litre for the first time — [Info7](https://www.info7.mx/nacional/ieps-2026-productos-que-suben-de-precio-e-impactan-al-bolsillo/v3291883818); [Expansión, 2025-12-31](https://expansion.mx/economia/2025/12/31/ieps-impuesto-2026); [Lyra Consultores](https://lyraconsultores.com/cambiaste-a-cero-azucar-para-no-pagar-ieps-en-2026-igual-pagas/)

El Poder del Consumidor and Fundar propose raising the rate to 20%. On 2026-09-02, Finance Committee chair Carol Antonio Altamirano said sodium and alcohol items remain pending for discussion. The article proposes no change for pan dulce — [Expansión, 2026-09-02](https://expansion.mx/economia/2026/09/02/impuestos-sopas-maruchan-botanas-alcohol-ieps-2027)

### Inferences
- **The key BIMBOA tax risk is an increase in the snack and confectionery IEPS** from 8% (for example to the NGO-proposed 20%) applied to Barcel snacks, Marinela/Gansito-type sweet baked goods and cookies above 275 kcal per 100 g. - The soft-drink precedent (an 87% rate increase leading to visible volume declines) shows the volume elasticity risk if snack IEPS rises.
- Tight fiscal space (RFSP 3.9%, debt about 55%, Pemex support) together with ratings one notch above junk limits countercyclical support and raises the risk of further "health" tax revenue measures in 2027-2028.
