# US Consumer and Packaged Bakery / Snacks Environment for Grupo Bimbo North America (as of 2026-10-08)

Scope: US demand, category volumes, GLP-1, MAHA/regulation, private label, food inflation, labor, Fed (brief), Canada (brief). Team context (not re-verified here): Bimbo NA sales excl-FX -4.0% FY25, +0.7% 1Q26, +1.3% 2Q26; NA EBITDA margin 10.0% in 2Q26.

## 1. US consumer spending, sentiment, low-income stress and SNAP

### Takeaway
Real spending is still growing (real PCE +0.6% m/m in Aug-2026), but sentiment is at crisis lows (UMich 48.1, Conference Board 81.9, lowest since 2014), driven by prices, a September fuel-price surge and a Fed rate hike. Low-income households are under the most pressure, and SNAP enrollment is down more than 4.5 million since OBBBA. State soda/candy waivers hit beverages and candy directly. No source quantifies an effect on bread or salty snacks.

### Cited Findings
**Spending and income (BEA, released 2026-09-30)**
- August 2026: real PCE +0.6% m/m (+$92.8bn), nominal PCE +0.9% m/m. Personal income +0.2%, DPI +0.3%, real DPI 0.0% m/m. July real PCE was +0.1% m/m — [BEA Personal Income and Outlays, Aug-2026](https://bea.gov/news/2026/personal-income-and-outlays-august-2026)
- PCE price index +0.3% m/m and +3.4% y/y. Core +0.2% m/m and +3.0% y/y. Saving rate 4.1% (July 4.6% after revision). The release included benchmark revisions back to 2021 — [BEA](https://bea.gov/news/2026/personal-income-and-outlays-august-2026); [First Trust, 2026-09-30](https://www.ftportfolios.com/Commentary/EconomicResearch/2026/9/30/personal-income-rose-0.2percent-in-august)
- One secondary source calls August's real PCE gain the largest monthly increase since March 2025 — [First Trust, 2026-09-30](https://www.ftportfolios.com/Commentary/EconomicResearch/2026/9/30/personal-income-rose-0.2percent-in-august)

**Sentiment**
- UMich final September 2026: 48.1, released 2026-09-25 (prelim 47.8; August 51.7; -12.7% y/y). Current conditions 50.9, expectations 46.3. Year-ahead inflation expectations rose from 4.0% to 4.6%. Survey director Joanne Hsu said views of personal finances fell about 10%, on high prices, elevated fuel prices and renewed trade disputes — [Advisor Perspectives, 2026-09-25](https://www.advisorperspectives.com/dshort/updates/2026/09/25/consumer-sentiment-falls-again-in-september); [Trading Economics](https://tradingeconomics.com/united-states/consumer-confidence/news/586955)
- Conference Board September 2026: 81.9, down from 88.6 and the lowest since 2014 (consensus 89.2). Expectations Index 63.6, below the 80 recession signal every month since Feb-2025. Present Situation 109.3. Write-ins cite prices and oil/gas. The survey window included a federal funds rate hike — [GV Wire/Reuters, 2026-09-29](https://gvwire.com/2026/09/29/us-consumer-confidence-dives-to-more-than-12-year-low-in-september/); [Conference Board podcast](https://www.conference-board.org/podcasts/c-suite-perspectives/State-of-the-Economy-for-September-2026-Consumer-Confidence-Takes-a-Tumble)

**Low-income stress and household credit**
- NY Fed Q2 2026 (released 2026-08-11): household debt $18.8tn (-$13bn q/q). 4.73% of debt delinquent (Q1 4.76%). Credit-card flow into serious delinquency 6.97% (Q2-25 6.93%). Mortgage flow into serious delinquency 1.52% (Q2-25 1.29%). The NY Fed said new auto and card delinquencies "remain at elevated levels" — [NY Fed press release](https://www.newyorkfed.org/newsevents/news/research/2026/20260811); [ABA Banking Journal](https://bankingjournal.aba.com/2026/08/new-york-fed-household-debt-dipped-to-18-8t-in-q2/)
- Numerator: base spending by lower-income households has declined in recent months and financial stress is rising. Staples and QSR are more stable than discretionary retail — [Numerator Visions 2026](https://www.numerator.com/visions-consumer-trends/)
- McKinsey Q2-2026 survey: the intent to cut back is strongest among low-income consumers — [McKinsey State of the US Consumer](https://www.mckinsey.com/industries/consumer-packaged-goods/our-insights/the-state-of-the-us-consumer)
- Counterpoint: BofA economists said that excluding gasoline, lower-income spending growth recently outpaced higher-income growth, and NRF said no cohort cut spending in 1H26 y/y. NRF flagged gas prices as the key risk for low-income consumers — [Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/economy/article/k-shaped-spending-trends-might-be-reversing--or-theyre-just-in-hibernation-191631697.html)
- Mondelez Q2-26: consumer sentiment "subdued" on inflation concerns. Growth is "K-shaped," with shoppers choosing either value packs or premium formats — [Baking Business](https://www.bakingbusiness.com/articles/66657-north-america-bolsters-mondelez-in-q2)

**SNAP (OBBBA 2025 and state waivers)**
- Preliminary USDA data: SNAP participation fell by more than 4.5 million (1.5 million of them children) between OBBBA's July 2025 enactment and April 2026. OBBBA set work requirements of 80 hrs/month for ages 18-64 and narrowed exemptions (veterans, homeless, former foster youth) — [NPR, 2026-08-03](https://www.npr.org/2026/08/03/nx-s1-5906797/snap-food-stamp-federal-changes-what-to-know)
- Other trackers: about 5 million lost (CBPP), or enrollment down 13% to 36.4M by June 2026. CBO estimates work requirements alone cut average monthly participation by 2.4M over 2025-2034 — [Propel](https://www.propel.app/snap/cuts-to-snap-big-beautiful-bill-food-stamps-changes/); [Stateline, 2026-04-10](https://stateline.org/2026/04/10/snap-work-requirements-dont-boost-jobs-but-drop-participation-research-finds/)
- The state benefit cost-share tied to error rates (based on FY ending Sept-2026 error rates) is slated to start in October 2027. Experts warn states could scale back the program. Exact timing differs across sources — [ABC News](https://abcnews.com/US/millions-dropped-fromsnapfood-assistance-funding-faces-september-uncertainty/story?id=136386092); [NPR](https://www.npr.org/2026/08/03/nx-s1-5906797/snap-food-stamp-federal-changes-what-to-know)
- State waivers: 19 states will restrict SNAP purchases of soda/candy by end-2026, covering about one-third of participants. Numerator estimates lost sales of $830M: soda $430M, candy $300M, energy drinks $100M. 63% (soda) and 60% (candy) of SNAP shoppers say they would pay with non-SNAP dollars instead — [Shelby Report, 2026-05-27](https://theshelbyreport.com/2026/05/27/report-19-states-to-restrict-snap-soda-candy-purchases-by-end-of-2026/); [Food Business News](https://www.foodbusinessnews.net/articles/30403-lost-sales-from-snap-waivers-may-reach-830-million-in-2026); [FoodNavigator-USA, 2026-06-03](https://www.foodnavigator-usa.com/Article/2026/06/03/snap-restrictions-could-cost-grocery-and-c-stores/)
- A separate March-2026 count put participating states at 22. An AP headline dated 2026-06-23 (cited on Wikipedia) reports a judge ruling the government cannot stop SNAP dollars from buying candy/sugary drinks. Scope and appeal status are unverified — [Wikipedia: SNAP food restriction waivers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SNAP_food_restriction_waivers)
- A Farm Bill amendment proposing a national SNAP exclusion of unhealthy foods was under debate in 2026 — [FoodNavigator-USA, 2026-04-30](https://www.foodnavigator-usa.com/Article/2026/04/30/farm-bill-leaves-snap-rules-unchanged-for-now-as-amendment-fight-looms/)

### Inferences
- The macro picture is aggregate spending holding up while sentiment, real income (real DPI flat in August) and low-income cohorts deteriorate. That pattern favors value/price-point packs and private label over premium branded bread volumes, and matches the "persistently challenging" language from Bimbo and Flowers.
- The SNAP enrollment loss (about 11-13%) removes purchasing power from exactly the households that over-index on mainstream white bread, buns and snack cakes. The waivers I found target soda, candy and energy drinks. If waivers broaden (for example, a Farm Bill national exclusion), snack cakes (Bimbo's Little Bites, Hostess, Little Debbie) would be the next exposed category.
- Rising fuel prices (Sept-2026) hurt Bimbo twice: consumers have less discretionary income, and its DSD fleet costs more to run.

### Gaps
- I found no quantification of SNAP cuts' effect on bread or salty snack sales. Some packaged-food companies discussed SNAP qualitatively, but I did not find figures.
- I did not verify which state waivers include "prepared desserts" or snack cakes.
- The OBBBA benefit-formula changes (Thrifty Food Plan cost neutrality) are not covered here.

## 2. Category data: bread, sweet baked goods, salty snacks (2025-2026)

### Takeaway
US fresh packaged bread is in volume decline. Flowers' Q2-26 volume fell 5.8% (branded retail -7.6%), and an analyst cited fresh packaged bread category volume down 9.5% (management did not confirm it). Circana shows center-store bread dollars about -1.4% to -2.4% and units -3.6%, while sourdough grows about 30%. Sweet baked goods are also contracting (Hostess segment -7%). Salty snacks are stabilizing only after price cuts (PepsiCo PFNA volume +0.5pt with pricing -1pt in Q3-26; Campbell's Snacks volume/mix -6% in Q4 FY26). Against this backdrop, Bimbo's claim of US share gains in every category in 2Q26 stands out.

## 3. GLP-1 adoption, oral launches, and measured impact

### Takeaway
About 12% of US adults currently take a GLP-1 (KFF, Nov-2025), and about 22-23% of households include a user (Numerator/Circana 2026). Oral pills launched in 2026: Wegovy pill in January, Lilly's Foundayo in April, both at about $149/month for the lowest cash dose. These should broaden adoption. Measured spending effects are modest at the basket level (about -4% after one year, Numerator Aug-2026; packaged F&B about -1.6%, Circana) but concentrated in chips, baked goods, cookies and bread (-6.7% to -11.1%, Cornell/Numerator).

### Cited Findings
- KFF poll (fielded 2025-10-27 to 11-02): 18% of adults have ever used a GLP-1 and 12% currently use one (6% in May-2024). Current use is 22% among ages 50-64 and 15% among women vs 9% among men — [KFF](https://www.kff.org/public-opinion/poll-1-in-8-adults-say-they-are-currently-taking-a-glp-1-drug-for-weight-loss-diabetes-or-another-condition-even-as-half-say-the-drugs-are-difficult-to-afford/)
- Gallup 2026: 11% of adults currently take a GLP-1 for weight loss (3% in 2024). The question differs from KFF's — [Forbes Health](https://www.forbes.com/health/weight-loss/glp-1-statistics/)
- Numerator (2026-08-07): 22% of households include a current user, double Oct-2023. After one year, GLP-1 households spend nearly 4% less on groceries than matched non-users. Spending shifts away from pantry staples and "carb-heavy items such as pasta and bakery goods" toward protein, fiber and intentional snacks — [Grocery Dive](https://www.grocerydive.com/news/glp1-weight-loss-drugs-medication-grocery-spend-numerator/827277/)
- Circana: nearly 23% of households have a user (about 11% two years earlier). Circana projects GLP-1 households will account for 35% of F&B units by 2030. It finds packaged F&B spending down only about 1.6% after one year, and says cost concerns are a bigger driver of changing habits — [FreshFruitPortal, 2026-01-26](https://www.freshfruitportal.com/news/2026/01/26/glp-1-grocery-circana/); [The Fence Post](https://www.thefencepost.com/news/circana-costs-then-glp-1-drugs-change-food-purchases/)
- Cornell/Numerator study (Journal of Marketing Research): grocery spend -5.3% within six months of starting. Savory snacks about -10%. Chips, baked goods and cookies -6.7% to -11.1%. Bread also declined, and higher-income households fell about 9%. Yogurt, fresh fruit, nutrition bars and meat snacks rose — [Food Business News](https://www.foodbusinessnews.net/articles/29532-glp-1-users-cut-food-spending-by-53); [Food Dive](https://www.fooddive.com/news/glp-1-drug-use-cuts-grocery-spending-by-6-study-finds/736313/); [ScienceDaily, 2026-01](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2026/01/260112001029.htm)
- Morgan Stanley (brief published around April 2026): US users about 55M by 2035 (previously 33M). Base case is a 1.6% reduction in annual US calories by 2035, with the decline accelerating before moderating in 2029. Lower persistence and higher discontinuation temper the impact. Cakes, cookies, chips and candy fall more than 4% by 2035, and ice cream 5.3% — [CMC Markets summary](https://www.cmcmarkets.com/en-gb/news-and-analysis/glp-1-drugs-are-changing-the-food-market-how-much-could-the-industry-lose-by-2030); [Food Business News (headline)](https://www.foodbusinessnews.net/articles/30123-morgan-stanley-research-raises-glp-1-usage-projection)
- Other estimates: FTI Consulting puts about $95bn of packaged-food spending at risk by 2030. JPMorgan puts F&B revenue at risk at $30-55bn/yr by 2030 — [CMC Markets](https://www.cmcmarkets.com/en-gb/news-and-analysis/glp-1-drugs-are-changing-the-food-market-how-much-could-the-industry-lose-by-2030)
- Wegovy pill (Novo): US launch 2026-01-05. Cash price $149/month for 1.5mg and 4mg (4mg rising to $199 after Apr-15) and $299 for 9/25mg. As low as $25 for commercially insured patients — [Fierce Pharma](https://www.fiercepharma.com/pharma/novos-wegovy-pill-makes-us-debut-starter-dose-launching-149-month-cash-paying-patients); [CNN, 2026-01-05](https://www.cnn.com/2026/01/05/health/wegovy-pill-weight-loss)
- Foundayo (orforglipron, Lilly): FDA approval 2026-04-01 via priority voucher (50-day review). Self-pay $149-$349/month by dose, $25 with insurance savings card, about $50/month for Medicare Part D from July 2026, and also on TrumpRx from about $150. ATTAIN-1 weight loss was 12.4% at the top dose — [Fierce Pharma](https://www.fiercepharma.com/pharma/lilly-answers-novos-glp-1-pill-highly-anticipated-fda-nod-foundayo); [KMPH](https://kmph.com/news/nation-world/fda-food-and-drug-administration-grants-expedited-approval-to-eli-lillys-glp-1-weight-loss-pill-foundayo-obesity-orforglipron-appetite-fullness-trump-rx-wegovy)
- Industry response: Flowers is launching protein breads and half loaves. Campbell's is extending Goldfish into protein, whole grain and gluten-free. Circana reports protein-claim bakery units up sharply — [Investing.com](https://www.investing.com/news/transcripts/earnings-call-transcript-flowers-foods-misses-q2-2026-estimates-as-bread-volumes-fall-93CH-4871639); [Investing.com Campbell's](https://www.investing.com/news/company-news/campbells-q4-fy26-slides-meals-growth-offset-by-snacks-pressure-93CH-4887625)

### Inferences
- Pill pricing at about $149/month plus Medicare Part D coverage from July-2026 should extend adoption to older and lower-income cohorts in 2026-2028. These are core buyers of mainstream bread and snack cakes. A plausible structural drag on bread/sweet-baked-goods volume is about 0.5-1.5pt per year (my inference from about 22% household penetration times roughly 5-10% category cuts, phased in), not a cliff.
- Half-loaf, protein and portion-pack formats are the main defensive levers. Bimbo would need equivalent offerings (Oroweat/Arnold protein, smaller packs).

### Gaps
- I found no 2026 prescription or volume data for the oral pills.
- I did not find a Goldman Sachs 2026 GLP-1 food report, or a 2026 KFF update on current use.
- No study isolates sweet baked goods versus bread.

## 4. Health / MAHA regulation and trends

### Takeaway
Federal action is mostly voluntary or still pending as of Aug-2026: the dye phase-out is voluntary, the UPF definition is a white paper under OMB review, and front-of-pack labeling is proposed but not final. State laws are the sharper risk. Texas SB 25 lists bleached flour, DATEM and dyes but is preliminarily enjoined. California AB 1264 defines UPFs for schools, with dye bans from end-2027 and a phase-out by 2029-2032.

### Cited Findings
- The FDA synthetic dye phase-out is voluntary, with no binding ban on the remaining six dyes. Sources conflict on a target of end-2026 vs end-2027. Revocations of Orange B and Citrus Red No. 2 were expected in 2026 — [MarketScale](https://www.marketscale.com/industries/food-beverage/fda-slows-synthetic-dye-phase-out-as-160-food-and-ag-groups-push-to-renew-usmca); [DWT, 2026-03](https://www.dwt.com/insights/2026/03/2026-ingredients-regulation-and-litigation)
- UPF definition: FDA chose a white paper over a proposed rule, which was under OMB review as of Aug-2026. It has limited legal force but significant policy impact (it could underpin a UPF claim or front-of-pack symbol) — [FoodNavigator, 2026-08-06](https://www.foodnavigator.com/Article/2026/08/06/fda-upf-white-paper-could-reshape-food-policy/); [FoodNavigator, 2026-06-16](https://www.foodnavigator.com/Article/2026/06/16/fda-ultra-processed-food-definition-could-reshape-front-of-pack-labels/)
