# Macro outlook for Grupo Bimbo's LatAm (~11% of sales) and EAA (~13% of sales) segments, as of 2026-10-08

Scope: Brazil, Colombia, Argentina, Peru, Chile, Central America (LatAm). Eurozone/ECB, Spain, UK, Romania, India, China, Morocco (EAA). Research date: 2026-10-08. The IMF October 2026 WEO was **not yet published** on this date. It normally appears mid-October. The latest IMF set is the **July 2026 WEO Update** (released 8 Jul 2026, data cut-off 10 Jun 2026).

**Cross-cutting theme.** A 2026 Middle East war and a Strait of Hormuz disruption caused an energy shock. The IMF projects crude oil +32% and fertilizer +26% in 2026, and **global food prices +8%**. Disinflation has "stalled", with global headline CPI at 4.7% in 2026 ([IMF WEO Update Jul-2026 PDF](https://www.imf.org/-/media/files/publications/weo/2026/update/july/english/text.pdf); [IMF press briefing 8-Jul-2026](https://www.imf.org/en/news/articles/2026/07/08/tr070826-weo-press-briefing-transcript-july-8-2026)). As a result, several central banks have turned hawkish: the ECB, BanRep and RBI hiked, and the BoE and BCRP are split or under pressure. Brazil is the exception and is still cutting. A forecast El Niño adds food-price risk in the Andean countries.

## Brazil: GDP, IPCA, Selic path, BRL, election risk, food inflation, wheat import cost

### Takeaway
Brazil is the only large Bimbo market still easing. The Selic is at 13.75%, down from 15.00%, after five 25 bp cuts. Food is deflating and the BRL is up about 10% YTD, which lowers the cost of imported wheat. The 25-Oct-2026 runoff is the main swing factor: Flávio Bolsonaro led round 1 and prediction markets favour him.

### Cited Findings
- 2025 GDP grew 2.3%, with agriculture +11.7%, services +1.8% and industry +1.4%; nominal GDP was R$12.7tn — [IBGE via Poder360, 3-Mar-2026](https://www.poder360.com.br/poder-economia/pib-do-brasil-cresce-23-em-2025/)
- IMF Jul-2026 projects 2.4% for 2026 (+0.5pp vs April) and 2.2% for 2027. It describes Brazil as "resilient in 2026" but slowing next year — [IMF WEO Update Jul-2026](https://www.imf.org/-/media/files/publications/weo/2026/update/july/english/text.pdf)
- Focus survey (5-Oct-2026) medians: 2026 IPCA 5.01% (3rd straight weekly rise), 2027 IPCA 4.30%, 2026 GDP 1.85%, Selic 13.50% at end-2026, BRL 5.20 at end-2026 — [Rio Times, Oct-2026](https://www.riotimesonline.com/brazil-focus-survey-inflation-october-2026/). This is secondary reporting of the BCB Focus survey.
  - The Focus inflation path (~5%) is well above the latest actual print of 4.22%. This needs reconciling once the Sep IPCA is released on 9-Oct.
- IPCA Aug-2026: −0.32% m/m and 4.22% y/y. Food and beverages fell 0.34% m/m, the third monthly decline after June and July, driven by food at home — [InfoMoney, 11-Sep-2026](https://www.infomoney.com.br/economia/ipca-inflacao-agosto-2026-dados-ibge/)

Next meeting is 3–4 Nov 2026 — [Ricoaospoucos](https://ricoaospoucos.com.br/en/interest-rate/); [Rio Times](https://www.riotimesonline.com/brazil-selic-cut-95-percent-copom-september-2026/). An earlier step is confirmed by the BCB: the cut to 14.25% — [BCB press note](https://www.bcb.gov.br/en/pressdetail/2673/nota)
- BRL: 4.9929/USD on 7-Oct-2026 against 5.5018 on 31-Dec-2025 — [IMF rates Oct-2026](https://www.imf.org/external/np/fin/data/rms_mth.aspx?reportType=REP); [IMF rates Dec-2025](https://www.imf.org/external/np/fin/data/rms_mth.aspx?SelectDate=2025-12-31&reportType=REP). - Wheat: Conab (13-Aug-2026) projects 2026/27 imports of 7.103 Mt, the highest in nearly two decades, as domestic output falls to 5.8 Mt. ### Inferences
- A stronger BRL and Argentine wheat priced competitively in BRL should limit cost inflation for Bimbo Brasil and Wickbold, despite record import volumes. Global fertilizer and energy shocks are the offset.
- Food deflation is a mixed signal. It supports volumes, but it limits pricing power for packaged bread.
- The early-October BRL rally coincides with a market-friendly first round. A Lula comeback on 25-Oct is the key FX downside risk.

## Argentina: inflation trend 2026, peso regime, GDP rebound, consumption, IAS 29

### Takeaway
Monthly inflation sits around 1.7–2% and is not falling further. Y/y inflation is about 33%, with 21.3% YTD to August. The peso is still in an inflation-indexed band, trading 26% below the ceiling. Activity is stalling in 2026 after +4.4% in 2025, and consumer-facing sectors (retail, manufacturing) are contracting. IAS 29 hyperinflation accounting continues to apply.
