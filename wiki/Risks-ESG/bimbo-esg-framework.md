## 1. Strategy structure

- Strategy "Alimentamos un mundo mejor" launched 2021, replacing the pre-2021 four pillars (health and wellness, planet, community, employees) [sourced, SFF p.6; RA25 p.79]. It integrates a double-materiality approach and the UN SDGs [sourced, RA25 p.79].
- Three pillars plus foundations [sourced, RA25 p.79; SFF p.6]:

| Pillar | Initiatives |
|---|---|
| Para Ti (nutrition) | Better nutritional profiles for all; sustainable and transparent brands; healthier plant-based diets |
| Para la Vida (people and communities) | Strengthen communities; care for our people |
| Para la Naturaleza (nature) | Zero waste; net-zero carbon; regenerative agriculture |
| Cimientos (foundations) | Environmental standards and sustainable buildings; food quality and safety; human rights and labour standards; sustainable raw-material sourcing |

- Commitments tied to the UN Global Compact (signatory since 2014) and its ten principles [sourced, IC25 p.174; SFF p.5]; business ambition for 1.5 °C and Race to Zero joined in 2021 (first Mexican food company to commit) [sourced, RA25 p.75]; RE100 member since 2018 [sourced, RA25 p.95].
- Reporting frameworks used in IC25: CSRD/ESRS, GRI, ISSB S1/S2, SASB processed foods, information requested by the Mexican Stock Exchange via S&P Global for its sustainability index, UN SDGs [sourced, IC25 p.174]. Climate risk follows TCFD, being aligned to IFRS S2 [sourced, IC25 p.158].

## 4. Governance of ESG

[sourced, IC25 pp.143–146, 158–159, 153–155 unless noted]

- Board of Directors: highest body responsible for sustainability strategy and commitments; directors get annual sustainability training (2025: anti-corruption and integrity). Executive/steering committee takes part. CEO leads integration and reports to the Board.
- Chief Supply Chain and Sustainability Officer (CSCSO) designs and implements the strategy; the VP-level Global Sustainability function reports to the CSCSO and informs the steering committee twice a year. RA25 p.190 records the Supply Chain officer appointment dated 2024-01-05 [sourced]; confirm the person/role mapping in [[bimbo-governance-and-ownership]].
- Mechanisms: Global Sustainability Committee (accountability to steering committee and board members), Global Sustainability Board (chairman, steering committee, functional directors, CSCSO), Operating Committee (quarterly), 1:1 sessions with champions (semi-annual), Regional Sustainability Committees (quarterly) and local champions.
- Climate: TCFD structure (governance, strategy, risk management, metrics); climate studies updated every 3 years and risk reviews every 4 months; quarterly reports to senior management and the Global Risk Committee; board-level Global Sustainability Committee gets climate updates; water stress now among prioritised strategic risks (IC25 pp.158–159).
- Enterprise risk management: five-stage cycle; quarterly global-risk updates to the Board in 2025; "Torre de Control" digital tracking; monetary quantification guide for risk owners (IC25 pp.153–154). Global risks listed with mitigation plans: cybersecurity, natural-resource/water crisis, consumption habits, food safety, "Mexican context" (regulation, tariffs), workplace safety (IC25 p.155).
- Double-materiality update in 2025 (previous exercise 2022, every three years): 11 doubly material topics, 5 impact-only, 2 financial-only, 4 emerging; extracted list of doubly material topics: climate change, energy, water, nutrition and healthy living, biodiversity, corporate culture, talent management, product safety and quality, supplier management, local community development (11th item not captured by text extraction, see IC25 p.147 chart) [sourced, IC25 pp.146–147]. 2022 exercise (eight countries, GRI plus SASB-based financial lens) had eight material topics: consumer experience; product safety and quality; responsible communication and labelling; innovation in healthy products; sustainable agriculture practices; energy efficiency and renewables; sustainable packaging and circular economy; climate adaptation and mitigation (physical risks) [sourced, SFF p.11].
- Board composition (as of 2026-04-30): 28% women, 72% men; chart also shows 39% (read as independence share, label partly lost in extraction), average age 58, 83% Mexican nationals [sourced, IC25 p.137]; RA25 gender table agrees on 28% women and records 0% women among "directivos relevantes" [sourced, RA25 p.190]. The Audit and Corporate Practices Committee is made up exclusively of independent directors and its remit includes sustainability topics [sourced, IC25 p.140]. Board changes in 2025: Jaime Chico Pardo left; Roberto Mauricio Servitje Labarrere joined; León Lawrence Kraig Eskenazi named (IC25 p.138 [sourced]).
- Executive compensation linked to ESG metrics: not found in the sources reviewed (searched RA25 and IC25 for compensation-sustainability links).

## 7. What the annual reports say about ESG risk and cost

- ESG regulation is itself a disclosed risk: EU due-diligence, deforestation and carbon-border rules "significant" investment; Mexican water, circular-economy and emissions-trading regimes [sourced, RA25 pp.38–40; see [[bimbo-risk-factors]]].
- Since 2025 a separate CapEx envelope funds the organisations' sustainability activities (vehicles, ovens, water treatment, regenerative agriculture); amount not disclosed [sourced, IC25 p.168].
- Capex: 91% of the roughly USD 1,200 million spent in 2025 went to manufacturing, logistics and sustainability initiatives (not split by category) [sourced, RA25 p.81]; five pilots of forest improvement funded with MXN 5 million [sourced, RA25 p.96].
- Climate physical risks cited: water stress, extreme weather, wildfires; higher insurance cost for exposed sites [sourced, RA25 pp.22, 33].
- Sustainability-linked debt exposes interest cost to these KPIs; see [[bimbo-sustainable-financing]] for the 2025 target miss on one bond and the 2026 declassification of loans.

## Key Takeaways

- The strategy has three pillars plus foundations and has run since 2021; the headline climate targets are −50% Scope 1 and −28% Scope 3 by 2030 vs 2019 and net zero by 2050 [sourced].
- Reported 2025 status: 100% renewable electricity (third-party validated, partly via certificates), 100% treated-water reuse vs 2020 base, 99% recyclable packaging, >520,000 ha regenerative agriculture, TRIR 1.59, 30.51% women in leadership [sourced].
- The 2025 Scope 3 step (−12.5%) attached to the Bimbo 23-2L bond was reported as not met, SBTi certification of the targets is temporarily paused for a re-baselining, and 2023–2025 absolute emissions are not in the filings reviewed [sourced; gap flagged].
- Last disclosed Scope 3 in the financing framework (2022: 9.87 Mt, unaudited) is +23% vs 2019 [calc], so the Scope 3 path depends on the re-baselined numbers.
- ESG is governed top-down (Board, steering committee, CSCSO) with quarterly committees; limited assurance by KPMG covers materials, water, waste and nutrition KPIs, and Carbon Trust covers renewable electricity and treated-water reuse.
- External recognitions are mostly reputation and ethics rankings plus CDP (A climate in 2023, B water in 2025); no MSCI, Sustainalytics or DJSI ratings appear in the filings.
