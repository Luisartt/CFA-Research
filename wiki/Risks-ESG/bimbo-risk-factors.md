## 2. Demand, customers, competition, brands

- Competition: price and promotion pressure, private label, exclusion from price increases when costs rise [sourced, RA25 p.24].
- Brand reputation and IP (most sales under own brands) [sourced, RA25 pp.24–25]; competition from low-cost brands [sourced, RA25 p.25].
- Consumer preference shifts (low-carb diets; concerns on trans fats, sugar, refined flour, gluten) and innovation dependence [sourced, RA25 pp.25–26]; operations concentrated in North America where industry growth "has been moderate" [sourced, RA25 p.26].
- Limited number of customers in some channels (supermarkets, grocery, fast-food restaurants) with purchase orders, no signed contracts [sourced, RA25 p.26]; reliance on supermarket chains and alternative retail platforms; retailer consolidation [sourced, RA25 pp.31–32].
- Food safety and product liability; social-media amplification [sourced, RA25 pp.27–28].
- Consumer confidence and habits tied to Mexican/other macro indicators [sourced, RA25 pp.26, 49].

## 3. Regulation and legislation

- **Food safety, labelling, advertising (Mexico).** 2020 front-of-pack labelling reform; on 2025-07-31 an agreement extended phase 2 and set phase 3 start for 2028-01-01 [sourced, RA25 p.29]; school-food guidelines published 2024-09-30, mandatory since 2025-03-29, include universities and ban "seal" products in school meal preparation [sourced, RA25 p.30]; Sept 2022 advertising decree requires health-authority permits for advertising products with front-of-pack seals [sourced, RA25 p.31]. Oaxaca prohibits sale of high-calorie food to minors (2020) [sourced, RA25 p.30].
- **Environmental.** State "environmental taxes" in 18 jurisdictions listed (Baja California, Campeche, Coahuila, Colima, Durango, Estado de México, Guanajuato, Ciudad de México, Morelos, Nayarit, Nuevo León, Oaxaca, Querétaro, Quintana Roo, San Luis Potosí, Tamaulipas, Yucatán, Zacatecas) [calc: count of names, RA25 p.38]; all Mexican states have some single-use-plastic restriction [sourced, RA25 p.38]; emissions-trading system pilot ran 2020–2025 and SEMARNAT had not published the operative phase (RA25 p.38 [sourced]); NOM-001-SEMARNAT-2021 wastewater limits [sourced, RA25 p.39].
- **Water (new items in RA25).** 2024-12-19 National Agreement on the Human Right to Water (Group participated; temporary partial return of concession volumes to CONAGUA); 2025-12-11 reform to the National Waters Law ends transfer of concession titles and limits payments to suspend lapse of unused volumes to two [sourced, RA25 p.39]. FY2024 report instead flagged a pending new General Water Law initiative (Nov 2024) [sourced, RA24 p.35].
- **Circular economy (new in RA25).** General Circular Economy Law published 2026-01-19 creating extended producer responsibility; requirements not yet published; Group expects spending and possible product redesign [sourced, RA25 p.39].
- **EU ESG directives (new in RA25).** Due-diligence, deforestation and carbon-border rules; non-compliance could bar sales in the EU [sourced, RA25 p.40]. Palm oil: 100% of volume RSPO-certified 2023–2025 via Book & Claim plus Mass Balance [sourced, RA25 p.40].
- **Price controls and taxes.** Price controls exist on some products in some countries [sourced, RA25 p.43]; possible tax-rate reforms in Mexico and the US [sourced, RA25 p.43].
- **Labour (Mexico).** 2019 reform; 2021 outsourcing ban; 12 paid vacation days in first year from 2023-01-01; "Ley Silla" fully in force 2025-12-15; platform-work and complaints-system changes in 2025; presidential initiative of 2025-12-03 to cut the workweek gradually by two hours per year from 2027-01-01 to 40 hours [sourced, RA25 p.48]. Group reports no known union-organising efforts, strike notices or collective claims at report date [sourced, RA25 p.47].
- **Competition, anti-corruption, AML, sanctions** [sourced, RA25 p.41]. See litigation below.
- **Accounting disclosure** differences (IFRS vs US GAAP; new IFRS) [sourced, RA25 p.49].

## 4. Tax and customs

- 2020 Mexican reform: net interest deductions capped at 30% of adjusted profit; limits on related-party payments and hybrid mechanisms [sourced, RA25 p.44]; 2021 outsourcing reform; 2022 reform on related-party financing withholding and transfer pricing [sourced, RA25 p.45].
- Tax-authority enforcement outlook: 2026 Revenue Law adds no new taxes but raises surcharge rates; Federal Tax Code amendments from 2026-01-01 strengthen verification powers [sourced, RA25 p.46].
- Customs Law reform in force 2026-01-01: penalties of 250% and 300% for inaccuracies, inventory-control failures, omitted taxes and origin violations; Group expects more customs audits [sourced, RA25 p.46]. FY2024 report instead described ANAM set-up, Carta Porte and HS-code changes [sourced, RA24 p.42].
- Audited notes: ISR rate 30% in Mexico [sourced, RA25 p.105]; Pillar Two: Group expects no material top-up tax using safe-harbour rules [sourced, RA25 PDF p.276]; Mexican tax changes of 2025-09-08 effective 2026-01-01 "did not generate financial impact" [sourced, RA25 PDF p.276].

- **FX:** 44% of consolidated debt in USD at 2025-12-31; peso moves vs USD: +13.4% appreciation 2025, +13.1% 2023, +4.9% 2022; −22.9% depreciation 2024, −2.8% 2021 (nominal, Banxico) [sourced, RA25 p.35]. See [[bimbo-fx-exposure]].
- **Funding and leverage:** covenants and restrictions, variable-rate exposure, rating-downgrade risk (also linked to Mexico's sovereign rating) [sourced, RA25 pp.42–43]. See [[bimbo-capital-structure-and-debt]].
- **Holding-company structure:** Group depends on subsidiary dividends; Mexican subsidiaries pay only from retained earnings after legal reserve (5% of net income until 20% of capital) [sourced, RA25 p.27].
- **Goodwill and intangibles impairment** [sourced, RA25 pp.41–42].
- **Acquisitions:** unknown liabilities, limited indemnities, antitrust limits, integration [sourced, RA25 pp.34–35]. See [[bimbo-acquisitions]].
- **Related-party transactions** reviewed by the Audit and Corporate Practices Committee [sourced, RA25 p.37].
- **Key-person dependence;** no key-man insurance [sourced, RA25 p.37].
- **Enforcement of judgments** against directors and controlling shareholders (Mexican residents) [sourced, RA25 pp.50, 60–61].

## 7. Litigation and regulatory contingencies

- **Canada Bread competition matter.** 2017 Canadian Competition Bureau price-fixing investigation (alleged conduct since 2001; Canada Bread acquired 2014). Canada Bread resolved it on 2023-06-21 with a fine of USD 38 million (CAD 50 million), paid in full 2023-07-20 [sourced, RA25 pp.41, 44, 119]. Two consolidated class actions (from 12 earlier suits) remain; notes say no provision recorded for them [sourced, RA25 PDF p.307; note text refers to "2024-12-31", likely a carry-over].
- **Claim against Maple Leaf.** Filed 2024-11-29 in the Ontario Superior Court against Maple Leaf Foods and former senior officers over the 2014 Canada Bread sale; seeks more than CAD 2 billion for alleged fraudulent/negligent misrepresentation or restitution [sourced, RA25 pp.41, 44, 119].
- **General statement:** party to routine judicial proceedings; apart from items described, no known proceedings with a significant adverse effect in the prior twelve months; not in insolvency scenarios (LCM arts. 9–10) [sourced, RA25 p.119].
- **Brazil:** labour and civil suits guaranteed with deposits of MXN 88 million [sourced, RA25 PDF p.307].
- **Remote-to-less-than-probable:** aggregate tax/legal/labour claims rated "less than probable but more than remote" = MXN 279 million at 2025-12-31 [sourced, RA25 PDF p.307].

## 8. Pensions and multi-employer plans (MEPP / "PPM")

- Risk-factor text: Bimbo Bakeries USA (BBU) contributes to multi-employer plans for some US and Canadian plants; required contributions could rise if other employers become insolvent or withdraw without paying their liability, from asset returns or funding shortfalls; annual cost sensitive to discount rate and health-cost inflation [sourced, RA25 p.47].
- Accounting: BBU participates in plans treated as defined contribution unless withdrawal is judged probable; unpaid withdrawal liabilities of departing employers are spread among remaining employers; if a plan enters critical status, BBU may be asked for extra contributions [sourced, RA25 PDF pp.305–306].
- Cash contributions to MEPPs: MXN 2,700 million (2025), 2,609 (2024), 2,430 (2023); expected 2026: MXN 2,406 million [sourced, RA25 PDF p.306].
- Withdrawal liabilities recorded in other LT liabilities: MXN 985 million (2025), 1,205 (2024), 2,033 (2023) [sourced, RA25 PDF p.306]. In 2024 trustees of a plan BBU had earlier exited said it was fully funded and no more withdrawal payments were required, producing a gain of MXN 921 million [sourced, RA25 PDF p.311].
- Retiree health/social-security (US post-retirement benefits, defined contribution): 1,389 short term + 3,272 long term = 4,661 (2025); 4,838 (2024); 4,044 (2023) [sourced, RA25 PDF p.306; sum checks].
- Defined-benefit plans (consolidated experience table): obligation 29,033 vs plan assets 27,509 → deficit 1,524 (2025); deficit 1,698 (2024); 2,375 (2023); expected 2026 contribution MXN 67 million [sourced, RA25 PDF p.305; deficit = obligation − assets, [calc] ties].

## Key Takeaways

- The FY2025 risk section is 40 pages (RA25 pp.22–61) and its largest additions versus FY2024 are Mexico-policy items: water-law reform, circular-economy law, energy reform, customs and tax enforcement, labour changes, and the 2026 USMCA review.
- Quantified contingencies sit in Note 19: provisions MXN 10,684 million at end-2025 [sourced], of which uncertain tax positions are MXN 8,537 million [sourced] (about 80% [calc]).
- Multi-employer pension exposure is cash-visible: MXN 2,700 million contributed in 2025 and MXN 2,406 million expected in 2026 [sourced]; recorded withdrawal liabilities fell from 2,033 (2023) to 985 (2025) [sourced].
- The Canada Bread antitrust episode has a paid fine (USD 38 million, 2023) [sourced], open class actions without a booked provision [sourced], and an offsetting claim against Maple Leaf above CAD 2 billion [sourced].
- 44% of debt is USD-denominated at end-2025 [sourced] and the peso swung +13.4% in 2025 and −22.9% in 2024 [sourced], so FX appears both as a financing and an operating risk.
- Items dropped between reports (COVID-19, LIBOR, Brexit, GM maize) show what management no longer treats as live; this is a disclosure change, not a risk judgement.
