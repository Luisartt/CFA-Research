# S&P Global ESG Score History for Grupo Bimbo, 2023-2025: Reports, Criteria and Controversies

The six "S&P Global ESG Scores Report" PDFs in the Capital IQ research folder, read line by line: score by methodology year and by update date, the 25 or so criteria behind it, how the industry benchmark moved, and the one controversy S&P recorded. ==Facts only; reconciled with the CIQ ESG export in [[ciq-esg-scores-and-profiles]].== Company-side targets and claims are in [[bimbo-esg-framework]] and [[bimbo-sustainable-financing]]; legal background in [[bimbo-risk-factors]]. Companion articles: [[bimbo-press-release-timeline-2010-2026]], [[bimbo-event-study-inputs]], [[bimbo-sell-side-research-2014-2020]].

**Conventions.** Scores are 0-100. `[sourced]` = printed in the cited report (SPG-yymmdd = report "last updated" date, defined in section 7; page numbers are PDF pages, scorecards sit on p.1-4, the controversy on p.5 of the 2023 report). `[calc]` = my arithmetic. Industry = "FOA Food Products" (S&P Global ESG Score industry); company status "Survey Respondent" throughout. The S&P Global ESG Score = CSA score (Corporate Sustainability Assessment, no modelling) + a modeled contribution that fills gaps. Dimension weights differ by methodology year, so criteria are not like-for-like between 2023 and 2024 (section 3).

### 3.3 The methodology change is part of the 2023-to-2024 jump

Between 2023 and 2024 S&P re-based the criteria (Climate Strategy weight 5% to 8%; Human Capital Management 8% added; Governance weight 33% to 29%; Environmental weight 33% to 38%). Climate Strategy rose 59 to 77 and Biodiversity 28 to 47 in the same year `[sourced]` SPG-231027 p.4 vs SPG-240823 p.4. Treat the 52-to-59 CSA step as partly methodology and partly performance; S&P does not split it `[unverified]`.

## 5. Controversies (Media & Stakeholder Analysis)

| Report | Controversy status |
|---|---|
| SPG-231027 | ==1 case, impact "Minor", 0 Severe / Major / Medium== |
| SPG-240823, SPG-250512, SPG-250805, SPG-251009, SPG-251208 | "Has not been impacted by any recent controversies" |

The 2023 case: "Subsidiary's involvement in bread price-fixing in Canada". In Jun-2023 the Ontario Superior Court fined Canada Bread USD 50m (the report's figure; Bimbo's own release says CAD 50m, about USD 38m) after guilty pleas on four counts under the Competition Act, described as the highest fine ever imposed by a Canadian court; two price increases (2007, 2011) set with Weston Foods (Canada); Canada Bread was then owned by Maple Leaf Foods and was acquired by Bimbo in 2014; it received a reduced sentence for full cooperation under the leniency programme; the executive involved is no longer with the company. S&P rated the company response "Adequate communication, appropriate measures". Score impact: -0.46 points on the total CSA score; -1.4 points on the Governance & Economic dimension; -9.06 points on Business Ethics and -2.52 on Risk & Crisis Management criteria `[sourced]` SPG-231027 p.1, p.5.

- Currency flag: S&P writes "USD 50 million"; Bimbo's release gives CAD 50m = US$ 38m `[sourced]` Bimbo release 21-Jun-2023 p.1 and [[bimbo-risk-factors]] (USD 38m). The S&P number is a currency slip `[calc]`; use CAD 50m.
- Not in S&P's controversy list in any report: the Aug-2019 Little Bites recall (US$ news, [[bimbo-press-release-timeline-2010-2026]]), the Nov-2024 lawsuit against Maple Leaf (a claim by Bimbo, not against it), the 2018 voluntary separation of about 600 BBU staff, and the 2024 bakery closures. S&P's controversy screen therefore covers only what reached a court or regulator and the media.
- The case disappears from the 2024 report even though the class actions are still open `[sourced]` Bimbo release 29-Nov-2024 p.1; treat "no controversy" as a screen result, not a clean bill.

## 6. What the numbers do and do not say

- Bimbo is a strong disclosure-and-programme company by S&P's yardstick (CSA percentile 89-96) but scores low on Governance (41; percentile 74), Information Security (0), Tax Strategy (21) and Corporate Governance (28 vs 42). This is consistent with the controlling-family structure described in [[bimbo-governance-and-ownership]] `[unverified]` causal link.
- Disclosure vs performance: Dec-2025 required-disclosure score 36 of a potential 53 and additional-disclosure 21 of 33, so about a third of the available points are lost on content even though 84-87% of the disclosures are made `[sourced]` SPG-251208 p.2.
- Score changes of 1-3 points between updates are within what S&P re-scoring did to the same company in the same year (Aug-2025 56 to Oct-2025 57 CSA); do not read single-point moves as signals.
- No link from ESG score to price, cost of debt or sustainability-linked coupon is established here; the 2023 sustainability-linked issue (MXN 15,000m, Scope 3 KPI) is in [[bimbo-sustainable-financing]].

## Key Takeaways

- S&P ESG Score: 55 (Oct-2023), 61 (Aug-2024), 57 then 58 (Aug-Dec 2025); the CSA score fell 59 to 57 in 2025 while the industry average CSA rose 22 to 31, so the percentile slid 96 to 89.
- Governance is the structural weakness: 41 vs industry 33 on the dimension and a governance percentile of 74; Corporate Governance 28 vs 42, Information Security 0, Tax Strategy 21 are below industry average.
- The one controversy S&P logged was the Canada Bread price-fixing fine (CAD 50m, 2023), -0.46 CSA points; it vanished from the 2024-2025 reports while class actions remain open.
- Methodology changed between 2023 and 2024 (Climate weight 5% to 8%), so the 2024 jump to 61 is not like-for-like.
- S&P's numbers reconcile exactly with the CIQ export once the modeled contribution is added (CIQ dimensions are modeled-inclusive).
- Re-scoring noise is real: Health & Nutrition went 100 (Aug-2024) to 48 (Aug-2025) to 70 (Oct-2025) with no change in the business.
