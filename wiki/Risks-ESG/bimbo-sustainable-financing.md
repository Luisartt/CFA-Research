# Grupo Bimbo (BIMBOA) — Sustainable Financing Framework (April 2023) and instruments issued

IC25 = FY2025 integrated report "Celebramos" (PDF page). 4Q25 / 2Q26 = BMV quarterly filings for the period ended 2025-12-31 / 2026-06-30. MXN millions unless stated.

## 1. What the framework is

- Umbrella framework to raise funds through green or sustainability-linked bonds, loans, commercial paper, derivatives or a mix, called "Green or Sustainability-Linked Financing Instruments" [sourced, SFF p.4].
- Two parts [sourced, SFF pp.12, 20]: (a) a **Green Financing Framework** (use of proceeds), aligned to the ICMA Green Bond Principles (2021 edition with the June 2022 appendix) and LMA Green Loan Principles (2023); (b) a **Sustainability-Linked Financing Framework**, aligned to ICMA Sustainability-Linked Bond Principles (2020) and LMA/LSTA/APLMA Sustainability-Linked Loan Principles (2023).
- Date and status: April 2023 [sourced, file name and SFF p.4]. It can be updated; material updates require prior external-reviewer approval and publication on the investor section of the company website [sourced, SFF p.29].
- Second-party opinion (SPO): **Sustainalytics** [sourced, SFF pp.4, 19, 28]. SPO to be published on the provider's site and the company's; the SPO document itself is not among the sources reviewed (see open items).
- Framework context: "Sustainable financing commitments" section records the Sept 2021 syndicated revolving line of USD 1,750 million, "first food and beverage company in Latin America" to close a sustainability-linked syndicated RCF, with a five-year term and a margin-adjustment mechanism based on two KPIs: renewable electricity as a share of total electricity and treated-water reuse at production plants [sourced, SFF p.11].
- Company profile at the time: 141,000+ employees, 214 bakeries and plants in 34 countries (January 2023), 55,000+ routes, 10,000+ products [sourced, SFF p.4]. Compare [[bimbo-segments-and-regions]].

## 2. Green financing (use of proceeds)

### Eligible categories and criteria (SFF pp.13–16) [sourced]

| # | Eligible green category | Eligibility criteria (condensed) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Green buildings | New, upgraded or expanded properties with LEED Gold or Platinum, BREEAM Excellent or Outstanding, or Energy Star score of 85 or more (US plants); for refurbishments, primary energy demand cut by at least 30% |
| 2 | Renewable energy | Construction, development, acquisition, operation and connection of solar, wind, certified hydro (run-of-river without storage, or power density above 10 W/m2, or life-cycle emissions below 50 gCO2e/kWh) and green hydrogen below 100 gCO2e/kWh; on-site solar and wind, long-term or virtual PPAs for renewable projects, lithium-ion storage for renewable energy |
| 3 | Clean transport | Electric and hybrid vehicles, including propane, renewable natural gas, green hydrogen or ethanol vehicles (passenger 75 gCO2/p-km, buses with BRT systems 50 gCO2/p-km, heavy trucks 25 gCO2/p-km); EV charging stations powered by solar or wind |
| 4 | Energy efficiency | Equipment, systems and operational improvements, e.g. lighting and energy-management upgrades yielding at least 5% potential efficiency gain |
| 5 | Pollution prevention and control: fossil-fuel elimination | Converting fossil-fuel ovens to electric ovens on renewable power, hydrogen or renewable-gas ovens; fryers to electric or solar-thermal; removing boilers via heat recovery |
| 6 | Pollution prevention and control: GHG reduction | Refrigeration upgrades that reduce greenhouse-gas emissions |
| 7 | Environmentally sustainable management of living natural resources and land use | Raw materials certified by FSC, SFI, PEFC or recycled (paper, board); sustainable agriculture programmes for key ingredients such as corn and wheat (regenerative practices); protection or restoration of natural resources |
| 8 | Eco-efficient and/or circular-economy adapted products, production technologies and processes | Food-waste reduction technologies extending shelf life; packaging optimisation (less plastic, bio-based, compostable ASTM 6400 / EN13432, biodegradable ASTM 6954); recyclability and recycled content; post-consumer recycling programmes (e.g. Ecoins, Ecolana, Ecoce); mechanical recycling only (plastic-to-fuel excluded) |
| 9 | Sustainable water and wastewater management | Wastewater treatment, closed-loop internal water circuits, water-reduction and efficient-use projects and technologies |

### Process (SFF pp.12, 16–19) [sourced]
- **Allocation.** Net proceeds equal to eligible portfolio; new and existing projects; refinancing look-back of 24–36 months before issuance (p.12 says 24–36 months; p.17 says 36 months).
- **Evaluation and selection.** Sustainable Investments Committee with representatives from Sustainability, Treasury, Net Zero, Financial Planning, Agribusiness, Vehicles and Acquisitions; meets at least once a year; Sustainability, Net Zero, Financial Planning and Treasury track actual spend and avoid double-counting (p.16).
- **Risk management.** Four-criterion rating (impact, probability, control, velocity); four impact levels, four probability levels, four control levels, three velocity levels; materiality example: failure to meet budgeted sales, regulatory fines or damages up to 1% of Adjusted EBITDA, or loss of confidence in a location (p.16). TCFD-style climate scenario work began in 4Q22 with physical-risk and climate-change transition exercise (pp.16–17).
- **Management of proceeds.** Allocation targeted within 3–5 years of issuance; unallocated net proceeds kept in cash or equivalents, excluding carbon-intensive activities; principal and interest paid from general company funds, not tied to a project; reallocation if a project stops qualifying (p.17).
- **Reporting.** Allocation and impact report within one year of issuance, updated annually until full allocation and for any material change until maturity; published on the website for bonds, or to lenders for other instruments (p.17). Allocation items: total allocated, by category, share of new financing vs refinancing, unallocated balance (p.17).
- **Impact metrics (examples)** (p.18): number of sustainable buildings; renewable share of electricity, annual renewable generation (MWh/GWh), installed MW, avoided GHG (tCO2e); zero-emission vehicles and chargers; energy savings; ovens, fryers and boilers converted or removed; hectares under regenerative agriculture; share of certified raw materials; hectares protected or restored; packaging recyclability and tonnes recycled; reused treated wastewater as a share of treated; annual water-use reduction (t) and efficiency gain (%).
- **External review.** Pre-issuance SPO (Sustainalytics) against ICMA GBP 2021 and LMA GLP 2023; annual independent verification of the management assertion on allocation, starting one year after issuance and until full allocation (p.19).

## 3. Sustainability-linked financing

### KPIs and SPTs (SFF pp.20–27) [sourced]

| KPI | Definition and base year | Sustainability performance target (SPT) | Observation date |
|---|---|---|---|
| KPI 1: absolute Scope 1 GHG reduction | % change in tCO2e vs base year 2019 (SBTi-validated) | −50% by 2030 | 2030-12-31 |
| KPI 2: absolute Scope 3 GHG reduction | % change in tCO2e vs base year 2019 (SBTi-validated) | −12.5% by 2025; −17.5% by 2027; −28% by 2030 | 2025-12-31, 2027-12-31, 2030-12-31 |
| KPI 3: renewable energy share (Scope 2) | renewable kWh / total kWh, base year 2020 | 100% renewable electricity by 2025 | 2025-12-31 |
| KPI 4: treated water reused | reused treated water (m3) vs treated water in 2020 base, re-based for closures or legal bans | 96% (2023), 98% (2024), 100% (2025) | 2023-12-31, 2024-12-31, 2025-12-31 |

- KPIs can be used alone or combined; the relevant KPIs for each instrument are fixed in its final terms (SFF p.20).
- Baseline history in the framework (tCO2e, kWh, m3) is reproduced in [[bimbo-esg-framework]] section 3.
- Planned measures (SFF pp.24–26): zero-emission mobility, fossil-fuel elimination, natural refrigerants, innovation; Scope 3 actions (low-carbon supply, regenerative agriculture, waste reduction, employee and third-party logistics, customer carbon footprint, investments); on-site and long-term clean power, storage and electrical installations; internal water-treatment circuits and wastewater upgrades.
- Instrument features (SFF p.27): the financial adjustment (coupon or margin step-up, or reduction as specified in the documents) is triggered if a KPI misses its SPT on the observation date, or if the external verification is not provided and published by the notification date; if the SPT is met and verified, the financial characteristic stays unchanged.
- Re-baselining (SFF p.27): baselines may be recalculated for divestments, acquisitions or significant structural changes per the WRI GHG Protocol; recalculations disclosed in annual sustainability reports.
- Reporting (SFF pp.27–28): annual KPI performance and baselines, an independent verification report, qualitative or quantitative explanation of drivers (including M&A), and any KPI or SPT reassessment.
- Verification (SFF p.28): SPO by Sustainalytics confirming alignment with ICMA SLBP and LMA SLLP; annual independent external verification of KPI performance.

## 5. Where performance stands (latest disclosures)

- KPI 3 (renewable electricity): 100% reported for 2025, third-party validated; 2024: 97%; 2023: 92%; 2022: 89% (framework basis) [sourced: IC25 pp.12, 106; RA24 p.93; RA23 p.123; SFF p.22]. Note the 2025 figure includes renewable-energy certificates bought for sites that cannot source locally (Korea, Ukraine, Venezuela) (IC25 p.106).
- KPI 4 (treated water reuse): 100% for 2025 vs 2020 base [sourced, RA25 p.93; IC25 p.12]; interim 2023 and 2024 milestones were 96% and 98% (SFF p.26).
- KPI 2 (Scope 3): Bimbo 23-2L step of −12.5% at end-2025 not met; company says progress on regenerative agriculture (>500,000 ha) and BEAR supplier programme (>300 suppliers) but reports a lag in supply-chain transformation and growth of operations; a re-baselining of the carbon inventory under updated international standards is under way (IC25 p.169) [sourced]. SBTi certification of targets is provisionally suspended during that update (IC25 p.101) [sourced].
- KPI 1 (Scope 1): observation only in 2030; no interim test.
- Capital-allocation link: since 2025 the company has a separate CapEx envelope for the organisations' sustainability activities, used in part for vehicle and equipment replacement, ovens, water treatment and regenerative agriculture; water-stress criteria added to projects (IC25 p.168) [sourced]. Amount not disclosed in sources reviewed.

## Key Takeaways

- The April 2023 framework has two arms: nine green use-of-proceeds categories and four sustainability-linked KPIs, with a Sustainalytics second-party opinion; only the sustainability-linked arm shows up in the instruments actually reported [sourced].
- Sustainability-linked instruments reported: the syndicated RCF (USD 1,750 million in 2021, 1,931 million in 2023, 2,350 million in 2025), a BBVA MXN 5,000 million line, bilaterals of USD 125 million and USD 100 million, and two certificates totalling MXN 15,000 million (Bimbo 23L and 23-2L) [sourced; sum calc].
- The first Scope 3 test failed: Bimbo 23-2L missed its −12.5% by end-2025 and carries a 0.20% nominal increase (MXN 6 million) [sourced]; the financial cost is small, the signal is the Scope 3 path.
- From 2026-01-01 the revolver and three bilaterals stopped being classified as sustainability-linked, because their targets ended in 2025; the two certificates remain linked to Scope 3 [sourced].
- The next test dates are 2027-12-31 (Scope 3 −17.5%) and 2030-12-31 (Scope 1 −50%, Scope 3 −28%), both measured against a 2019 baseline now being recalculated [sourced].
