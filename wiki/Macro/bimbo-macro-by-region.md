## 7. Macro and political items in RA25 that touch demand

`[sourced]` RA25:
- Global conditions: lower growth, higher inflation or rates in any country can reduce demand and realised price; consumer and supplier financial stability can weaken (RA25 p.52).
- US-Mexico trade tension episodes; US tariffs announced 2-Apr-2025; USMCA review July 2026 (RA25 p.52-54).
- Mexico: high-inflation history, Banxico easing, violence, a Morena-controlled Congress, fiscal austerity law (RA25 p.56-58).
- Brazil: high real interest rates (SELIC 15.00% in 2025) (RA25 p.58-59). Argentina: inflation and labor-cost pressure (RA25 p.59).
- Venezuela-US tensions and Venezuela's FX restrictions (RA25 p.56, p.59-60).
- Geopolitics: Russia-Ukraine; Middle East; US-Iran conflict mentioned as a commodity-price driver (RA25 p.22, p.55).

## Key Takeaways
- North America excl.-FX sales improved sequentially from -5.7% (4Q24) to +0.7% (1Q26) and +1.3% (2Q26), and management reports share gains across US categories while calling the consumer environment still challenging `[sourced]` (section 2).
- Mexico grew every quarter in the period (+0.3% to +6.6%), led by mix and, in 2024, volume; the 3Q25 +0.3% was against a high base `[sourced]` (section 3).
- EAA and LatAm excl.-FX growth was boosted by acquisitions (Don Don, Karamolegos, Wickbold, Pagnifique); LatAm margin pressure in 4Q25 and 2Q26 is tied to Wickbold integration and, earlier, Brazil/Argentina FX-linked raw materials `[sourced]`.
- Several margin points are non-recurring or non-operating: the 1Q26 gain on sale of an associate stake, 4Q24 MEPPs benefit, and Spain bakery closures `[sourced]`.
- RA25 macro: Mexico CPI 3.69% and Banxico rate 7.0% (2025); Brazil SELIC 15.00%; Argentina inflation 31.5% with IAS 29 accounting `[sourced]` RA25 p.57-59.
- Gaps: no quantified volume vs price/mix split, and no external consumer data in the filings.
