## 1. Structure of the exposure

Bimbo reports in MXN but earns most of its sales outside Mexico. Three channels `[sourced]` EEFF25 p.63; RA25 p.35-36, p.130:

| Channel | What it is | Where it shows up |
|---|---|---|
| Translation | Foreign subsidiaries (functional currency not MXN) are converted to MXN; income statement at average rate, balance sheet at closing rate (hyperinflationary economies at closing rate) | Reported vs "excl. FX" growth; OCI |
| Transaction | Forecast raw-material purchases, firm contracts, monetary assets/liabilities in other currencies. Main pairs named: ==MXN/USD, MXN/CAD, CAD/USD== | Cost of sales (e.g. USD-priced inputs, Mexican product imported into the US), FX gain/loss in financing cost |
| Financing | Debt and intercompany loans in USD/EUR/CAD/GBP | Interest, FX loss, net-investment-hedge designation |

- Company statement: revenues are earned "mainly in Mexican pesos and US dollars and, to a lesser extent, other local currencies"; FX differences affect the statements "particularly" for the US and Canada results `[sourced]` RA25 p.130.
- At 31 Dec 2025, ==44% of consolidated debt== and "an important part" of revenues, operating costs and taxes were USD-denominated; a "significant portion" of revenues, costs and taxes is in MXN and other currencies `[sourced]` RA25 p.35.
- FX can also hamper imports of raw materials and finished goods priced in USD into businesses outside the US `[sourced]` RA25 p.35.
- Company says it manages USD exposure "selectively" (USD debt and USD-denominated raw-material purchases) and that hedges may not be sufficient `[sourced]` RA25 p.36.

## 7. Hedging policy and instruments

### 7.1 Policy `[sourced]`
- Derivatives are used ==exclusively for hedging==; each derivative must be tied to a primary position and notional amounts must be consistent with the primary position; no trades aimed at earning premium income. Counterparties are institutions of recognised solvency with limits per institution (EEFF25 p.62).
- Transaction FX policy: hedge expected cash flows, mainly of "highly probable" forecast obligations, under hedge accounting; when the purchase occurs the hedged non-financial item is adjusted by the gain/loss previously in OCI (EEFF25 p.64).
- Roles: corporate treasury manages interest-rate, FX, liquidity and credit risk; the purchasing department manages commodity price risk and checks open derivative positions against corporate strategy; both report to Risk Management. Stated aim: "neutral and balanced position" versus the exposure (RA25 p.149).
- Hedge ratio 1:1 for FX and commodity hedges, economic relationship via matching notional/payment dates (EEFF25 p.68, p.72).

### 7.2 Instruments named `[sourced]` EEFF25 p.62
Interest-rate swaps; cross-currency swaps; FX forwards; FX calls; zero-cost collars; commodity futures; options on commodity futures; input swaps.

## Key Takeaways
- ==Translation is large and flips sign with the peso==: consolidated reported minus excl.-FX growth was +11.1 pp in 1Q25 and -7.8 pp in 1Q26; -6.7 pp in 2Q26 `[calc]` (section 5).
- North America is 42.6% of LTM sales at Jun-26 (47.3% at Mar-24); excl.-FX NA sales turned positive in 1Q26 (+0.7%) and 2Q26 (+1.3%) while reported MXN was -13.0% and -9.7% `[sourced]`.
- Debt is 42% USD / 42% MXN / 12% EUR at Jun-26; USD share fell from 53% (Sep/Dec-24) `[sourced]`. Net debt/EBITDA 2.5x with average cost 6.5% `[sourced]` 2T26 rel. p.6.
- Hedging is hedge-accounting only: net-investment hedges (USD, CAD, EUR, GBP loans/derivatives) cover translation; FX forwards and collars cover forecast purchases (US$586m at 19.58 at Dec-25) `[sourced]`.
- Company-stated sensitivity: ±$1 MXN/USD moves profit about ±43 on closing balances (FY2025) — balance-sheet only `[sourced]` EEFF25 p.64.
