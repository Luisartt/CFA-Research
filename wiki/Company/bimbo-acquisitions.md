# Acquisitions and divestitures, FY21 to 2Q26

**Citation key.** `RA25`/`RA24`/`RA23`/`RA22`/`RA21` = annual reports to BMV for that fiscal year (`filings/bmv/...`); `p.` = PDF page index (equals the "N de 205" footer up to p.207 in RA25; later pages are the unnumbered statements). `IA25-EEFF` = audited FY25 statements; `Celebramos` = FY25 integrated report; `BMV-2Q26` = 2Q26 quarterly filing. MXN millions unless stated. IFRS. Latest data point: 2Q26 (period ended 2026-06-30).

**What the filings disclose, and what they do not.** Dates, targets, stakes, segments and business descriptions are disclosed. **Individual purchase prices are not disclosed in the annual reports** (the notes give the aggregate cash paid and a preliminary purchase-price allocation per year). Where a deal-level figure is shown below it is derived from the allocation tables and tagged [calc]. Deal-level prices would have to come from press releases or the team's own sources.

## 4. Pattern across the five years

- **Mostly bolt-on** (single-country, bakery and QSR targets) rather than platform deals; the largest in the period are St. Pierre (~MXN 6.7 bn implied) and the 2025 cluster (~MXN 11.7 bn implied, with Wickbold and Don Don the biggest by footprint) [calc].
- **Regional focus**: India (2021), Spain and UK (2021-2022), Romania (2023-2025, four deals), Tunisia (2024, 2026), US snack/bakery niches, Canada, Brazil (QSR 2021, Wickbold 2025), Balkans (2025). EAA accounts for 12 of the 24 listed deals, North America 6, Latin America 5 and the UNO associate 1 [calc]; see [[bimbo-segments-and-regions]].
- **Categories**: premium and specialty bread (brioche, sourdough, bagels), QSR bakeries (Brazil, Switzerland, Colorado), sweet bakery (Spain, Romania, Tunisia) and healthy snacks (Emmy's, Popcornopolis) [sourced] RA21 p.69; RA23 p.262; RA25 p.222-223.
- **Exit logic**: confectionery was sold in 2022 to concentrate on grain-based foods; after that, disposals have been small (associate stake 1Q26) [sourced] RA25 p.76; 1T26 p.5.
- **Governance**: acquisitions above 3% of consolidated assets (~MXN 12.3 bn) need board approval; above 20% need shareholders' approval ([[bimbo-capital-allocation]], [[bimbo-governance-and-ownership]]).

## Key Takeaways

- Bimbo completed 24 acquisitions between 2021 and mid-2026 (six in 2021, one in 2022, six in 2023, five in 2024, five in 2025, plus Bonel in April 2026) [sourced] RA25 p.75-78; 1T26 p.2, with cash paid of MXN ~41.0 bn in FY21-FY25 [calc].
- FY25 was the largest M&A year since FY21 (MXN 11.3 bn cash plus 1.5 bn for the Colombian minority), concentrated in Brazil (Wickbold), the Balkans (Don Don), Romania and US sourdough [sourced] RA25 p.220-222.
- The one major disposal in the window is Ricolino to Mondelez (MXN 25.8 bn / US$1.37 bn, Nov-2022), which funded the FY22-FY24 investment cycle and low FY22 leverage (1.5x) [sourced] RA25 p.76; RA22 p.133, p.220.
- Deal-level prices and earnings contributions are not disclosed in the filings; derived consideration figures here are approximations.
- Integration is currently a margin drag in Latin America (Wickbold) and a growth tailwind for EAA and Latin America sales.
