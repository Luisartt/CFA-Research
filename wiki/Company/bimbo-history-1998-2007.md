# Grupo Bimbo 1998–2007: expansion, acquisitions, structure, management and capital

What the filings say about Grupo Bimbo's first full decade as an internationalising group: Mrs. Baird's (1998), the exit from milling (1999), the Weston western-US bakeries (2002), the transformation programme (2000–2004), the shift to a Mexican bolt-on strategy (2004–2006) and the Latin American roll-up of 2007. Financial detail: [[bimbo-income-statement-fy98-fy07]] and [[bimbo-balance-sheet-and-debt-fy98-fy07]]. The IFRS-era story starts in [[bimbo-acquisitions]] (FY21+). MXN millions in each report's own constant pesos unless stated; Mexican GAAP/NIF, inflation-restated.

## 3. Timeline by year

### 1998 — Mrs. Baird's, a stock split and seven "organisations"
- May-1998: acquires 100% of Mrs. Baird's Bakeries, Texas (10 plants; annual sales above USD 300m). Total 1998 investment USD 410.4m. Financed by a USD 200m Bank of Montreal loan on 17-Aug-1998. In the stub period Baird's added 2,237 of sales and 77 of operating profit [sourced] AR98 p.4-5, p.14, p.24.
- The Group is run as seven "organisations": Bimbo, Marinela, Barcel, Ricolino, Altex (wheat milling and agro-industrial), Latin America and US operations (consolidated into Bimbo Bakeries USA, Inc.) [sourced] AR98 p.4-6.
- Other 1998 moves: Kodiz (tortillas/tostadas, Monterrey) bought; the Tía Rosa sweet-bread line moved from Marinela to Bimbo (distribution with Wonder); new plants in Hidalgo (Industrial de Maíz) and La Paz, B.C.; Park Lane Confectionery (Germany) bought; Navojoa wheat mill bought; ice cream exited (40% of Quan sold, May-1998) and the Chilean savoury snacks business sold; Peru plant started in June [sourced] AR98 p.4-5, p.19.
- Latin America (about 9% of sales) lost 321 at operating level; Argentina, Venezuela, Colombia and Peru named as loss-makers [sourced] AR98 p.4, p.14.
- 4-for-1 split approved 19-Feb-1998; cash increase of 27.4m shares on 28-Apr-1998 [sourced] AR98 p.25.
- Management: the chairman writes of "the new administration" continuing the previous one's work (CEO change in 1997, see section 6) [sourced] AR98 p.6.

### 1999 — focus: mills out, Europe and Mexico bolt-ons in
- Feb-1999: 50% alliance with Day-Hoff (US confectionery distribution). Mar-1999: Four-S Baking (California) bought; 51% of Pastas Cora, Pastas Cora de la Laguna and Rex (with Grupo Mac'Ma). Jul-1999: bread assets in Cali, Colombia. Sep-1999: exclusive supply of buns to McDonald's in Colombia, Venezuela and Peru (Mexico since 1985). Oct-1999: Tulipán brand and Panacea assets, Costa Rica (52.1% market share at year-end). Nov-1999: two Ricolino plants in Europe (chocolates in Vienna; gums in Ostrava), opened Apr-2000 [sourced] AR99 p.5-6, p.14, p.18; AR07 p.23.
- 23-Dec-1999: sale of six wheat flour mills and the fruit-and-vegetable processors (Altex) to Cocape, a group led by Roberto Servitje Achútegui, until then Group vice-president. Price USD 106m (USD 134.5m in AR99 and USD 140.6m in AR00/AR07 including liabilities); gain MXN 133m; the buyer paid USD 9.4m in convertible subordinated notes [sourced] AR99 p.6, p.17, p.26; AR00 p.21, p.29.
- 13-May-1999: public offering of 54.2m shares for MXN 1,073m. Name changed to Grupo Bimbo, S.A. de C.V. (EGM 10-Aug-1999). Included in the IPC index from 1-Feb-1999 [sourced] AR99 p.18, p.25; AR07 p.14.
- US operations consolidated into two vehicles (Texas and California) [sourced] AR07 p.23. Net debt/equity falls from 33.9% to 13.6% [sourced] AR99 p.4.

### 2000 — Bimbo XXI and four plant openings
- March: Bimbo XXI (ERP on Oracle, Sun and Cap Gemini Ernst & Young, first announced in 1999; Ernst & Young adviser) [sourced] AR00 p.7-8; AR07 p.23. Its cost shows in selling and administration expense through FY2002 [sourced] AR00 p.20; AR01 p.30; AR02 p.24.
- Four plants opened: Bimbo Tijuana, Bimbo Cali (Colombia), Park Lane Vienna and Ostrava. Pan del Hogar (artisan bread, Cuautitlán) project started; an e-business project announced [sourced] AR00 p.7.
- Acquisitions: Pan Pyc (second-largest Peruvian bakery, Nov-2000), La Mejor (Guatemala, Dec-2000) [sourced] AR00 p.7, p.21.
- Mexico operations of Bimbo and Marinela regionalised; three board committees formalised on CNBV best-practice recommendations (Finance and Planning, Compensation, Audit); new directors added [sourced] AR00 p.7-8.
- Reports no price increases in main Mexican lines; US reorganisation and systems problems cause a loss there; net debt/equity falls to about zero [sourced] AR00 p.6, p.19.

### 2001 — Brazil, the tender offer and the USD 400m loan
- Mar-2001: Plus Vita (Brazil, Sao Paulo/Rio/Recife; Pullman, Plus Vita, Ana María, Van Mill) bought for USD 63.5m; operating loss of 76 in nine months [sourced] AR01 p.10, p.41; AR07 p.24.
- Aug-2001: public tender offer for 238.8m shares at MXN 17.25 (MXN 4,119m, ~17% of shares). Oct-2001: USD 400m syndicated loan. Nov-2001: Gruma bread assets (Costa Rica fresh and frozen bread incl. Breddy; equipment from the closed Escobedo plant) for USD 70m [sourced] AR01 p.8, p.10, p.32.
- Oct-2001: Pastas Cora sold to Grupo La Moderna for 4.5m La Moderna shares (5.8% of its capital) [sourced] AR07 p.24.
- Mexican legal entities simplified from "almost 40" to three (chairman's letter); the two operating companies Bimbo, S.A. de C.V. and Barcel, S.A. de C.V. take over all operations from 1-Jan-2002 [sourced] AR01 p.9, p.32.
- Jan-2002: purchase of the Weston western-US bakeries agreed (USD 610m), closed on 4-Mar-2002 [sourced] AR01 p.10, p.55.

### 2002 — Weston: the largest purchase in the Group's history
- 4-Mar-2002: acquires, via BBU, the western-US bakery business of George Weston Ltd. for USD 610m (a bridge loan of USD 680m): five plants (Texas, Colorado, California, Oregon), the Oroweat bread brand, ~1,300 direct routes, the Entenmann's licence for the western US, and the Thomas' and Boboli distribution rights in the region. 2001 sales of that business were USD 620m and EBITDA USD 56m [sourced] AR02 p.37; BMV02 p.11.
- Debt: certificates MXN 6,500m placed May and August; bridge repaid in full on 7-Aug-2002 [sourced] AR02 p.27.
- 1-Jan-2002: Bimbo, S.A. de C.V. (34 plants) and Barcel, S.A. de C.V. (10 production centres, incl. confectionery and cajeta) absorb all Mexican operating companies [sourced] AR02 p.27. 11-Dec-2002: merger with Central Impulsora, so the Group's holding company owns the main brands [sourced] AR07 p.25.
- Charges and closures: Texas routes sold to independent operators; Mrs. Baird's Dallas plant closed; Vienna plant closed; Abastex (Mexico retail distribution) partly suspended; EDS outsources IT assets and staff from 1-Mar-2002; 80% of the ERP and ~15,200 hand-held computers rolled out [sourced] AR02 p.8, p.24-26; BMV03 p.43.
- Day-Hoff stake raised to 70%. "Reforestamos México" created [sourced] AR07 p.23; AR02 p.7.

### 2003 — repair year, tax win and impairment
- 21-Jan-2003: exclusive Mexican distribution of Wrigley gum (Doublemint, Juicy Fruit, Orbit, Spearmint, Winterfresh) through Barcel [sourced] AR02 p.27; AR07 p.25.
- Divestment of the 41.8% stake in Novacel (flexible packaging) with partner Arteva to Pechiney for USD 90m in total (Jun/Aug-2003) [sourced] AR03 p.36-37. Jul-2003: minority (30%) in a consortium led by Fernando Chico Pardo that bought assets and debt rights of Compañía de Alimentos Fargo (Argentina) [sourced] AR03 p.37.
- Fuchs (Santiago, Chile) bakery acquired; Fripan frozen bread plant in Lerma begins (Mar-2004, USD 11.5m, with Rich's Products) [sourced] AR03 p.9; AR04 p.27.
- Abastex closed; Dallas plant closure prepared; "La Mejor" plant folded into Guatemala; routes opened 1,765 while 1,800 positions were cut [sourced] AR03 p.8, p.34.
- Supreme Court ruling of 12-Nov-2003 allows deduction of 2001 share-sale losses (MXN 1,606m recognised; an additional MXN 4,861m of deductible losses) [sourced] AR03 p.37. C-15 adopted early: impairment of MXN 1,834m (mainly US) [sourced] AR03 p.35, p.41. Prepayments of USD 263m [sourced] AR03 p.36.
- US bread category down ~5.5% in the year (low-carbohydrate diets); Oroweat launches "Carb Counting" bread with the Atkins Physicians Council [sourced] AR03 p.32-33.

### 2004 — Joyco, an extraordinary dividend and the first clean US numbers
- 18-Mar-2004 agreement (closed 13-May-2004): Joyco de México, Alimentos Duval and Lolimen (from a Mexican group and Spain's Corporación Agrolimen) for MXN 290m cash, of which ~MXN 27m for financial liabilities: two plants and the Duvalín, Bocadín and Lunetas brands; ~MXN 500m of annual sales; Bimbo becomes #2 in Mexican confectionery. Day-Hoff acquired in full [sourced] AR04 p.6-7, p.27; AR07 p.23, p.25.
- Closures: Dallas plant; La Paz B.C.; Alajuela (Costa Rica); La Mejor (Guatemala); Tía Rosa Ohio sold; 15% of US SKUs cut; plants end at 71 [sourced] AR04 p.5, p.23.
- Extraordinary dividend of MXN 0.60 on 8-Nov-2004 (MXN 705m), the second since the 1980 IPO [sourced] AR04 p.27.
- First positive US EBITDA (MXN 46m, 0.3% margin); the US lost USD ~24m and Latin America ~USD 14m at operating level [sourced] AR04 p.5, p.26.
- Revolver USD 250m signed 21-May-2004; "Vision 2010": to be "the world leader in baking" [sourced] AR04 p.7, p.40.

### 2005 — 60 years, Mexican bolt-ons and first profits abroad
- La Corona (chocolate and confectionery assets and brands, MXN 471m, closed 29-Jul-2005), Pastelerías El Globo (fine pastry retail, MXN 1,350m cash, closed 23-Sep-2005; 4 production units, 170+ stores), Lalo (Colombian bakery), Lagos del Sur (Chilean alfajores) and, in December, Corporación PVC de Guatemala (USD 14.5m) [sourced] AR05 p.7, p.35; AR06 p.41-42.
- 30-Sep-2005: distribution agreement with Arcor (Bon o Bon) and a planned joint plant in Mexico [sourced] AR05 p.35.
- Plant rationalisation: La Mirada (California), Naucalpan (Ricolino, from Joyco) and a Costa Rican plant closed; Coronado cajeta moved into the San Luis Potosí plant; 72 plants and 3 trading companies [sourced] AR05 p.7-8.
- First positive operating income ever in both the US (MXN 75m) and Latin America (MXN 52m). Shared Services Center created (all Mexican administration moved in by year-end); revolver upsized to USD 600m on 20-Jul-2005 [sourced] AR05 p.7, p.9, p.32-33, p.48.

### 2006 — Asia, S.A.B. and the first years of positive foreign profits
- 30-Jan-2006: Walter M. Doldán and Los Sorchantes (Montevideo, Uruguay; USD 7m, of which USD 5.5m for equity). 24-Mar-2006: 98% of Beijing Panrico Food Processing Center (China; EUR 9.2m plus EUR 1.3m net debt; 800 staff, one plant) — first Asian operation. 19-Jun-2006: assets and brands of El Molino pastry shops (MXN 42m, 2005 sales MXN 45m). Late 2006: Productos Roma (Medellín; a fourth Colombian plant), Quizz (powdered drinks) and a Guatemalan bakery ("Pan Europa" per the chairman's letter) [sourced] AR06 p.7, p.41; AR07 p.26.
- Five plants closed (Caucagua in Venezuela, and four inherited through La Corona, Duvalín, Productos Confitados and Industrial de Maíz Monterrey) [sourced] AR06 p.7.
- 14-Nov-2006 EGM and 16-Nov-2006 deed: new statutes under the Securities Market Law; company becomes S.A.B.; Audit and Corporate Practices committees replace the earlier committee set [sourced] AR06 p.31; AR07 p.94.
- Sale of the stake in Agusa (gain in other income); Fargo litigation in New York (a bondholder petition was dismissed on 12-Oct-2007) [sourced] AR06 p.34; AR07 p.27. Union agrees an additional retirement fund [sourced] AR06 p.8.

### 2007 — Latin American roll-up and the Interstate Bakeries (IBC) bid that did not happen
- Seven deals (MXN m, Dec-2007 pesos): La Favorita (Panama, 31-Jan) 9; Agua de Piedra (Chile, 7-Feb) 46; Moderna assets and brands (Panama, 30-May) 57; Pan Catalán (Uruguay, 12-Jun) 21; Rolly's Pastelería and King Baker Home (Peru, 21-Jun) 77; El Maestro Cubano Florentino Sande (Uruguay, 31-Jul) 93; Temis (Paraguay, 31-Oct) 17. Total 320 `[calc]`; goodwill booked 97 `[calc]` [sourced] AR07 p.112.
- 5-Nov-2007: announces, with Yucaipa Companies and the Teamsters, an intention to propose a reorganisation plan for Interstate Bakeries Corporation (Chapter 11 since Sep-2004; Wonder, Hostess, Dolly Madison, Merita; 40+ plants, 6,400 routes, ~25,000 staff). 13-Dec-2007: Bimbo says it is "not in a position" to present a proposal [sourced] AR07 p.26-27.
- 28-Nov-2007: Fargo (30% indirect holding) agrees a reorganisation with creditors paying 33.81% of verified unsecured debt; the company had been in Argentine insolvency (concurso preventivo) since Jun-2002 [sourced] AR07 p.26.
- 10-May-2007: MXN 2,750m Bimbo 02 certificate repaid. Capex 4,330, the highest of the decade; Latin America sales +21.1% with 77,000 new customers [sourced] AR07 p.75, p.110, p.119.
- Organised in two divisions: bakery (Bimbo S.A., El Globo, BBU, OLA) and salty snacks and confectionery (Barcel); European and Asian results consolidated in Mexico [sourced] AR07 p.9, p.30.

## 8. What the decade's strategy was, in the company's words

1. **Focus on consumer-facing baking, snacks and confectionery** and exit integration (mills sold Dec-1999; Novacel sold 2003; agro-industrial division closed) [sourced] AR99 p.6, p.14; AR03 p.36-37.
2. **Build a second home market in the US** (Baird's 1998, Four-S 1999, Weston 2002), reaching 24–26% of sales; the US lost money until 2005 [sourced] AR02 p.4; AR05 p.7.
3. **Systems and route specialisation** (Bimbo XXI/ERP, hand-held computers, channel-specialised routes, outsourced IT) cost margin through FY2003 and delivered it from FY2004 [sourced] AR02 p.8; AR03 p.8, p.34; AR04 p.7.
4. **Deleverage after each large move** (bridge repaid in five months; USD 263m of prepayments in 2003) [sourced] AR02 p.27; AR03 p.36.
5. **Bolt-ons in Mexico and Latin America** (Joyco, La Corona, El Globo, El Molino, seven 2007 Latin American bakeries) rather than another US mega-deal; the IBC bid was explored and abandoned [sourced] AR05 p.9; AR07 p.26-27.
6. **Health and nutrition positioning** (Bimbo Doble Fibra, Oroweat, reformulations) and a "Vision 2010" to lead world baking [sourced] AR04 p.7-9.

## Key Takeaways

- Two deals define the decade: Mrs. Baird's (1998) and the USD 610m Weston purchase (2002, 0.98x sales and ~10.9x EBITDA `[calc]`); together they turned the US into 24–27% of sales, but the US only earned a positive operating profit in 2005.
- ==The strategy was focus plus a second home market==: mills and packaging were sold (Dec-1999, 2003), the US was built, and Bimbo kept deleveraging after each large move (bridge repaid in five months).
- Corporate and systems restructuring (ERP, hand-helds, route specialisation, two merged Mexican operating companies) is the main reason margins fell in FY2001–FY2003 and recovered by FY2005.
- From 2004 the model changed to many small deals: Joyco (MXN 290m), La Corona (471m), El Globo (1,350m), first Asian entry (China, 2006) and seven Latin American bakeries (MXN 320m, 2007); the IBC bid in Nov-2007 was explored and dropped within six weeks.
- Control and management were stable: the Servitje and Jorba families hold ~70% through seven vehicles, Roberto Servitje Sendra has chaired since 1994 and Daniel Servitje Montull has been CEO since 1997.
- Scale tripled in a decade: net sales MXN 25.1bn to 72.3bn (own-year pesos), employees 64,685 to 91,289, routes 20,000 to 35,000+, plants 70 to 80, countries 16 to 18.
