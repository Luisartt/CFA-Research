# Grupo Bimbo — Corporate History 2008–2013

Deal-by-deal record of how Bimbo became the largest US baker and added Iberia and Argentina: acquisitions with price and financing, segment changes, the NIF to IFRS transition and debt issuance. Units: ==MXN millions== unless stated (historical pesos at deal date); US$ and EUR as stated. Financial effects are in [[bimbo-income-statement-fy08-fy13]], [[bimbo-balance-sheet-and-debt-fy08-fy13]] and [[bimbo-quarterly-trend-2008-2013]]; the FY21+ M&A log is [[bimbo-acquisitions]].

**Citation key.** RA08–RA14 = Bimbo's annual reports to the BMV for FY2008–FY2014 (`filings/bmv/BIMBO_Reporte_Anual_2008_esp_2.pdf`, `Grupo_Bimbo_Reporte_Anual_CNBV_2009_25.06.10_ESP.pdf`, `Bimbo_ReporteAnual_2010_port_1_0.pdf`, `infoanua_0.pdf` (FY2011), `Reporte_Anual_2012_FINAL.pdf`, `Grupo_Bimbo_Reporte_Anual_2013__0.pdf`, `BIMBO_Reporte_Anual_2014__final.pdf`); `p.` = PDF page index. Where a price is stated it is the figure in the filing; multiples are `[calc]`. Deal "sales" columns are the acquiree's annual sales as listed by Bimbo, not the amount consolidated.

## 4. NIF to IFRS transition

- The CNBV required BMV issuers to report under IFRS from 1-Jan-2012; FY2012 is Bimbo's first IFRS annual report, with 1-Jan-2011 as the transition date and FY2011 restated as comparative `[sourced]` RA12 p.10, p.209.
- Effects: total equity 1-Jan-2011 44,537 (NIF) → 41,853 (IFRS); FY2011 net income 5,660 → 5,206; no cash-flow effect `[sourced]` RA12 p.210. Main items: componentised PP&E and revised useful lives, reversal of inflation re-expression of goodwill and intangibles (1999–2007 amounts), employee-benefit recognition, deferred PTU eliminated, debt issuance costs netted against debt, and deferred tax on translation recycled `[sourced]` RA12 p.210–211.
- Exemptions: business combinations before 1-Jan-2011 not restated (Weston stays on NIF values); deemed cost for PP&E; actuarial gains/losses recognised at transition; cumulative translation reset to zero; IAS 23 only prospectively `[sourced]` RA12 p.209.
- Presentation: PTU inside operating costs (it sat below operating profit under NIF); pension financing costs moved from operating to financing in 2012 `[sourced]` RA12 p.99, p.212.
- Bimbo first reported IFRS quarterly with 1Q12 (restating 1Q11 sales to 29,500 from 29,312 NIF) `[sourced]` `filings/quarterly/Grupo_Bimbo_PR_1T12_0.pdf` p.2; `PR_1T11_0.pdf` p.2.
- The Sara Lee purchase allocation was finalised under IFRS with higher goodwill (9,361 vs 8,451 NIF) and a larger pension exit liability (8,354 vs 7,353) `[sourced]` RA12 p.147; RA11 p.140.

## 5. Debt issuance and the Canada Bread context

Debt issuance by date and purpose is in [[bimbo-balance-sheet-and-debt-fy08-fy13]]; the sequence was bridge and bank loan (Jan-2009), local peso bonds (Jun-2009), US$ 144A bonds (Jun-2010, Jan-2012), US$1.3bn syndicated loan (Apr-2011), euro loan (Oct-2011), peso bond (Feb-2012) and a US$2bn revolver (Dec-2013) `[sourced]`.

**Canada Bread (agreed 12-Feb-2014, closed 23-May-2014).** Bimbo agreed to buy all shares of Canada Bread (TSX: CBY) for C$1,830m; Maple Leaf Foods owned ~90% and committed to vote in favour through a court-approved plan of arrangement needing two-thirds of votes cast; Canada Bread had ~5,400 employees, 25 plants (Canada, US, UK), annual sales of C$1,439m and EBITDA of C$185m; funding from own resources and committed credit lines, with pro forma total debt / EBITDA guided at 2.9–3.1x `[sourced]` RA13 p.36, p.216 `[guidance]` for the leverage range. The acquisition closed on 23-May-2014 for a cash price of C$1,830m, entering Canada and the UK `[sourced]` RA14 p.42. This is the first deal after the sample period; see [[bimbo-segments-and-regions]] for today's Bimbo Canada (17 bakeries).

## Key Takeaways

- ==Two US deals made Bimbo's current shape: Weston Foods (US$2.5bn, Jan-2009, 8.7x EBITDA) and Sara Lee North America (US$749m, Nov-2011, 6.9x closing EBITDA)==; together they raised the US from 22% to ~45% of sales `[calc]`.
- Weston was financed with US$2.3bn of new debt and lifted debt / EBITDA from 1.1x to 2.3x; Sara Lee, Iberia and Fargo took it to 3.0–3.1x; refinancing into US$ bonds and peso bonds (2009–2012) extended average maturity from 3.2 to 5.9 years `[sourced]`.
- Sara Lee came with a pension exit liability (MXN 7.4–8.4bn) and divestiture conditions; it lost money at the operating line in 2012 (−677) and diluted margins before synergies of ~US$120m began to show `[sourced]`.
- Iberia (EUR 115m) opened Europe but lost 451 of EBITDA in FY2012; it is the ancestor of today's EAA segment `[sourced]`.
- The reporting basis changed twice: a new Iberia segment (Q4 2011) and IFRS from FY2012 with restated FY2011; pre-2011 numbers are not comparable with 2012+ on EBITDA, pensions or PTU `[sourced]`.
- Canada Bread (C$1,830m, 9.9x equity price over EBITDA, closed 23-May-2014) is the next transaction and was pre-announced with a 2.9–3.1x leverage guide `[sourced]`/`[guidance]`.
