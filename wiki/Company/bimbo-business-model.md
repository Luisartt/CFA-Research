## 1. One-line model

Grupo Bimbo bakes perishable, branded grain-based food in ==249 owned/leased bakeries and plants== and delivers it fresh, mostly through its own direct-store-delivery (DSD) route network, to everything from corner stores to Walmart to fast-food chains. The filing states bakery and salty snacks are a single line of business; the split is organizational only [sourced] RA25 p.122-123.

## 4. Route to market: direct-store-delivery

**Mechanics** [sourced] RA25 p.100:
- Product leaves bakeries/plants for >1,700 sales centers and agencies; sales force follows predetermined itineraries. Visit frequency (daily, every 3 days, twice weekly, weekly) depends on customer volume, channel and profile.
- Shelf life ranges from 14 days (bread) to several months (cookies, snacks), printed on pack.
- Returns: every route can pick up returned or unsold product at each visit and replace it with fresh product at no charge. Picked-up product goes to discount outlets or animal feed, depending on remaining shelf life and condition.
- Customer classes: hypermarkets, supermarkets, price clubs, family businesses, foodservice (institutional, quick-service restaurants, schools, vending) and traditional stores.

**Owned vs independent routes** [sourced] RA25 p.100:

| Region | Route ownership |
|---|---|
| Mexico | All routes operated directly |
| Latin America | Most routes operated directly |
| United States | More than half operated by independent distributors |
| Canada, Europe | Most routes operated by independent distributors |

- Independent distributors sign long-term exclusive contracts; the contract fixes territory and compensation (based on sales performance). Bimbo keeps control of brand management, advertising strategy and pricing, and holds a right to repurchase routes in certain circumstances [sourced] RA25 p.100.
- The filing's rationale: independent distributors in the US lower distribution cost and add flexibility to add points of sale [sourced] RA25 p.100.

**How BBU (US) finances its independent operators** [sourced] RA25 p.249-250 (note 7, structured entities):
- Independent business partners hold rights to distribute in defined territories; BBU finances up to 97.5% of the route purchase price (receivables at 9.75%-11% annual interest, weighted average 10%, 120 monthly payments); the partner pays 2.5% up front, often financed by a third party.
- Partners organised as legal entities qualify as **structured entities** and are **consolidated** by the company; intercompany balances are eliminated.

## 5. Pricing

- Stated policy: pricing is tied to market conditions and input/operating cost, aims for low-cost production to offer competitive prices, and also weighs competition, product sensitivity and potential, and market research [sourced] RA25 p.98.
- In results commentary pricing shows up as "price/mix": positive in FY25 consolidated sales growth (4T25 p.2), and in 2Q26 sales growth of 4.5% ex-FX attributed to favorable price mix, positive volume and acquisitions (2T26 p.2) [sourced]. The releases do not give a numeric price vs volume split [sourced] 4T25, 2T26.
- Industry framing in the annual report: price and perceived quality are the two competitive factors; private label gains when macro pressure raises price weight [sourced] RA25 p.109. See [[bakery-market-structure]] and [[bimbo-competitive-position]].

## 6. Customers and channels

- Largest customer: Walmart (all banners) ~15% of FY25 sales; no other customer >10% [sourced] RA25 p.103. Audited note: 14.85% (FY25), 14.97% (FY24), 17.77% (FY23), mostly Mexico and North America [sourced] IA25-EEFF p.86.
- Named customers by type [sourced] RA25 p.102-103 (region mapping in the extraction is partly garbled, so names are not allocated to every region):
  - United States: Walmart, Kroger, Albertsons, Ahold-Delhaize, Costco, Sam's Club. Canada: Sobeys, Costco, Walmart, Metro, Loblaw.
  - Mexico: Walmart, Oxxo, Chedraui, Soriana, Bodega Aurrera; quick-service restaurants such as McDonald's and Burger King.
  - Europe: Mercadona, Tesco, Lidl, Carrefour, Sainsbury's, Costco; quick-service restaurants.
  - Asia / Africa: Walmart (Asia), McDonald's, Wendy's, Burger King, KFC.
  - E-commerce: Amazon, FreshDirect, Peapod, ShopRite and others.
- Channel structure differs by development stage: modern trade is 53.3% of global bakery sales and ~77.7% in North America; Latin America and MENA rely 59%-60% on specialist food stores and traditional outlets [sourced] RA25 p.110 (Global Data). Grocery e-commerce in fast-moving consumer goods grew 15.3% in 2025 (NielsenIQ) [sourced] RA25 p.110.
- **Bimbo QSR** (frozen buns and breads for fast-food chains) is a separate business: 40 countries, 91% of the global QSR market per GlobalData (2025); headquarters in Ohio [sourced] RA25 p.87. It reports inside the regional segments (North America plants table lists 4 QSR bakeries, RA25 p.115; EAA growth commentary cites Bimbo QSR, 4T25 p.5).

## 7. Inputs and vertical integration

- Main raw material: wheat flour (traded in US dollars, hedged); others: sugar, edible oils, fats, eggs, packaging resins. Plastic packaging is hedged [sourced] RA25 p.97. Hedging detail in [[bimbo-input-costs]] and [[bimbo-fx-exposure]].
- Supply integration is partial: corporate leads negotiation of main inputs, plants manage inventories (FIFO) and buy local items; minority stakes in some key egg and sugar suppliers; no dependence on a single supplier in any market [sourced] RA25 p.97.
- Captive/associated suppliers appear in related-party notes: Beta San Miguel (raw materials), cookie maker Fabrica de Galletas La Moderna, uniforms and stationery (Efform, Uniformes y Equipo Industrial), Fin Comun (financial services), Pan-Glo (finished goods) [sourced] RA25 p.273-274 (full list in [[bimbo-governance-and-ownership]]).
- Equipment and displays in-house: Moldes y Exhibidores operates as one of Mexico's 38 production units [sourced] RA25 p.115.
- Finished-goods inventory turns fast; dry goods (toasted bread, breadcrumbs, cookies, snacks) turn slower [sourced] RA25 p.98.
- Seasonality: higher consumption in holiday periods, rainy season and cold weather; offset by promotions and launches in weak periods, and not synchronous across the group's geographies [sourced] RA25 p.89.

## Key Takeaways

- Bimbo is a **fresh, short-shelf-life, DSD** business: ~36% of sales is distribution and selling vs ~48% cost of sales, so route density and fleet productivity matter as much as bakery efficiency [calc] RA25 p.217.
- Route model is **geography-dependent**: owned routes in Mexico/LatAm, independent distributors (financed by BBU up to 97.5% of route price, consolidated as structured entities) in the US, Canada and Europe [sourced] RA25 p.100, p.249-250.
- Plants run well below 24/7 capacity (43%-66% utilization), so volume recovery or footprint consolidation both lift margins [sourced] RA25 p.116-117.
- Customer concentration is moderate: Walmart ~15% of sales, no other customer >10% [sourced] RA25 p.103.
- Pricing is cost-plus-market driven; filings quantify price/mix only qualitatively, so a numeric price/volume bridge must come from outside the filings [sourced] 4T25, 2T26.
- Data gaps to close: split of distribution and selling expense; headcount by region (only rounded figures in the integrated report); route economics by owned vs independent.
