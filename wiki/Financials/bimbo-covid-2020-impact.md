# Grupo Bimbo — COVID-19 Impact in 2020

How the pandemic year looked in the numbers: quarterly P&L against 2019, regional and channel effects, volume and price evidence, one-off costs, liquidity and cash, and what to normalise before using FY20 as a base. Units: ==MXN millions==; IFRS. 2019 comparatives are the **recast** figures in the 2020 releases (IFRS 16 basis for EBITDA). Context: [[bimbo-income-statement-fy14-fy20]], [[bimbo-quarterly-trend-2014-2020]], [[bimbo-balance-sheet-and-debt-fy14-fy20]], [[bimbo-cash-flow-and-capex-fy14-fy20]], [[bimbo-history-2014-2020]].

Source key: 1T20/2T20/3T20/4T20 = `filings/quarterly/Grupo-Bimbo-Reporta-Resultados-del-1T20_0 / 2T20 / 3T20 / 4T20_0.pdf` (printed page); RA20 = `filings/bmv/Grupo-Bimbo-Reporte-Anual-2020-BMV.pdf` (PDF page); IA20 = `filings/annual/GB-Informe-Anual-2020_0_1.pdf`.

> [!warning] Basis and comparability
> - Adj. EBITDA in 2020 is on the **IFRS 16 basis** and the 2019 comparatives were recast to it (FY19 37,873 vs the 33,426 headline of the 4T19 release); net debt/EBITDA excludes IFRS 16 `[sourced]` 4T20 p.2, p.6-7. Do not compare 2020 with FY18 or FY19 headline figures.
> - Net income, controlling, in 1Q20 (20) includes a non-cash multi-employer pension (MEPP) charge, not a COVID effect; adjusted for it, 1Q20 net income was 1,880 (+42.3%) `[sourced]` 1T20 p.6. The 2020 MEPP and other non-cash item in North America is 2,494 (2019: 1,760) `[sourced]` RA20 PDF p.286.
> - COVID costs are described but never quantified in any filing except about MXN 200m of 1Q donations, so a "pure COVID" EBITDA cannot be derived.

## 5. Volume and price in 2020

- ==No volume figure is published by region or category.== The company says growth came from "favourable price mix, solid volume performance in all regions and FX" (4Q and FY) and from "outstanding volume growth in North America" (2Q, 3Q) `[sourced]` 4T20 p.3; 2T20 p.2-3; 3T20 p.3.
- Group production volume (TPE, tonnes of packaged product) rose 8.1% in 2020 to 5,025,741 tonnes (+7.1% against the restated 2019 of 4,693,880) `[calc]` from `[sourced]` IA20 PDF p.103; ESG annex PDF p.21. Net sales per tonne rose 4.9% (to 65.9 thousand MXN per tonne), so on this crude proxy about two-thirds of the 13.4% sales growth was tonnage and one third price/mix/FX/perimeter `[calc]` `[assumption]`. The production boundary moves with acquisitions (Lender's, Paterna), so some of the tonnage is acquired.
- North America US-dollar sales: +6% (1Q), +11.5% (2Q), +8.5% (3Q), +11.3% (4Q) `[sourced]` 1T20 p.3; 2T20 p.3; 3T20 p.3; 4T20 p.3. In 2019 the region was flat in pesos with private-label and breakfast volumes down `[sourced]` 4T19 p.3.
- Mexico: sales +6.2% (1Q, volume-led) then −1.2%, +0.8%, +1.6%; price/mix positive, volumes pressured by the closure of wholesale, convenience and food-service channels `[sourced]` 1T20 p.3; 2T20 p.4; 3T20 p.4; 4T20 p.4.

## 6. Costs and non-recurring items

- Distribution and selling expense rose 12.0% to 123,511 (37.3% of sales vs 37.8%); administration rose 34.5% to 22,383 (6.8% vs 5.7% of sales; the filings do not split it, but accrued remuneration and bonuses were 11,473 vs 8,517 and COVID costs are named `[unverified]`); integration expenses fell 19% to 1,968; other expenses rose 28.9% to 5,173 `[calc]` from `[sourced]` RA20 PDF p.195, p.249. The company links the margin to lower raw-material costs, lower integration/restructuring and productivity, partly offset by "COVID-19 related expenses in all regions" `[sourced]` 4T20 p.5.
- COVID expense categories named (not quantified): extra hiring, donations to associations supporting customers and consumers, sanitation measures at all plants and centres, one-time costs in Mexico (donations, labour, safety equipment) `[sourced]` RA20 PDF p.205; 2T20 p.5.
- Reported cost of sales fell as a share of sales: gross margin 53.9% vs 52.7% (+120 bps), helped by lower raw materials, lower overheads from past investments and restructuring `[sourced]` 4T20 p.4; 3T20 p.4.
- The company states it does not expect material changes to its operating and financial conditions from COVID in the short or long term `[sourced]` RA20 PDF p.205, while the risk factors say the effects "have exceeded the initial estimates" and may continue `[sourced]` RA20 PDF p.23.

## Key Takeaways

- ==2020 was Bimbo's best year to that point: sales +13.4%, Adj. EBITDA +19.3% (IFRS 16 to IFRS 16) to 45,193, net income +44.2%, and leverage down to 1.9x, the lowest in ten years.==
- North America (53% of sales) delivered 83% of the extra sales and 89% of the extra EBITDA on retail-channel and share gains plus a weaker peso (US-dollar sales about +9% for the year).
- Food service, QSR, convenience and wholesale were the victims (EAA QSR, Mexico convenience); Mexico EBITDA fell 3.4%; Latin America's EBITDA more than doubled.
- Liquidity was never a problem: a US$720m revolver draw in March (debt 120.1bn at Mar-20) was repaid by December and distributions (dividend 2.3bn, buybacks 3.7bn, 4% share cancellation) continued.
- FY20 flatters the base: peso translation, a 9bn working-capital inflow, a one-time demand shift and a MEPP charge that distorts operating income; no volume series exists except group tonnage (+8.1%).
