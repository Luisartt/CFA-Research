### Why debt jumped in 2023–2024
- Dec-22 → Dec-23: +25,881 total debt, mainly the reclassification of the USD perpetual subordinated notes from equity to debt (+8,845 in 2023; redeemed at the first call in March–April 2023 and refinanced with Mexican sustainability-linked certificates), plus record capex (> US$2bn) and acquisitions `[sourced]` 4Q23 release p.7; EEFF25 p.52 (reconciliation line "reclassification of equity instrument 8,845").
- Dec-23 → Dec-24: +40,970, driven by capex and acquisitions funding and by a ~20% depreciation of the peso against the dollar on USD debt `[sourced]` 4Q24 release p.7.
- Dec-24 → Dec-25: +2,758; acquisitions and capex offset by the stronger peso (FX revaluation −12,269 in the debt roll-forward) `[sourced]` EEFF25 p.52; 4Q25 release p.8.

## Key Takeaways

- ==Net debt more than doubled from 71.7bn (Dec-22) to 145.1bn (Dec-25)==; the ratio peaked at 2.9x (Dec-24) and had fallen to 2.5x by Jun-26 (net debt 137.1bn) `[sourced]`.
- Net debt excludes leases (34.8bn at Dec-25) and MEPP/pension items; on a with-leases basis against reported EBITDA, leverage is about 3.0x at Dec-25 and 2.8x at Jun-26 `[calc]`.
- The company's leverage ratio is consistent with net debt divided by Adj. EBITDA less right-of-use depreciation (6.1bn in FY25) `[calc]`; the exact definition is not disclosed `[unverified]`.
- Interest cover has compressed from 6.6x (FY22) to 4.1x (FY25) as the average cost stayed at 6.3–7.1% on a much larger stack `[calc]`; maturity is long (9.5 years at Jun-26) with 2027 maturities of ~10bn `[sourced]`.
- Dec-21/Dec-22 debt excludes the 8bn perpetual notes later reclassified as debt, and Dec-22 equity/benefit lines carry the MEPP release; do not compare 2021–22 leverage to 2023+ without adjusting.
