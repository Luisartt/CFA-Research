# Grupo Bimbo — Income Statement FY2008–FY2013

Annual P&L, regional sales and EBITDA (UAFIDA), margins and the effect of the Weston Foods (2009) and Sara Lee / Iberia / Fargo (2011) acquisitions. Units: ==MXN millions== unless stated; fiscal year = calendar year. Continues into [[bimbo-income-statement-fy21-fy25]] (FY2014–FY2020 is not covered by the wiki yet). Related: [[bimbo-history-2008-2013]], [[bimbo-balance-sheet-and-debt-fy08-fy13]], [[bimbo-quarterly-trend-2008-2013]], [[bimbo-ifrs-adjustments]].

**Citation key.** RA08, RA09, RA10, RA11, RA12, RA13 = Bimbo's annual reports to the BMV for that fiscal year (`filings/bmv/BIMBO_Reporte_Anual_2008_esp_2.pdf`, `Grupo_Bimbo_Reporte_Anual_CNBV_2009_25.06.10_ESP.pdf`, `Bimbo_ReporteAnual_2010_port_1_0.pdf`, `infoanua_0.pdf` (= FY2011), `Reporte_Anual_2012_FINAL.pdf`, `Grupo_Bimbo_Reporte_Anual_2013__0.pdf`). `p.` = PDF page index of the file, not the printed folio. "Selected data" = the Item 2 tables near the front of each report; "MD&A" = Item 3(d).

> [!warning] GAAP break: NIF to IFRS between FY2011 and FY2012
> FY2008–FY2011 are Mexican NIF; FY2012 is the first IFRS annual report (transition date 1-Jan-2011) and restates FY2011 `[sourced]` RA12 p.209–210 (note 24). The FY2011 column therefore exists twice below (NIF as filed in RA11, IFRS restated in RA12/RA13). Never chain growth rates across the break: FY2012 growth is computed on IFRS-restated FY2011 only. Line labels also change: NIF "utilidad después de gastos generales" (operating profit before other items) is not IFRS "utilidad de operación"; under IFRS the employee profit-sharing (PTU) sits inside operating costs and pension financing cost moved to financing `[sourced]` RA12 p.99, p.212.

> [!warning] Share split
> Bimbo split its shares 4-for-1 on 29-Apr-2011 `[sourced]` RA11 p.27, p.127. Per-share data for FY2008–FY2010 below are split-adjusted; RA08/RA09 print them pre-split (e.g. FY2009 EPS 5.07, DPS 0.46).

## 5. What the acquisitions did to margins

**Weston Foods / Bimbo Foods Inc. (BFI), closed 21-Jan-2009.** US sales rose from 18,049 to 49,850 (+31,801, +176%) and US EBITDA from 539 (3.0% margin) to 5,727 (11.5%) `[calc]` from RA09 p.88. Weston's trailing sales were US$2,197m and adjusted EBITDA about US$275m (~12.5% margin) to 4-Oct-2008, against Bimbo's legacy US business at ~3% `[sourced]` RA08 p.31. Group EBITDA margin therefore rose from 11.9% to 13.6% and gross margin from 51.1% to 52.8%; US gross margin went from 44.0% to 50.5% (BBU East's lower cost structure, lower commodities, a plant closure) `[sourced]` RA09 p.91, p.94. Group profit after general expenses rose 64.5% to 12,054 (10.4% margin) `[sourced]` RA09 p.92. Financing cost jumped from 539 to 2,012 on the acquisition debt, so net income grew only +37.9% against EBITDA +61.1% `[sourced]` RA09 p.92–93.

**Sara Lee US (6-Nov-2011), Iberia (5-Dec-2011), Fargo (19-Sep-2011).** These were margin-dilutive. For 55 days of FY2011 Sara Lee added sales of 4,074 and EBITDA of only 26 (0.6%); Iberia 392 of sales and (70) of EBITDA; Fargo 722 and 42 `[sourced]` RA12 p.150. For FY2012, Sara Lee contributed sales 25,816, an operating loss of (677) and EBITDA of 298 (1.2% margin); Iberia sales 5,182 and EBITDA (451); Fargo 2,441 and 41 `[sourced]` RA12 p.149. Excluding Sara Lee and Iberia, FY2012 EBITDA would have been about 14,229 on sales of 142,141, a 10.0% margin against 8.1% reported, so the two deals diluted group margin by roughly 1.9 pp `[calc]` (14,076 − 298 + 451; 173,139 − 25,816 − 5,182). Integration costs of 1,950 in FY2012 (US 1,553, Iberia 213, Latin America 121, Mexico 64) and a MEPP exit charge of 1,102 hit operating income, which fell 22.5% to 7,387 `[sourced]` RA12 p.99.

**Recovery, FY2013.** Gross margin recovered to 52.3% (+1.6 pp) on stable raw materials and a more efficient US manufacturing footprint; US EBITDA grew 15.3% to 5,798 (7.3% margin, still below the 11.5% reached after Weston) and the group EBITDA margin returned to 9.8% `[sourced]` RA13 p.84–85; `[calc]` for margins. US synergies and waste reduction were about US$120m in FY2012 `[sourced]` RA12 p.99. At announcement Bimbo guided US$150–200m of annual Sara Lee synergies by 2013 `[sourced]` RA10 p.37.

**Latin America as a drag.** Latin America EBITDA fell from 951 (FY2009) to (253) in FY2012 on new-route investment, a Brazilian plant opening, a Brazil goodwill impairment, a Venezuelan labour charge and Brazil restructuring; it was back to +103 in FY2013 `[sourced]` RA11 p.98; RA12 p.99; RA13 p.84.

## Key Takeaways

- ==Reported EBITDA margin peaked at 13.6% in FY2009 (Weston Foods) and fell to 8.1% in FY2012 (Sara Lee, Iberia, IFRS)==; it was 9.8% in FY2013 `[calc]`/`[sourced]`.
- Sales grew from 82,317 (FY2008) to 176,041 (FY2013), a 16.4% CAGR, but ~79% of the increase came in two acquisition-driven steps (+34,036 in FY2009 on the NIF base, +39,643 in FY2012 on the IFRS-restated base) `[calc]`; organic sales were flat to low growth in FY2010 (+0.7%) and FY2013 (+1.7%).
- Adding Weston Foods lifted the US EBITDA margin from 3.0% to 11.5% in one year; Sara Lee contributed 25,816 of FY2012 sales at a 1.2% EBITDA margin and diluted the group by ~1.9 pp `[calc]`.
- Net income (majority) fell from 5,956 (FY2009) to 2,028 (FY2012) while EBITDA moved from 15,837 to 14,076: financing cost (539 to 2,810), integration costs (1,950), MEPP charges and a 47.4% tax rate did the damage; FY2013 net income more than doubled to 4,404 `[sourced]`.
- Latin America moved from 951 of EBITDA (FY2009) to negative in FY2012 and Iberia lost 451 in FY2012; both were still dilutive at FY2013 (EBITDA margins of 0.5% and (3.8%)) `[calc]`.
- The NIF to IFRS change cut FY2011 EBITDA by 657 (4.3%) and net income by 454 (8.0%); never mix the two bases `[calc]`.
