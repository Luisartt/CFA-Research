> [!warning] Balance-sheet breaks and mismatches to know before using any series
> - **IFRS 16 (1-Jan-2019, modified retrospective, no restatement):** right-of-use assets (25,550) and lease liabilities (25,340) appear in Dec-19. Lease liabilities at 1-Jan-2019: 24,220 = operating lease commitments 17,114 + expected renewals 9,368 + other 1,241 = 27,723 undiscounted, discounted at a 3.8% weighted incremental rate to 21,023, plus 3,197 of finance leases already on balance sheet `[sourced]` RA19 PDF p.209. Dec-14 to Dec-18 carry no operating-lease liability: leverage before and after 2019 is not comparable unless leases are added back.
> - **Hybrid in equity:** the US$500m 5.95% perpetual subordinated notes (17-Apr-2018) sit in equity (9,138 at Dec-18; 8,931; 8,996) and are excluded from every "total debt" figure below `[sourced]` RA18 p.183; RA20 p.194. Adding them gives a Dec-18 leverage of (89,846 + 9,138) / 31,705 = 3.12x `[calc]` instead of the reported 2.8x. The ratio is not published on that basis.
> - **Release vs audited debt:** Dec-16 total debt is 82,500 in the 4T16 release but 81,803 (1,452 + 80,351) on the audited balance sheet and in the RA19 debt roll-forward opening balance; Dec-17 is 94,313 in the 4T17/1T18 releases but 93,431 audited (93,432 in the 4T18 release comparative) `[sourced]` 4T16 p.8; RA18 p.183; RA19 PDF p.252; 4T17 p.8; 4T18 p.8. The 697 and 882 differences are not explained in the documents reviewed `[unverified]`.
> - **Release vs audited assets:** Dec-14 total assets 177,120 in the 4T14 release, 177,760 in the 4T15 release comparative and 177,761 audited; Dec-17 259,155 in the 4T17 release vs 259,249 audited `[sourced]` 4T14 p.9; 4T15 p.12; RA15 p.135; 4T17 p.12; RA18 p.183. Use the audited figures.
> - **Reclassifications:** Dec-17 and Dec-18 "long-term receivables from independent operators" (557 / 404) were folded into other assets in RA19; Dec-19 trade payables were cut from 23,105 to 22,972 (related parties 1,064 to 1,197) in RA20 `[sourced]` RA19 PDF p.232; RA20 p.194. Segment total assets for Dec-20 show a 17,850 elimination against 1,675 in Dec-19, with no explanation in the note `[sourced]` RA20 p.286 `[unverified]`.
> - **Adoptions on 1-Jan-2018 (no restatement):** IFRIC 23 (−2,283), IFRS 15 (−157), IFRS 9 (+32) and IAS 29 for Argentina (−2,180) were booked to opening retained earnings `[sourced]` RA18 p.186.

## Key Takeaways

- ==Company-reported total debt/EBITDA went 3.4x (Dec-14, after Canada Bread) to 2.8x (Dec-16), back to 3.5x (Dec-17, Bimbo QSR) and then down every year to 2.1x at Dec-20; net debt/EBITDA fell from 2.7x (Dec-15) to 1.9x at Dec-20, the lowest in ten years `[sourced]` 4T20 p.2.==
- Reported debt stayed in a MXN 62–94bn band; the real drivers of the swings were acquisitions (+21,968 in FY14; +12,482 cash for acquisitions in FY17) and USD revaluation (−3,128 in FY19, +4,429 in FY20).
- Pre-2019 and post-2019 leverage are not comparable: IFRS 16 added 25,340 of lease liabilities in Dec-19 (29,089 in Dec-20) that stay outside company net debt; the US$500m perpetual (about 9bn) is in equity.
- Maturity extended from 8.3 years (Dec-16) to 13.2 years (Dec-20) with cost rising from 4.5% to 6.1% as cheap USD revolver money was replaced by long USD notes and peso certificates; peso share of debt rose from 10% to 40%.
- Working capital turned into a source of cash: trade working capital fell from 6.5% to 3.5% of sales and the cash conversion cycle turned negative in FY19-FY20.
- Equity is hostage to peso translation (OCI swings of ±7–8bn a year) and goodwill plus intangibles still equal about 1.4x equity.
