# Grupo Bimbo — Cash Flow and Capex FY2014–FY2020

Audited cash flows, capex intensity, acquisitions, distributions and a derived free cash flow for FY14–FY20. Units: ==MXN millions==; IFRS. Continues in [[bimbo-cash-flow-and-fcf]] (FY21 onward). Companion articles: [[bimbo-income-statement-fy14-fy20]], [[bimbo-balance-sheet-and-debt-fy14-fy20]], [[bimbo-history-2014-2020]], [[bimbo-covid-2020-impact]]. Source key: RA15 = `filings/bmv/BIMBO_Reporte_Anual_2015_FINAL.pdf`; RA16 = `filings/bmv/infoanua_vf_0.pdf`; RA18 = `filings/bmv/Grupo-Bimbo-Reporte-Anual-BMV-2018_0.pdf`; RA19 = `filings/bmv/Reporte-Anual-2019-Grupo-Bimbo-Consolidado_0.pdf`; RA20 = `filings/bmv/Grupo-Bimbo-Reporte-Anual-2020-BMV.pdf`; PDF page indexes.

> [!warning] How this cash-flow statement is built, and three comparability breaks
> - **Interest and leases sit in financing.** CFO is before interest paid (3,128 to 7,280 a year) and, from FY19, before lease payments (4,784 in FY19; 5,544 in FY20). "CFO minus capex" therefore overstates cash available to shareholders, and the overstatement jumps in FY19 by the IFRS 16 reclassification: the company states IFRS 16 added 4,151 to FY19 CFO, and the MEPP provision revaluation another 2,540 `[sourced]` RA19 PDF p.130. Compare FY14–FY18 with FY19–FY20 only after deducting lease payments (the FCF line below does).
> - **Restated FY19 investing and financing.** RA19 printed FY19 CFI (12,872) and CFF (16,603); RA20 re-presents them as (12,642) and (16,833), moving 230 of derivative settlements from investing to financing `[sourced]` RA19 PDF p.200; RA20 PDF p.198. FY16 CFO is 23,077 in RA16 and 23,127 in RA18 (CFF (4,333) vs (4,383)) `[sourced]` RA16 PDF p.99; RA18 PDF p.187.
> - **Press-release cash flows use another layout** and differ from the audited statement (table 3). Always take cash flows from the annual report.

- ==Capex intensity peaked at 5.2% of sales in FY16 and FY18== (PP&E only) and fell to 4.0% in FY20; PP&E capex ran at 1.5x D&A in FY16–FY18 before the IFRS 16 step-up in D&A (right-of-use depreciation) took the ratio below 1.0x. The releases and annual reports mention the new Córdoba plant in Argentina (FY16), the Guadalajara plant in Spain (FY15) and US route and plant optimisation as capex and restructuring themes `[sourced]` RA15 PDF p.42; RA16 PDF p.92; 4T18 p.6; the capex split by project is not disclosed `[unverified]`.
- ==FY18 is the only year with negative FCF after interest and leases (−1,810):== record capex (15,067), interest paid up 64% to 7,280 (higher debt from the 2017 acquisitions and a higher average cost, 5.97% vs 5.2% `[sourced]` 2T18 p.6; 4T17 p.8; 4T18 p.8) and employee-benefit payments of 2,809 `[sourced]` RA18 PDF p.187; 4T18 p.8.
- FY20 free cash flow after interest and leases of 17,941 is 4.1x FY19: operating earnings (+7,949), working capital (+9,047 swing) and lower capex per sale; see the bridge below. Distributions of 6,821 absorbed 38% of it and acquisitions 3,453, leaving 7,667 `[calc]`; gross debt fell by 1,443 despite a 4,429 FX revaluation and cash rose by 3,017 `[sourced]` RA20 PDF p.198, p.249.
- The company's own FY20 "gross cash flow" of 31,796 is defined before capex, acquisitions, dividends and buybacks `[sourced]` 4T20 p.2 fn 3. A rebuild from the audited statement as CFO less interest paid less lease payments gives 31,923, 127 higher `[calc]`; the company's formula is not published `[unverified]`. The FY19 "Flujo Libre de Caja" of 2,441 has no definition in the 4T19 release `[sourced]` 4T19 p.2, and no like-for-like audited line reproduces it `[unverified]`. Quarterly company FCF in 2020 (1Q 2,664; 1H 6,768; 9M 13,364, "before dividends and buybacks") is in [[bimbo-covid-2020-impact]].

## 5. Capex and investment narrative `[sourced]`

- FY14: capex 6,829; the Canada Bread purchase (22,351 of acquisitions cash) was funded by a US$2,000m revolver and own cash, then refinanced with US$1,300m of notes (24-Jun-2014) RA15 PDF p.41. No dividend was paid in FY14–FY15.
- FY15: capex 9,604 plus 1,060 of intangibles; new Guadalajara (Spain) plant able to bake 15,000 loaves per hour (RA15 PDF p.42); tax paid 3,884; derivative settlements (2,161) hit CFO.
- FY16: capex 13,153 (5.2% of sales, "mayor inversión en capital") and Panrico cash (3,966 of acquisitions including Argentina and Colombia frozen-bread assets) RA16 PDF p.99, p.133.
- FY17: capex 13,446; Bimbo QSR 9,641 net plus Ready Roti 1,305, Bay Foods 1,210 and others (12,482 total); funded by the 40,772 of borrowings, the MXN 10bn Bimbo 17 and the US$650m 2047 note RA18 PDF p.192-195.
- FY18: capex 15,067; Mankattan 2,921 plus three small deals (3,600 total); funded by the perpetual issue (8,986) RA18 PDF p.188-192.
- FY19: capex 13,117 (the first fall in four years), acquisitions only 94; buybacks 1,748 and dividends 2,103; lease payments 4,784 RA19 PDF p.200.
- FY20: management revised the capital plan and postponed some restructuring in the first half to preserve cash (note on COVID liquidity) RA20 PDF p.204; capex 13,218 (4.0% of sales); acquisitions 3,453 (Lender's, Siro Paterna, Blue Label México control, Ready Roti minority) RA20 PDF p.198-200.

## Key Takeaways

- ==Cumulative FY14–FY20: CFO 174.1bn funded capex of 84.4bn, acquisitions of 47.6bn, interest of 35.3bn and distributions of 16.7bn; gross debt still rose by 45.0bn (net debt +38.2bn), most of it traceable to the FY14 Canada Bread (22.4bn) and FY17 Bimbo QSR (12.5bn) cash outlays.==
- Capex intensity peaked at 5.2% of sales (FY16, FY18) and eased to 4.0% in FY20; FCF after interest and leases was positive in six of seven years and negative only in FY18 (−1.8bn).
- IFRS 16 inflated CFO from FY19 (+4,151 in FY19, per the company); use FCF after lease payments (4.4bn FY19; 17.9bn FY20) when comparing with earlier years.
- FY20 CFO of 43.9bn (+54%) was mostly working capital (+9.0bn swing) and higher operating earnings; roughly 1.8bn more tax was paid. Treat the payables and accrual inflows as non-recurring.
- Distributions rose from nil (FY14–FY15) to 6.8bn in FY20 (dividends 2.4bn, buybacks 3.7bn, perpetual coupons 0.6bn), 38% of FY20 FCF after interest and leases.
