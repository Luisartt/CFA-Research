# Bakery and salty-snack market structure (as stated by Grupo Bimbo)

Scope: only what Bimbo's own filings state, mostly quoting third-party data (Global Data, Nielsen, NielsenIQ, Innova, Circana) that Bimbo cites. No outside numbers. Source key: **RA25** = FY2025 annual report to the BMV (`filings/bmv/REPORTE ANUAL 2025 OFICIAL VF.pdf`; PDF page = printed page); **RA24** / **RA23** = FY2024 / FY2023 annual reports. Retail-value basis for Global Data tables is "RSP" (retail sales price, includes sales tax, US$ at 2024 FX) [sourced, RA25 p.108]. Companion article: [[bimbo-competitive-position]].

> [!warning] Internal inconsistency in RA25
> RA25 p.107 (section 8.1 intro) gives sales CAGRs "2019-2024 of 2.8% and 4.7%" and "2024-2029 of 5.2% and 4.4%" for bakery and snacks "respectively". Section 8.2 (p.107) and the tables (p.108) give bakery 2.8% (2019-24) and 4.7% (2024-29E); p.113 gives snacks 5.3% and 3.7%. The intro figures do not match the detailed ones. Use the detailed tables; treat the intro as `[unverified]`.

> [!warning] Superseded / restated basis across years
> Global Data's series is re-based every year. RA24 (2023 data): bakery US$641bn, CAGR 2018-23 2.9%, 2023-28E 4.3%; snacks US$194.4bn (RA24 p.105) [sourced]. RA23 (2022 data): bakery US$440bn, snacks US$326bn (RA23 p.135) [sourced]. The snack market size moved from US$194.4bn to US$106.9bn between RA24 and RA25 and the bakery size from US$440bn to US$639bn between RA23 and RA25, so definitions clearly changed. Do not build a growth series by chaining these figures. Latest (RA25) is kept in this article.

## 2. Fragmentation and who the "competitor" is

- ==Top-10 manufacturers hold 11.7% of the global bakery industry and none controls more than 4.0%== (Global Data 2024) [sourced, RA25 p.109]. Comparable RA24 figure: top-10 = 13.4% (2023) [sourced, RA24 p.107]; RA23: 8.4% (2022) [sourced, RA23 p.137]. The series is not monotonic, which points to basis changes rather than a real trend.
- Artisanal and private-label products were 44.3% and 9.6% of the global market in 2024; Bimbo, the largest branded player, was 3.7% (RA25 p.107) [sourced]. RA24 equivalents: 40.9%, 9.1%, 3.8% (2023) [sourced, RA24 p.105].
- Bimbo says the largest competitors of branded bakery makers in most markets are small family-owned local bakeries or retailers' own brands, because of short shelf life (RA25 p.109) [sourced].
- Historically **low barriers to entry** have kept concentration limited; RA25 says there are four significant global players but only Bimbo operates at true global scale (RA25 p.109) [sourced]. The detail on named players is in [[bimbo-competitive-position]].
- Europe: artisanal plus private label are 52.6% of the regional market; top-10 non-private-label manufacturers hold less than 10% and most have under 1% each (RA25 p.111) [sourced].
- Key competitive factors named by Bimbo: price and perceived product quality (RA25 p.109) [sourced]. The risk-factor section adds brand, service, loyalty, promotions, shelf access and responsiveness to consumer preferences (RA25 p.24) [sourced].

## 3. Channels

Bimbo's industry classification (Global Data) has four groups: modern trade (supermarkets, hypermarkets, convenience, discounters, e-commerce), specialist food and drink stores (bakeries, butchers and so on), traditional (independent and mom-and-pop stores, general merchandise, dollar and department stores, pharmacies), and wholesalers (cash-and-carry and clubs) (RA25 p.110) [sourced].

| Channel fact | Value | Tag / cite |
|---|---|---|
| Modern trade share of global bakery sales | 53.3% (RA24: 47.0%; RA23: 50.9%) | [sourced] RA25 p.110; RA24 p.108; RA23 p.138 |
| Modern trade, North America | 77.7% (RA24: 79.2%) | [sourced] RA25 p.110; RA24 p.108 |
| Specialist plus traditional stores, Latin America / Middle East and North Africa | about 59% / about 60% of sales | [sourced] RA25 p.110 |
| E-commerce growth in 2025, fast-moving consumer goods | +15.3% (NielsenIQ) | [sourced] RA25 p.110 |

- Pattern stated by Bimbo: emerging markets are dominated by traditional channels and local or artisanal bakeries; modern trade matters more in developed markets (RA25 p.110) [sourced].
- **Direct-store-delivery (DSD) as a channel advantage**: short shelf life and retailers' expectation of restocking several times a day mean players with limited distribution can only serve local markets; packaging and technology advances are lengthening shelf life and so delivery range (RA25 p.110) [sourced]. Detail in [[bimbo-competitive-position]] and [[bimbo-business-model]].
- **Customer concentration and retailer power** (Bimbo's risk factors): consolidation of supermarkets and growth of retail chains raise reliance on few customers and put pressure on prices and promotion funding (RA25 p.32) [sourced]. Alternative formats (online retailers, apps, subscriptions, convenience, membership stores) are growing; Bimbo warns they may create price deflation at the customer level (RA25 p.31) [sourced]. Largest customer is Wal-Mart, about 15% of FY2025 net sales; no other customer is above 10% (RA25 p.103) [sourced].
- Recent channel commentary from earnings releases: convenience channel grew double digits in Mexico in 4Q25 (4Q25 ER p.4); "convenience and traditional" cited as growing channels in Mexico in 3Q25, 4Q25 and 1Q26; "convenience" highlighted again in 2Q26 (2Q26 ER p.3) [sourced]. Details in [[bimbo-competitive-position]] section 4.

## 4. Private label

- RA25 treats private label as a structural competitor: "the competition against private-label products could have a negative impact" and consumers may switch if relative value or quality moves (RA25 p.24) [sourced]. Retailers have an incentive to favour private label because it can raise their sales and margins (RA25 p.31) [sourced]. Macro stress can raise the weight of price and grow private label (RA25 p.109) [sourced].
- Bimbo itself supplies private-label bread to retailers and also sells QSR product; in Canada the company is "also in private label and QSR" (RA25 p.83) [sourced]. At the same time, management has repeatedly cited a **strategic exit from some private-label businesses in the US and Canada** as a drag on North America volume, from 1Q24 through 3Q25 (see [[bimbo-competitive-position]] and [[bimbo-north-america-turnaround-evidence]]) [sourced].
- Private-label share data by country: Global Data's world total only (9.6%, 2024) is given; no US, Mexico or Canada private-label share is stated.

## 6. Consumer trends stated

- Health and wellness, affordability and trust in the products purchased are the three most relevant macro-trends (RA25 p.107) [sourced].
- Innova Market Insights (cited): digestive health, sourdough, higher fibre and whole grain, protein (a purchase factor that has driven trading up to higher-value brands), fusion of traditions, and "clean label" with natural ingredients and no artificial preservatives; recyclable and biodegradable packaging follows (RA25 p.108) [sourced].
- Snacks: a Q1 2024 global consumer survey says short, recognisable ingredient lists are increasingly desired; functional and fortified ingredients; transparency; affordability stays key; indulgence and novel textures and flavours drive younger consumers (RA25 p.113) [sourced].
- Bimbo's own response measures: 12.6% of FY2025 sales came from innovations; examples are Artesano sourdough in Mexico and Latin America, half loaves in Mexico and the US, higher-protein bagels (RA25 p.80) [sourced]. See [[bimbo-brands-and-portfolio]].
- Current demand backdrop in earnings releases: "continued weak consumption environment across the industry in the US" is the stock explanation for North America sales declines in 1Q24-3Q25 (see [[bimbo-competitive-position]]); 1Q26 release cites "encouraging signs of improving demand" (1Q26 ER p.1) [sourced].

## Key Takeaways

- ==Bimbo sizes its bakery industry at US$638.9bn (2024, Global Data) and salty snacks at US$106.9bn==, growing 2.8% (2019-24) and expected 4.7% (2024-29E) for bakery [sourced, RA25 p.107-108].
- Extreme fragmentation: top-10 players hold 11.7% and none exceeds 4.0%; artisanal (44.3%) plus private label (9.6%) are more than half of global sales [sourced, RA25 p.107, p.109].
- Channels differ sharply by region: modern trade is 77.7% in North America while Latin America and MENA rely about 59-60% on traditional and specialist outlets [sourced, RA25 p.110]; this is why Bimbo runs different distribution models by region.
- Regulation is mainly Mexican: front-of-pack labelling (third phase pushed to 1 Jan 2028), school-food limits (mandatory since 29 Mar 2025) and advertising permits; no sugar-tax-on-bread is described, and the IEPS rate is not stated [sourced, RA25 p.29-31, p.130].
- Global Data figures are re-based every year (snack size US$194.4bn in RA24 vs US$106.9bn in RA25); avoid chaining them into trends and flag them as such in the report.
- Country-level market sizes, private-label shares and channel splits are not in the filings and are open items for external research.
