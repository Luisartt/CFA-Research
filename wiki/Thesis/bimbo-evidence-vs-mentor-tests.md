## 8. Deleveraging: how exactly

**Identity.** Change in net debt = -FCF + dividends + buybacks + acquisitions + FX and other `[calc]`.

| Period | FCF source | Returns | M&A | FX / other | Net debt change | Ratio (ex-IFRS 16) |

**The levers, ranked by what the evidence supports.**
1. **Capex.** PP&E capex fell from MXN 34,754 (FY23, 8.7% of sales) to 22,530 (FY25, 5.3%) and 6,892 in 1H26 (3.4%); US$ guide 2.0bn (2023), 1.6bn, 1.2bn, 1.0-1.2bn (2026) `[sourced]`. Capex/D&A is 0.91x (FY25); steady-state ratios in the company's own impairment tests imply about 4.0% of sales `[calc]` `[assumption]`: the gap is about 2.6bn a year. 2H26 must spend 10.6-14.1bn to meet the guide `[calc]`. Mexico alone has a US$2bn programme for 2025-2028 (call 2025-07-24 p.5) `[sourced]`.
2. **EBITDA growth.** Guide +70-120bp of margin with sales ex-FX mid-single-digit and a 500-600bp FX drag; 1H26 EBITDA 29,149 (14,036 + 15,113) `[calc]`; the floor of 60,465 needs 2H26 -1.4% vs 2H25.
3. **Buyback.** Cut 72% in 2025 and 1H26 only 496; S&P expects moderated buybacks (PR 2025-03-20) `[sourced]`.
4. **FX.** A stronger peso lowers USD (42%) and EUR (12%) debt in MXN: -12.3bn in 2025 `[sourced]`; this reverses if the peso weakens (2024: +~20% depreciation, net debt +39bn).
5. **Disposals.** None planned; Ricolino was the only large one (2.66x sales), 0.4-0.5x effect.
6. **Working capital.** Company FCF uses +2.6bn in 1H26; the cash-flow statement lines sum to -582 (G6).

**M&A ambitions.** About US$480m a year of M&A = 8.4bn at 17.5 = +0.14x of leverage a year on 60.9bn of EBITDA `[calc]`. Management says leverage falls "even with zero M&A" (call 2026-02-25 p.15); the 2H26 arithmetic: if 2H26 FCF repeats the proxy of 8,986, returns are ~0.9bn and no M&A, Dec-26 net debt would be ~129bn and 2.3x on a ~55.9bn ex-IFRS 16 EBITDA `[calc]` `[assumption]` (62,117 less ~6,200 right-of-use depreciation), against management's "slight deleverage from 2.7x". Consensus (CIQ) has Dec-26 net debt at 146,911 on a basis 3,903 above the company's, which is above the 137,097 reported at Jun-26: consensus has not absorbed 1H26 (C4).

**Credit constraint.** S&P (negative outlook) appears to hinge on lease-adjusted leverage near 3x (3.03x Dec-25, 2.80x Jun-26 `[calc]`; S&P's definition is not in the files `[assumption]`); an M&A of US$1bn would add +0.29x and put it back above the line `[calc]` (17.5bn / 60.9bn).

**Verdict: supported for 2026 on mechanics (capex plus EBITDA plus a smaller buyback, with FX help), with the quality caveats above.** The claim "deleveraging through FCF" is only as good as capex staying near US$1.0-1.2bn and the FCF reconciliation. **What would change it:** 2H26 capex above US$0.8bn, distributions above FCF, or an acquisition above US$500m.

## Key Takeaways

- ==M&A is unsupported as a 12-month thesis: 3 of 12 M&A targets met, 8.0-9.3x paid against a 6.3x own multiple, leverage +0.9-1.6x per large deal, and the one slow pay-off (EAA margin to 10.8%) took ten years.==
- The European healthy-food trend, the new-CEO track record and AI have no numbers in the files: drop AI, reword the CEO claim to management continuity and a conservative guide, and replace the Europe story by EAA margin and sales facts.
- "Inelastic demand" fails on five price-then-volume episodes, eight negative NA quarters and flat real sales in Mexico; the defensible claim is that Mexico outgrows ANTAD and the industry.
- Commodity relief is lagged and mostly consumed elsewhere: 61% of the 2.39pp raw-material relief of FY23-FY25 was absorbed by labour, depreciation and other cost lines; 2027 hedges are at higher cost with no price commitment.
- Deleveraging in 2026 is mechanically supported (1H26 net debt -8.0bn, 2.5x) but depends on capex staying near US$1.0-1.2bn, a reconciled FCF (3.2bn working-capital gap) and no large deal (+0.29x per US$1bn).
- Volume x price has to be built as a residual by region; the data to replace it is a 52-week Circana/NielsenIQ series, not management disclosure.
- The FCFF and comps rest on bases that conflict across sources (EBITDA 64,706 vs 60,919, net debt, leases, MEPP); choose one basis per method and show the terminal-value share.
