## Growth and Margins

Net sales grew from MXN 338,792 million in FY2021 to MXN 426,952 million in FY2025, a 6.0% CAGR that FY2022 (+17.7%) inflates; from FY2022 to FY2025 the CAGR is only 2.3%, as North America shrank in pesos and, until 1Q26, in constant currency (Grupo Bimbo, 2026f; team model). The Adjusted EBITDA margin has stayed in a narrow band: 14.0% in FY2021, 13.4%, 13.7%, 13.6% and 13.9% in FY2025 ({fig:rev_margin}) (Grupo Bimbo, 2026f, p. 85; team model). Gross margin slipped from 53.5% to 52.3% over the same years, with a trough of 51.4% in FY2023, because raw materials (30.4% of sales) and labour grew faster than prices (Grupo Bimbo, 2026f, p. 82; team model). In FY2025 salaries rose 7.5% and depreciation 13.3% against sales of 4.6% (Grupo Bimbo, 2026f, p. 82).

## Returns

[[table: ratios]]

Return on equity fell from 13.3% in FY2023 to 9.1% in FY2025 because the net margin fell from 3.9% to 2.6%. Interest expense rose from MXN 10,006 million to MXN 14,464 million as net debt, including leases, went from MXN 130 billion in FY2023 to MXN 180 billion in FY2025 (Bimbo closed 16 acquisitions in 2023-2025 while capex peaked), and depreciation rose from MXN 18.9 billion to MXN 24.8 billion (Grupo Bimbo, 2026a, pp. 75-78; 2026f; team model). Return on invested capital was 7.2% in FY2025, below the 10.4% WACC ({fig:roic_wacc}), with MXN 135.5 billion of goodwill and intangibles from past deals in the capital base (team model). Our DCF assumes that new capital earns 14.6%; that is the number the team must defend.

## Cash Flow and Capital Intensity

Cash flow from operations rose 20% to a record MXN 47,753 million in FY2025 while capex (PP&E acquired) fell 23% to MXN 22,530 million. Free cash flow after lease payments reached MXN 18,989 million, a 4.4% margin against 1.1% in FY2024 (Grupo Bimbo, 2026f; team model). Dividends took MXN 4,316 million, and the FY2026 dividend of MXN 1.075 per share (a 1.8% yield) was paid in May 2026 (Grupo Bimbo, 2026a). Management lowered 2026 capex guidance to US$1.0-1.2 billion, about MXN 17.5-21.0 billion, because projects are running behind schedule (2Q26 earnings call; team model).

## Balance Sheet

Net debt including leases was MXN 179,918 million at FY2025, 3.0x Adjusted EBITDA. On last-twelve-month data to June 2026 it is 2.8x on our peer basis against a peer median of 3.0x, and 2.5x on the company's definition ({fig:leverage}) (Grupo Bimbo, 2026d, p. 6; S&P Capital IQ, 2026; team model). Debt is long and diversified: average maturity 9.5 years, average cost 6.5%, 96% long term, and a currency mix of 42% dollars, 42% pesos, 12% euros, 3% Canadian dollars and 1% sterling after swaps (Grupo Bimbo, 2026d, p. 6). Liquidity includes an undrawn US$2,350 million revolver (Grupo Bimbo, 2026a, p. 82). Interest cover (EBIT over interest) fell from 4.2x in FY2021 to 2.4x in FY2025, which is thin; S&P rates Bimbo BBB+ with a negative outlook since March 2025 (team model; LSEG, 2026).

## Forecast and Why It Differs From History

The consolidated forecast has three features that differ from the last four years. First, revenue growth recovers from -0.5% in FY2026E (peso translation) to 4.4-4.8% a year (a 4.6% CAGR over FY2026E-FY2030E), above the 4.2% a year expected for Bimbo's markets, because North America stops shrinking and Mexico grows 5.0-5.5% (team model). Second, the EBITDA margin rises 70 bp in FY2026E, dips 10 bp in FY2027E for wheat and reaches 15.0% in FY2030E, above the 13.4-14.0% band of FY2021-FY2025. Third, capex stays near 5% of sales instead of the 7-9% of FY2022-FY2024, which lifts FCFF to MXN 23-27 billion a year, 35-38% of EBITDA (team model). Each of the three is a thesis in the Investment Summary and a source of risk.
