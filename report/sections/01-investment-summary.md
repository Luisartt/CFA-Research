# Investment Summary

[[figs: thesis_map, na_margin, capex_cfo, cost_lines, growth_gap]]

**Theses validated by the team.** Rating: HOLD (provisional). Target price: to be set by the team.

We frame the case as three theses, each stated as a problem and its cause. One supports upside and two support downside. Their sizes are changes in our DCF value of MXN 46.42 per share, with the 12-month rating bands described under Valuation ({fig:thesis_map}) (team model).

### Thesis #1 (upside): North America's margin problem is being corrected — due to cost restructuring and better price/mix, with capex normalised and Mexico funding the turnaround

- **Evidence.** North America's Adjusted EBITDA margin rose from 5.9% in 4Q24 to 10.0% in 2Q26, expanding year on year in each of the last four quarters (+90, +330, +120 and +100 bp). Net sales excluding currency turned positive (+0.7% and +1.3%) after eight negative quarters, and management reports share gains in all US categories in 2Q26, the first time since 2020 (Grupo Bimbo, 2026b, pp. 4-7; 2026c, pp. 3, 5; 2026d, pp. 3-5) ({fig:na_margin}).
- **Cause.** Management credits the transformation programme (11 bakery closures in 2024 and "record productivity benefits" from 3Q25), lower raw materials, better price/mix and lower restructuring spend. The region's gross margin rose 330 bp, from 52.0% to 55.3% between 4Q24 and 2Q26 (Grupo Bimbo, 2026a, pp. 76-77; 2026b, p. 7; team model).
- **Cash.** Capex fell from MXN 34.8 billion in FY2023 to MXN 22.5 billion in FY2025 while cash flow from operations rose 20% to a record MXN 47.8 billion. Mexico, with 55% of EBITDA at a 20.4% margin, funds the turnaround ({fig:capex_cfo}) (Grupo Bimbo, 2026f, pp. 85-86).
- **Value.** North America earns 9.6% over the last twelve months against 11.1% for Flowers Foods on a lease-comparable basis. Closing that 150 bp gap adds MXN 4.22 per share; each 100 bp is worth MXN 2.81 (S&P Capital IQ, 2026; team model).
- **Why it matters for the rating.** A lasting recovery is what would support a premium to the core bakers' multiple. Without it, the 6.95x that the price requires is hard to defend (team model).
- **What would change our view.** A year-on-year fall in North America's margin in 3Q26 (3Q25: 10.4%) or capex back above 6% of sales (Grupo Bimbo, 2025, quarterly releases; team estimate).

### Thesis #2 (downside): 2027 margins are at risk — due to wheat, with futures up 35% year on year and hedges placed at 2025 prices rolling off

- **Exposure.** Raw materials are 30.4% of sales and 63.8% of cost of sales, and wheat flour is the principal input, priced in dollars (Grupo Bimbo, 2026a, p. 97; 2026f, p. 82). Gross margin follows wheat with about a one-year lag (correlation -0.74, 2017-2025) (team model).
- **The shock.** CBOT December 2026 wheat settled at US$6.865 a bushel, 34.9% above a year ago, and December 2027 is 5.4% higher still (Trading Economics, 2026; IndexBox, 2026). Hedges placed at 2025 prices roll off into 2027, so we expect 1H27 to be the cost peak (team estimate). Bimbo does not disclose its 2027 hedge coverage.
- **Leakage.** FY2025 shows costs outrunning sales: salaries +7.5%, depreciation +13.3% and other costs +12.3% against sales +4.6%, and gross margin fell 30 bp to 52.3% (Grupo Bimbo, 2026f, p. 82) ({fig:cost_lines}). Consensus expects 52.3% in FY2026 and 52.0% in FY2027, with no structural step down (S&P Capital IQ, 2026).
- **Value.** The question is persistence. A one-year, 100 bp hit in 2027 costs only MXN 0.66 per share. Losing 50 bp for good from 2027 costs MXN 3.55, and 100 bp costs MXN 7.09 (team model).
- **Against.** The 2Q26 gross margin was 52.6%, only 20 bp below a year earlier, and management raised its 2026 margin guidance to +70 to +120 bp (Grupo Bimbo, 2026d; 2Q26 earnings call).
- **What would change our view.** A gross margin above 52.5% in 3Q26 and 4Q26, together with 2027 guidance that absorbs wheat (team estimate).

### Thesis #3 (downside): Organic growth falls short of the model — due to record private label, a shrinking US bread category and a Mexico that slowed to +2.4% in 2025

- **What the forecast needs.** Our regional build assumes that North America grows 1.5% a year in dollars while the US bread category loses 2-4% of its units and price/mix adds 1-2%, so Bimbo must outgrow its category by about 3 points. Mexico grows 5.0-5.5% a year against a 3.5% market, which means about 0.3 points of share gain a year from an 18.2% base ({fig:growth_gap}) (team estimate; Grupo Bimbo, 2026a, p. 111).
- **Pressure.** Private label is about 35% of US fresh bread units and reached a record 23.8% of US grocery unit sales in 1H26. Flowers Foods' total volume fell 5.8% in 2Q26 (Flowers Foods, 2026; Bakery & Snacks, 2026; Investing.com, 2026).
- **Mexico slowed.** Net sales grew 4.0% in 2024 and 2.4% in 2025 (Grupo Bimbo, 2026a, p. 133).
- **Value.** One point less growth a year in North America costs MXN 1.12 per share and two points less in Mexico cost MXN 2.17; together they cost MXN 3.29 (team model).
- **Against.** The first half of 2026 contradicts part of this thesis: Mexico grew 4.5% and 4.7% and North America turned positive excluding currency (Grupo Bimbo, 2026c, p. 3; 2026d, p. 3). The forecast also grows group revenue 4.4-4.8% a year in FY2027E-FY2030E (a 4.6% CAGR for FY2026E-FY2030E), above the 4.2% a year expected for Bimbo's markets (team model).
- **What would change our view.** Mexico above +5% for two quarters in a row, or North America above +2% excluding currency (team estimate).

## Putting the Theses Together

The three theses together move the DCF value by -MXN 2.65, from MXN 46.42 to MXN 43.77 at the base multiple: the buy thesis offsets about 60% of the two sell theses (team model). The rating still depends mostly on the exit multiple. The price requires 6.95x; our base uses 5.92x; the core bakers trade at 5.53x. Whether the three theses justify a premium to the core bakers is the team's decision.

## Catalysts

- **3Q26 results, late October 2026.** The last four years' releases fell on 26-29 October; the 2026 date is not yet announced (Grupo Bimbo, 2025, quarterly releases).
- **FY2026 results and 2027 guidance, late February 2027.** Past releases fell on 19-27 February. This is where management shows how it absorbs wheat in 2027 (Grupo Bimbo, 2025, quarterly releases).
- **Wheat entering the 2027 hedge book, 1H27** (team estimate), and Mexico's cap-and-trade operating rules, which management says will affect profits (Grupo Bimbo, 2026a).
- **Shareholders' meeting by end-April 2027**, with the FY2026 dividend (Grupo Bimbo, 2026a, p. 62).
