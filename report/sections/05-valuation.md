# Valuation

[[figs: ev_bridge, multiple_curve, tornado]]

## Approach

We value Bimbo with a five-year DCF of free cash flow to the firm (FCFF) for FY2026E-FY2030E, discounted to 16 October 2026. The exit-multiple terminal value is the primary method and a Gordon-growth terminal value is the cross-check. Trading comparables and a reverse DCF test the result. All values are in pesos per share (team model). The terminal value is 76% of enterprise value, which is why the multiple matters so much.

## Forecast and Free Cash Flow

Revenue falls 0.5% in FY2026E because the peso translates North America lower (-7.4% in pesos), then grows 4.4-4.8% a year (a 4.6% CAGR for FY2026E-FY2030E). We set growth year by year and compound it; we use no CAGR as an input. Each year's rate is the midpoint between our regional build and consensus revenue (FY2026-FY2028, then faded) (S&P Capital IQ, 2026; team model). The Adjusted EBITDA margin rises from 14.6% to 15.0%, against about 14.7% in consensus. The 14.6% in FY2026E is +70 bp on FY2025, the low end of management's +70 to +120 bp guidance (2Q26 earnings call; team model). Capex is 4.9-5.1% of sales, close to consensus (about 5.0%) and above the 3.7% peer median. Tax is 32% of EBIT; the statutory rate is 30% and the effective rate has been about 34% (S&P Capital IQ, 2026; team model).

## Cost of Capital

We value in pesos. The M-Bono yield already contains Mexico's default risk, so we remove the 1.75% default spread to reach a default-free peso rate of 7.51% and add a country risk premium (CRP) weighted by where Bimbo earns its EBITDA: North America 0.17% (28.8% of EBITDA), Mexico 2.72% (55.4%), Europe, Asia and Africa 2.63% (9.8%) and Latin America 3.94% (6.0%), which gives 2.05% (Damodaran, 2026; Grupo Bimbo, 2026e, p. 17). The unlevered beta of 0.545 is in line with Damodaran's global food-processing betas (0.53 to 0.56) and with Bimbo's own five-year regression on the IPC (0.54). Relevered at a market debt-to-equity ratio of 74.3% (leases included) it is 0.828, giving a cost of equity of 13.04% and a WACC of 10.38% (Damodaran, 2026; S&P Capital IQ, 2026; team model).

## Terminal Value and Cross-checks

The terminal value is worth MXN 289.2 billion today, 76% of the MXN 378.8 billion enterprise value ({fig:ev_bridge}) (team model). The base exit multiple is 5.92x FY2030E EBITDA, close to the core bakers' median (5.53x for Flowers Foods and Gruma, 5.83x with Yamazaki and Aryzta) and below Bimbo's own 6.94x today (S&P Capital IQ, 2026; team model). It implies perpetual growth of 4.2%, below the 5.0% nominal GDP growth we assume for Mexico in the long run (IMF, 2026; team estimate). As a cross-check, a Gordon-growth terminal value with g of 4.125% and a return on new capital (RONIC) of 14.6% gives MXN 30.76. Letting FCFF grow at g without a reinvestment link gives MXN 41.65, which implies a RONIC of 27.7%. FY2025 ROIC was 7.2% against a 10.4% WACC (team model). The Gordon values therefore warn that the market multiple capitalises more growth than current returns on capital support.

## Relative Valuation

We use EV/EBITDA as the main multiple because it ignores capital structure, and we put every peer on an IFRS 16-equivalent basis (leases in debt, EBITDA before lease cost) so that Bimbo's IFRS accounts compare with US GAAP peers (S&P Capital IQ, 2026; team model). Four core bakers (Flowers Foods, Gruma, Yamazaki and Aryzta) set the floor and five consumer-staples peers (Smucker, Campbell's, Mondelez, Arca Continental and PepsiCo) the ceiling. Yamazaki, Aryzta and Arca Continental are left out of the medians because their lease or minority-interest data are not comparable. The core median is 5.53x and the eligible median 8.27x, against 6.94x for Bimbo. On last-twelve-month P/E Bimbo trades at 18.5x against 16.4x for the core bakers and 16.2x for all eligible peers (S&P Capital IQ, 2026; team model). Bimbo therefore sits between the pure bakers and global consumer staples. Comparables give a range, not a point: the multiple that fits Bimbo's mix is the team's judgement.

The DCF with the exit multiple gives MXN 46.42, 21% below the price. Trading comparables span MXN 36-75 depending on whether the core bakers (5.5x) or all eligible peers (8.3x) are the reference, and analysts' targets (median MXN 69.50) sit above the price (S&P Capital IQ, 2026; team model).

## Reverse DCF and Rating Bands

To justify MXN 58.60, the exit multiple must be 6.95x, or the WACC 6.96%, or revenue growth 3.1 points higher every year (team model). The first is where the stock trades today (6.94x), so the market is pricing no multiple compression.

Our rating convention uses a 12-month view. The 12-month value is today's value times (1 + cost of equity) less the next dividend (MXN 1.05). The expected return is the 12-month value over the price, minus one, plus the dividend yield. BUY is +15% or more, SELL is -10% or worse and HOLD is in between. At 5.92x the expected return is -10.5%, on the SELL edge. A clean HOLD (-5% to +10%) needs 6.16x to 6.82x, and BUY needs 7.04x or more ({fig:multiple_curve}) (team model).

## Sensitivity and Scenarios

Value moves most with the exit multiple (each 0.5x is worth MXN 5.90), then the margin (50 bp is worth MXN 3.72), capex (50 bp of sales, MXN 2.05), revenue growth (50 bp, MXN 1.84) and the WACC (50 bp, MXN 1.62) ({fig:tornado}). The WACC and multiple grid in the appendix runs from MXN 32.8 (11.4%, 5.0x) to MXN 63.1 (9.4%, 7.0x) (team model). Our scenarios are MXN 35.45 for the downside (wheat takes 50 bp from 2027, growth falls short and the multiple is the core bakers' 5.53x), MXN 46.42 for the base and MXN 61.70 for the upside (North America closes the gap to Flowers Foods at a 6.82x multiple) (team model).

We have not set a target price. The team will choose the exit multiple on evidence (the core bakers' multiple, Bimbo's mix of Mexico and North America, and the governance discount), and the target price and rating follow from the table above.
