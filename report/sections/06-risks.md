# Risks

[[figs: risk_matrix, ownership]]

We score 14 risks for probability and impact from 1 to 5 (team estimate; {fig:risk_matrix}) and size impact as the change in DCF value per share against the base of MXN 46.42 (team model). Six carry value and are discussed here; the full table is in the appendix.

## 1. Wheat and climate input costs (R1; probability 3, impact 4; Thesis 2)

Wheat, the main input, is up 35% year on year and is priced in dollars. Losing 50 bp of margin from 2027 costs MXN 3.55 per share and 100 bp costs MXN 7.09 (team model). Mitigation is partial: Bimbo hedges wheat and natural gas with cash-flow hedges (a book of about MXN 315 million at end-2025, 64% of it wheat), raises prices and cuts costs, but the hedges cover about a year, so they delay the cost rather than remove it (Grupo Bimbo, 2026f). Consensus gross margin (52.3%, 52.0% and 52.05% for FY2026-28) shows no structural step down, and our model has a 10 bp dip in FY2027, so little of this risk is priced in (S&P Capital IQ, 2026; team model).

## 2. The North America turnaround stalls (R2; probability 3, impact 4; Thesis 1)

The recovery is margin-led, not volume-led. Net sales excluding currency have grown only 0.7% and 1.3% in two quarters, and the company gives no volume, price or mix split for the region (Grupo Bimbo, 2026d, p. 3). If the margin fell back to FY2024's 8.4% from 9.6% over the last twelve months, we would lose about MXN 3.4 per share, since each 100 bp is worth MXN 2.81 (team model). Management points to record productivity benefits, 11 bakery closures and a 2026 margin guide of +70 to +120 bp (Grupo Bimbo, 2026a, pp. 76-77; 2Q26 earnings call). Our model assumes a gradual margin gain rather than a North America path, so the recovery is partly priced in.

## 3. Mexican labour and health policy (R4; probability 3, impact 4; Thesis 3)

Mexico is 32% of sales and 55% of EBITDA. The working week falls to 46 hours in 2027 and 40 hours by 2030, NOM-051 reaches its third phase in 2028 and the IEPS excise tax could rise above 8% (a tail risk). A 100 to 150 bp fall in the Mexican margin costs MXN 2.79 to 4.19 per share (Grupo Bimbo, 2026a, pp. 23-31, 48; team model). Mitigation is reformulation (98-99% of the daily portfolio has no artificial colours or flavours), pricing and route productivity. The 8% IEPS is in our base; labour-hour cuts are not modelled.

## 4. Capital allocation breaks the debt hold (R3; probability 3, impact 3)

Net debt is 3.0x Adjusted EBITDA at FY2025 and S&P has a negative outlook on the BBB+ rating. A deal at 8-9x EBITDA adds about 0.29x of leverage per US$1 billion (team model). Management cut 2026 capex guidance and reduced net debt from MXN 145.1 billion at December 2025 to MXN 137.1 billion at June 2026 (Grupo Bimbo, 2026d, p. 6). Our model has no acquisitions.

## 5. The exit multiple compresses (R5 and R9; probability 3 and 4, impact 3 and 2)

The price needs 6.95x while the core bakers trade at 5.53x; at that multiple the DCF is worth MXN 41.80, MXN 4.62 below the base (team model). Controlled-company governance is a reason for a discount to consumer peers: six vehicles hold 72.03% ({fig:ownership}), 7 of 18 directors are independent, and the board must approve any transfer that takes a holder to 3% (Grupo Bimbo, 2026a, pp. 181-182; 2026g, art. 17). The market already pays a premium to the bakers, so we add no WACC premium; each 0.5x on the exit multiple is worth MXN 5.90 per share (team model).

## 6. The peso depreciates less than assumed (R6; probability 3, impact 3)

North America's 3.0% growth in pesos includes about 1.5 points of peso depreciation, which assumes inflation in the United States above Mexico's (team estimate). Each point of lower North America growth in pesos costs MXN 1.12 per share (team model). About 42% of debt is in dollars, which hedges part of the exposure (Grupo Bimbo, 2026d, p. 6).

## Other Risks and the Downside Case

The remaining eight risks have an impact of 2 or less: USMCA and tariffs (R7), GLP-1 drugs and health habits (R8), Canada Bread antitrust class actions without a provision (R10), US multi-employer pension plans (R11), water stress and the new packaging law (R12), cyber attack (R13) and Argentina, Venezuela and Russia (R14) (Grupo Bimbo, 2026a, pp. 22-37; team estimate). A downside case that combines the wheat and growth theses (-50 bp from 2027; -1 point in North America and -2 points in Mexico) gives MXN 39.72. Adding the core bakers' 5.53x multiple gives MXN 35.45, an expected return of -31.6% (team model).
