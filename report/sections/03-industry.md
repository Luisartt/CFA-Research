# Industry Overview

[[figs: market_growth, peers, five_forces]]

## Market Structure and Growth

Bimbo competes in branded, packaged bakery delivered direct to store, country by country. That is narrower than the global bakery market, where artisanal bakeries hold 44.3% and private label 9.6% (Grupo Bimbo, 2026a, p. 107). Global bakery retail value was US$638.9 billion in 2024 and grew at a 2.8% CAGR in 2019-24. Global Data expects a 4.7% CAGR in 2024-29E, with 4.3% in North America (US$139.1 billion), 5.5% in Latin America (US$121.8 billion) and 3.5% in Mexico ({fig:market_growth}) (Grupo Bimbo, 2026a, pp. 107-111). Weighting the regional CAGRs by Bimbo's sales mix gives about 4.2% a year in value (team model). That growth is price and mix, not volume. In developed markets volume is flat or falling: Flowers Foods' total volume fell 5.8% in 2Q26 (Investing.com, 2026). We classify the industry as mature.

## Competitive Landscape

Bimbo is number one globally with a 3.7% share, ahead of Mondelez (3.0%) and Gruma (1.1%) (Grupo Bimbo, 2026a, p. 107). Local shares matter more. In US fresh bread, buns and rolls, Bimbo holds about 27%, private label 26%, Flowers Foods 16% and Pepperidge Farm 5% (Flowers Foods, 2026, citing Circana). In Mexico Bimbo holds 18.2% of packaged bread, 28.2% of sweet baked goods and 18.4% of salty snacks, and in Canada 13.3% (Grupo Bimbo, 2026a, pp. 111, 113).

On valuation, Bimbo trades at 6.9x last-twelve-month EV/EBITDA against 5.5x for the core bakers (Flowers Foods and Gruma) and 8.3x for all eligible peers ({fig:peers}). Over five years its share price is -3.8% in pesos, while Flowers Foods is -75.3% and Campbell's -50.3% in dollars (S&P Capital IQ, 2026; team model).

## Porter's Five Forces

Bimbo is two industries in one ({fig:five_forces}) (team estimate). In Mexico and Latin America the buyer is fragmented and Bimbo owns the route network, which supports an EBITDA margin near 20% in Mexico (20.4% in FY2025) (Grupo Bimbo, 2026f, p. 85). In the United States, Canada and Europe the buyer is concentrated, private label is strong and volume shrinks. Scale protects volume there, but not margin.

- **Rivalry** is high in the United States. Flowers Foods has cut its FY2026 guidance again, and Bimbo claims share gains in every US category in 2Q26 in a category that shrinks in volume (S&P Capital IQ, 2026; Grupo Bimbo, 2026d, p. 3). In Mexico rivalry is medium, against fragmented artisanal baking.
- **New entrants** are a low threat at scale and a high one locally. Baking is cheap; delivering a 14-day product to stores is the barrier. The real entrants are private label, hard discounters and in-store bakeries (Grupo Bimbo, 2026a, pp. 100, 110).
- **Supplier power** is medium. Raw materials are 30.4% of sales and 63.8% of cost of sales, and wheat, sugar and oils are dollar commodities with exogenous prices. Gross margin follows wheat with about a one-year lag (correlation -0.74, 2017-2025) (Grupo Bimbo, 2026f, p. 82; team model).
- **Buyer power** is high in developed markets, where the modern channel is 77.7% of North America and Walmart is about 15% of group sales, and low in emerging markets, where traditional stores are about 59-60% (Grupo Bimbo, 2026a, pp. 103, 110).
- **Substitutes** are medium-high. Private label is about 35% of US fresh bread units and reached a record 23.8% of US grocery unit sales in 1H26 (Flowers Foods, 2026; Bakery & Snacks, 2026). GLP-1 drugs may take 0.5-1.5 points a year from bread and sweet-goods volume (team estimate).

## Macro and Regulatory Drivers

About 44% of sales come from developed markets, mainly the United States, Canada and Europe, and 42% of debt is in dollars after swaps, so the peso drives reported growth (Grupo Bimbo, 2026a, p. 78; 2026d, p. 6). In Mexico, NOM-051 front-of-pack labelling reaches its third phase on 1 January 2028, the IEPS excise tax is 8% on foods of 275 kcal per 100 g or more, and the working week falls to 46 hours in 2027 and 40 hours by 2030 (Grupo Bimbo, 2026a, pp. 23-31, 48). In the United States, shoppers are trading down: store brands reached a record 23.8% unit share in 1H26 (Bakery & Snacks, 2026).

## Positioning and Moat

The delivery network is a wide moat in Mexico and Latin America, and the numbers show it: Mexico earns a 20.4% EBITDA margin against 9.0% in North America (Grupo Bimbo, 2026f, p. 85). It is not yet visible in the United States, where North America earns 9.6% over the last twelve months against 11.1% for Flowers Foods on a lease-comparable basis (S&P Capital IQ, 2026; team model). Bimbo has the highest gross margin of the bakers (52.6% against 48.6% for Flowers Foods) but spends the advantage on distribution, and it is more capital intensive than its peers (capex 5.2% of sales against a 3.7% median) (S&P Capital IQ, 2026; team model). The moat is eroded slowly by longer-shelf-life packaging, private label, Mexican labour costs and health regulation on the highest-margin categories (team estimate).
