# Appendix

## A1. Key Financial Modelling Assumptions

The valuation starts from the FY2025 audited IFRS accounts (Grupo Bimbo, 2026f). The DCF values free cash flow to the firm (FCFF) for FY2026E-FY2030E. For each year we set revenue growth, the Adjusted EBITDA margin, D&A (5.9% of sales), capex excluding new leases (4.9-5.1% of sales), new leases (1.4% of sales plus 8.1% of each year's revenue increase), working-capital changes (about 0.1% of sales) and tax on EBIT (32%) (team model).

Group revenue growth is set year by year and compounded: -0.5% in FY2026E, then 4.5%, 4.8%, 4.6% and 4.4% in FY2027E-FY2030E (a 4.6% CAGR for FY2026E-FY2030E and 3.5% for FY2025-FY2030E). We use no CAGR as an input; CAGRs appear only for history and market data. Each year's rate is the midpoint between our regional build and consensus revenue (FY2026-FY2028, then faded) (S&P Capital IQ, 2026; team model). The regional build assumes that North America drops 7.4% in pesos in FY2026E (the first half was -11.3% in pesos and +1.0% excluding currency, and we assume -3.5% in the second half) and then grows 3.0% a year in pesos, about 1.5% in dollars plus about 1.5 points of peso depreciation, and that Mexico grows 5.0-5.5% a year. The Adjusted EBITDA margin is 14.6% in FY2026E and 15.0% in FY2030E (team estimate; S&P Capital IQ, 2026). Regional sensitivities in the Investment Summary and the Risks section move group revenue or margin by the region's share of sales: a one-point change in North America's growth moves group growth by about 0.4 points (team model).

The equity bridge uses the 30 June 2026 balance sheet: financial debt of MXN 153,660 million and lease liabilities of MXN 33,361 million (MXN 187,021 million in total), cash of MXN 16,563 million, non-controlling interests at market value (MXN 18,069 million, capitalising their income of MXN 1,814 million at the cost of equity less 3% growth) and net associates of MXN 1,959 million. Pensions, provisions and derivatives (MXN 18,135 million) are deducted only in the Gordon method, because peer enterprise values exclude them (Grupo Bimbo, 2026d, p. 8; team model). FY2026E free cash flow counts only the 184 days after 30 June, flows are discounted at mid-period, and the equity value is rolled 108 days to 16 October at the cost of equity. There are 4,294.9 million shares outstanding (S&P Capital IQ, 2026).

## A6. Relative Valuation and Competitor Profiles

We compare Bimbo with nine listed companies on one basis: last-twelve-month data to June 2026, enterprise value and EBITDA on an IFRS 16-equivalent basis, and prices of 16 October 2026 (S&P Capital IQ, 2026; team model). Yamazaki, Aryzta and Arca Continental are excluded from the medians because their lease or minority-interest data could not be verified on the same basis.

[[table: comps]]

- **Flowers Foods (NYSE: FLO):** the US pure-play baker with direct-store delivery and the mirror of Bimbo's North America. It reports under US GAAP, has no Mexican business and is in distress, with a cut guidance and dividend and an S&P rating of BB+ with a negative outlook (S&P Capital IQ, 2026).
- **Gruma (BMV: GRUMA B):** a Mexican issuer under IFRS with Mexico and US operations in tortilla and flour. Its core is corn, not bread, and its gross margin is 38.8% against Bimbo's 52.6% (S&P Capital IQ, 2026).
- **Yamazaki Baking (TSE: 2212):** the largest pure-play baker outside the Americas, under J-GAAP, with operating leases off balance sheet.
- **Aryzta (SWX: ARYN):** a European bakery, quick-service and frozen business under IFRS.
- **J.M. Smucker (NYSE: SJM) and Campbell's (NASDAQ: CPB):** Smucker owns Hostess, which competes with Bimbo in US sweet baked goods; Campbell's owns Pepperidge Farm in bread and snacks.
- **Mondelez (NASDAQ: MDLZ), PepsiCo (NASDAQ: PEP) and Arca Continental (BMV: AC):** global and Mexican consumer staples with cookies and snacks (Mondelez, PepsiCo's Gamesa and Sabritas) or their own routes (Arca); they carry a quality premium and are not operating peers.
