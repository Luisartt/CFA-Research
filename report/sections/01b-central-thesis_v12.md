# Central thesis v12 — three pillars, figures from TP12m_v2, management statements from the 3Q26 call

Sources: TP12m_v2 for every figure (`analisis-dcf-2026-10-26/precio-objetivo/BIMBOA_statements_2021-2030_v2_pivot_waccV2_TP12m_v2.xlsx`, not modified; no new scenarios) and the 3Q26 earnings call for management statements.
Pillars: (1) growth without acquisitions and currency, (2) the margin management must prove, (3) raw-material costs.

---

**Bimbo is worth what it costs today — due to growth of about 3% once acquisitions and currency are stripped out and a new management that must still prove the margin it inherited, offset by raw-material costs that are hedged into the first half of 2027 and, in our model, stay inside the historical range after it.**

The recovery is visible in the numbers. In 3Q26 the adjusted EBITDA margin reached 15.7%, 100 bp above 3Q25, and 14.7% for 9M26, 105 bp above 9M25 (team model). Management raised its 2026 margin guide for the third time this year, to 85-120 bp, and net debt fell to 2.4x EBITDA (Grupo Bimbo, 3Q26 earnings call); our FY2026E margin of 14.8% takes the midpoint of that guide, 103 bp, less an 11.5 bp one-off (team model). But the base is softer than the headline. In 9M26 reported sales fell 2.2% in pesos; excluding currency they grew 4.4%, of which about 1.1 points came from acquisitions, leaving 3.4% organic (team model). In FY2027E the peso turns: 1.46 of the 4.75 points of growth are currency, and the business grows 3.3%, slowing to 2.6% by FY2030E (team model).

The margin is the test. Of the 120 bp we model from FY2025 to FY2027E, 114 come from two lines that fall almost to zero: other expenses, from 1.15% to 0.14% of sales, and integration costs, from MXN 541 million to zero; the rest of the business adds 6 bp (team model). Management inherited a margin that was already rising, from 13.6% in FY2024 to 13.9% in FY2025, and it must now deliver the two lines on which the forecast rests (team model). One of them is already under strain: the CEO said that integrating Wickbold "has taken us more time than we anticipated" and expects "some more pressure" (Grupo Bimbo, 3Q26 earnings call).

Costs are what keep this from being a sell. The main raw materials are fully hedged for 4Q26 and almost 100% for the first half of 2027, but Bimbo never hedges 15 months ahead, so the second half is open; the CFO called 2027 "a challenge" for cost of sales, while stating that he is not guiding to lower margins (Grupo Bimbo, 3Q26 earnings call). In our model wheat reaches the income statement with a one-year lag: raw materials fall from 30.4% to 29.9% of sales in FY2026E and rise to 30.6% in FY2027E, when the gross margin drops 58 bp to 52.2% (team model). From then on the gross margin holds at 52.2%, inside the FY2021-FY2025 range of 51.4% to 53.5%, and the adjusted EBITDA margin stays at 15.1% to FY2030E (team model). That makes 2027 the year of the test: Wickbold, acquired in October 2025, stops adding inorganic growth from 4Q26, the raw-material hit lands, and the two lines behind 114 bp of margin must reach almost zero.

The valuation says the same. The DCF values the share at MXN 60.18, 2.7% above the MXN 58.60 close of 16 October 2026, and the 12-month target is MXN 66.13 (team model). Moving the WACC 0.5 pp either way gives expected total returns of -0.7% to +33.5%, a range that contains the 12.26% cost of equity; we would move to BUY below MXN 51.84 and to SELL above MXN 69.68 (team model). We rate Bimbo HOLD, not because the recovery is in doubt, but because the price sits within 3% of what our model says the business is worth, and only 2027 can show whether it deserves more.
