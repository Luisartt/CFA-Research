## 1. Market definition, size and life cycle

**Where Bimbo competes** (product x geography x customer):
- **Product:** packaged bread, buns, sweet baked goods, cookies, packaged tortillas, bagels and muffins, salty snacks (Barcel, Takis), bread for quick-service restaurants. **Out:** artisanal and in-store bakery bread (a substitute, not the served market), corn tortilla and masa (Gruma's core), confectionery (Ricolino, sold in 2022).
- **Geography:** 39 countries; FY2025 sales: North America 44.6%, Mexico 32.4%, Europe/Asia/Africa 12.7%, Latin America 10.3% `[sourced]` 4Q25 release p.4.
- **Customer:** modern retail (Walmart ~15% of sales `[sourced]` RA25 p.103), traditional stores and convenience (OXXO), quick-service restaurants. Direct-to-consumer is immaterial.

**Size and growth** (Global Data via RA25; retail value incl. taxes and inflation):

|---|---|---|---|---|
| Global bakery | US$638.9bn | 2.8% | 4.7% | `[sourced]` RA25 p.107-108 |
| North America | US$139.1bn | 4.1% | 4.3% | `[sourced]` RA25 p.108 |
| Latin America | US$121.8bn | 4.0% | 5.5% | `[sourced]` RA25 p.108 |

- Growth weighted by Bimbo's regional mix: about **4.2% a year in value** 2024-29E `[calc]`. It is value (price and taxes), not volume.
- **Life cycle: mature** `[assumption]`. Value grows 3-5% mostly through price and mix; volume is flat or falling in developed markets (Flowers' bread volume -5.8% in 2Q26 `[sourced]`). Volume growth comes mainly from Latin America, Eastern Europe and Asia `[assumption]`: their value CAGRs (5.0-6.6% 2024-29E) run above developed regions (3.4-4.3%) `[sourced]` RA25 p.108, but Global Data does not split volume.
- **Against the macro cycle:** with long-run Mexican inflation of about 3% and real growth of about 2% (IMF WEO Oct-2026 `[sourced]`), nominal GDP grows about 5%, so the category grows a little slower than the economy `[calc]`.

## 2. Five forces

| Force | Intensity | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Rivalry | **High in the US; medium in Mexico** | Flowers cut its FY2026 guide again; its stock is -75% over five years and Campbell's -50% (USD) `[sourced]`/`[calc]` peer workbook v3. Bimbo claims share gains in every US category in 2Q26 `[guidance]`, in a category that shrinks in volume. In Mexico Bimbo leads packaged bread with 18.2% against fragmented artisanal competition `[sourced]` RA25 p.111 |
| Threat of entrants | **Low at scale, high locally** | Baking is cheap; the barrier is delivering fresh product with a ~14-day shelf life through direct-store delivery (RA25 p.100, p.110) `[sourced]`. Real entry comes from private label, hard discounters and in-store bakeries |
| Supplier power | **Medium** | Raw materials are ~30% of sales and ~64% of cost of sales (EEFF25 p.82) `[sourced]`; wheat, sugar and oils are USD commodities: suppliers have no pricing power of their own but prices are exogenous. Gross margin follows wheat with about a one-year lag (r = -0.74, 2017-2025) `[calc]` |
| Buyer power | **High in developed markets; low in emerging ones** | Walmart ~15%, no other customer >10%; modern channel 77.7% in North America vs ~59-60% traditional in Latin America and MENA (RA25 p.103, p.110) `[sourced]` |
| Substitutes | **Medium-high** | Artisanal 44.3% and private label 9.6% of the global market (RA25 p.107); private label ~35% of US fresh bread units (Flowers 10-K) `[sourced]`; GLP-1 users cut grocery spend 5.3% in six months (Cornell/JMR, observational) `[sourced]` |

**Implication for long-run margins** `[assumption]`: two industries in one. In Mexico and Latin America the buyer is fragmented and Bimbo owns the route network, which supports EBITDA margins of ~20% (Mexico LTM 20.9% `[calc]`). In the US, Canada and Europe the buyer is concentrated, private label is strong and volume shrinks; there, scale protects volume but not margin.

## 4. Positioning and moat

| Source of advantage | Evidence | In the numbers? | Verdict `[assumption]` |
|---|---|---|---|
| Direct-store-delivery network | 54,000+ routes, 249 plants in 39 countries `[sourced]` RA25 p.14, p.100 | **Mexico yes:** EBITDA margin 20.9% LTM `[calc]`. **US no:** North America 9.6% LTM vs Flowers 11.1% on a lease-comparable basis `[calc]` peer workbook v3 | Wide in Mexico and Latin America; not shown in the US |
| Brands | 9,000+ SKUs; 12.6% of FY2025 sales from innovation `[sourced]` RA25 p.80, p.87 | Mexico yes (28.2% in sweet baked goods `[sourced]` RA25 p.111) | Strong in Mexico; medium in the US |
| Purchasing scale | Cited without a number (RA25 p.109) | Gross margin LTM 52.6% vs Flowers 48.6% `[calc]`; the advantage is spent on distribution cost | Higher gross margin; not converted into EBITDA in North America |
| Quick-service restaurant supply | Main supplier in 40 countries `[sourced]` RA25 p.87, p.112 | Partly: EAA +8.4% ex-FX in 2Q26 `[sourced]` | Medium |

**Durability.** Eroded slowly by longer-shelf-life packaging (widens the delivery radius of players without DSD), private label and trading down in the US, the labour cost of Mexico's routes (46-hour week in 2027, 40 hours by 2030), and health regulation (NOM-051, IEPS, school rules) on the highest-margin categories.

## LatAm lens

- **Regulation:** NOM-051 (phase 3 on 1-Jan-2028), mandatory school rules since 29-Mar-2025, IEPS 8% on foods ≥275 kcal/100 g (20% only as a tail; 2027 Ley de Ingresos not final at 17-Oct), water-law and circular-economy reforms; no concessions or regulated tariffs `[sourced]` RA25 p.23, p.29-31.
- **FX:** ~44% of sales in USD/CAD/EUR regions; debt 42-44% USD; wheat priced in USD; translation moved reported growth between -7.8 and +11.1 pp a quarter `[sourced]` wiki FX exposure. USD/MXN 18.479 at 16-Oct-2026 vs 17.47 at 30-Jun `[sourced]` `macro/macro-view.yaml`.
- **Informal market and data gaps:** artisanal bakeries (44.3% globally) and the traditional channel are poorly measured; Nielsen and Global Data disagree; no numeric shares for Brazil, Colombia or Argentina `[unverified]`.
- **Family groups:** Bimbo is controlled by the Servitje family (72.03% across six vehicles) `[sourced]`; Gruma (~51% control group), Arca, Lala (delisted 2022) and Nutresa (Gilinski) are also controlled `[sourced]`/`[unverified]`.
- **Cross-border players:** Mondelez, PepsiCo and Mars-Kellanova (US); Gruma (Mexico); Nutresa (Colombia); FGF Brands (Canada); Lidl and Aldi in Europe. Private label is the most structural entrant.

## Coach moments (team decides; logged in `docs/context/thesis-journal.md`)

1. **Market definition.** *Recommendation:* "branded packaged bakery with direct-store delivery, by country", not "global bakery". *Against:* country shares mix Global Data and Nielsen definitions and are re-based every year. *Question:* in which market does the consumer choose, and who sets the price: the US retailer or the Mexican shopkeeper?
2. **Moat.** *Recommendation:* a regional moat: wide in Mexico and Latin America, not shown in the US. *Against:* Mexico's margin mixes Barcel and sweet baked goods, so it may be product mix, not distribution. *Question:* which US P&L number would prove that scale is an advantage and not just size?
3. **Peer set.** *Recommendation:* Flowers and Gruma for the core median, the eligible peers as the range (six for EV/EBITDA, five for P/E), and Yamazaki, Aryzta and Arca shown but excluded until their claims are verified. *Against:* a two-company median is fragile. *Question:* why should a baker with 55% of EBITDA in Mexico exit at a lower multiple than the 6.94x the market pays today?
