## Environmental

- **Energy:** IC25 reports 100% renewable electricity in 2025 (validated by Carbon Trust, partly with certificates); the preliminary annex sent to LSEG says 99%, with 43,967 t of residual Scope 2 `[sourced]`. Clear the difference before quoting "100%".
- **Targets:** Scope 1 -50% and Scope 3 -28% by 2030 vs 2019. With the recalculated baseline, Scope 1 in 2025 is +1.4% above 2019 `[calc]` LSEG; the 2019 Scope 3 baseline rose 42% `[sourced]`.
- **Assurance:** KPMG limited assurance covers materials, water, waste and nutrition, **not emissions**; Carbon Trust covers renewable electricity and treated water `[sourced]` IC25 pp.176-180.
- **Physical risk:** water stress, extreme weather and wildfires raise insurance costs (RA25 pp.22, 33) `[sourced]`. Mexico's cap-and-trade system is the one climate rule management says will hit profits; its operating rules are unpublished `[sourced]`.

## Link to valuation

- **Climate and input costs → `gross_margin`** → a structural -50 bp = -Ps 6.13 Gordon (-18.3%) and -Ps 4.40 exit (-9.6%) `[calc]` DCF v3 replica (risk R1). Sustainability-linked bond step-ups are small: MXN 6m on the 23-2L and +25 bp on MXN 12,000m of the 23L, about MXN 30m a year from Nov-2031 `[sourced]` `wiki/Risks-ESG/bimbo-sustainable-financing.md` = about Ps 0.007 per share a year `[calc]`.
- **Nutrition and health regulation → Mexico margin (`opex_pct_revenue`)** → -100 to -150 bp = -Ps 4.73 to -Ps 6.98 Gordon (-14.1% to -20.8%) `[calc]` (risk R4).
- **Water, packaging, sourcing → capex and opex:** material but **not quantifiable**: Bimbo does not publish its sustainability capex line (IC25 p.168), the extended-producer-responsibility cost has no regulation yet, and sales exposed to the EU are not disclosed (EAA is 12.9% of LTM sales `[calc]`).
- **Governance → the multiple, not the discount rate.** Recommendation: no WACC premium. The v3 peer data changes the argument: on one claim basis Bimbo trades at **6.94x against 5.53x for the core bakers** and 8.27x for all eligible peers `[calc]` peer workbook v3. So the market pays a premium to pure bakers and a discount to consumer peers; governance may explain part of the second gap `[assumption]`, but not a discount to the bakers. Each 1x on the exit multiple = ±Ps 9.11 on the exit value `[calc]`.

## What a judge may ask

1. **Q:** Does family control justify a discount? **A (draft):** Against consumer peers, partly: Bimbo trades at 6.94x vs 8.27x for all eligible peers, with anti-takeover clauses (minority protections 1/10) and an unexplained 8.5-point gap in the declared control stake. Against pure bakers it trades at a premium (5.53x), so governance is not what the market prices there. We add no WACC premium.
2. **Q:** Scope 3 up 3% and a missed target: is this greenwashing? **A (draft):** The 23-2L target was missed and costs MXN 6m; the issue is credibility, not cash flow. Bimbo discloses the miss and restated the baseline, but SBTi validation is paused.
3. **Q:** Which ESG issue moves value? **A (draft):** Climate through wheat (pillar 1) and nutrition regulation in Mexico (55% of EBITDA). The rest is material, but either small where it can be measured (the bond step-ups) or not quantifiable from disclosures (water, packaging, sourcing).
