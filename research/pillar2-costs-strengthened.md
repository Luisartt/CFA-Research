## Proposed text for the report (pillar 2)

**Pillar 2: Costs can go up without breaking the margin — Bimbo already absorbed a shock almost four times larger.**
Between FY2021 and FY2023, raw materials rose from 30.0% to 32.8% of sales, and gross margin fell 206 bp. Even so, adjusted EBITDA margin never left a range of 13.4% to 14.0%. Our model asks much less of Bimbo: raw materials rise 76 bp of sales in FY2027E, because it already passes through the 25.9% increase in wheat in 2026 with a one-year lag. The market sees the same thing: consensus expects a 52.0% gross margin in FY2027 against our 52.15%. Hedges give visibility, not protection: the fourth quarter of 2026 is fully covered and the first half of 2027 almost fully, so the first quarter exposed to new prices is the third of 2027. That is why this pillar argues against selling, not for buying. We would change our view if wheat averages above US$294 per tonne in the first half of 2027, when Bimbo hedges the second half.

## Strongest case against (for Q&A)

FY2022 was a high-inflation year: net sales grew 17.7% (FY2022 vs FY2021, report v13), so Bimbo passed costs through with price. Today North America volumes are soft (call l.137) and private label holds a record share of US grocery units (report v13), so pricing power in North America may be lower than in 2022. Answer: Mexico and Latin America, where the route network gives pricing power, carry 54% of regional EBITDA (report v13); and the model asks for 76 bp, not 279.
